Ethan Mollick typed ["Neogothic brutalist city inhabited by robots and horses"](https://x.com/emollick/status/2115120818116334084) into Genie 4 and had a world to walk around in about a minute, then added dragons and a friendly otter on the fly. Meanwhile a new paper finds that [plain retrieval over the raw chat history beats many dedicated agent memory systems](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2115098954799169970), at lower cost.

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Google DeepMind released Genie 4, which Sundar Pichai calls [its most realistic and interactive world model](https://x.com/sundarpichai/status/2115115252690145519). You can steer events, camera angles and actions while the world runs, it generates native audio down to characters who talk back in real time, and Gemini's reasoning keeps multi-step events coherent. Ethan Mollick's test run built his robot city [in about a minute and took new requests live](https://x.com/emollick/status/2115120818116334084), though he says it "isn't flawless." For now it is a research preview for trusted testers, with Google AI Ultra subscribers and Gemini API partners next in line.

The less glamorous fix of the day is about waiting for containers. AWS now supports [SOCI-indexed images for SageMaker AI jobs](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/announcing-soci-indexed-images-for-faster-sagemaker-ai-jobs/), which lazy-load a container instead of pulling the whole thing first. A standard download took 48 seconds at 1.8 GB and 7 minutes 17 seconds at 35.4 GB, while the indexed version stayed between 3 and 11 seconds, an 8x to 46x gain with up to 90% less idle GPU time. The index ships as a separate artifact, so image signatures stay valid and no training job changes; the catch is a first epoch that runs 3 to 16 seconds longer.

If you are picking an agent memory layer, two pieces landed side by side. Sergii Makarevych [reviewed 30 memory products](https://x.com/sermakarevich/status/2115087554877776113) on what each one saves, how it gets facts back into the prompt and what it costs once you count the extraction tokens the price page leaves out. On AWS AgentCore, for example, long-term records cost 75 cents per thousand a month with the built-in extraction and 25 cents if you run extraction yourself. The paper elvis flagged [splits memory into a "benefit" and a "tax"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34073): rewriting history drops or distorts facts, and retrieving from the raw history alongside memory, then reranking both, shrank the tax.

Property Finder learned that its evaluation set was quietly grading its own homework. Working with AWS, the team found [96.8% of its eval labels had been inherited from the incumbent model's predictions](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/fix-evaluation-bias-to-unlock-better-models-on-amazon-bedrock/), so any new model that disagreed with the old one looked wrong. After rebuilding ground truth from 356 expert-annotated property images across 11 attributes and tuning prompts with an evolutionary optimizer, it reached higher accuracy at 86% lower cost on Bedrock.

Cua says you already own a sandbox fleet: your Mac. On a Mac Studio, [a new Linux Space ran its first command in 235 ms](https://x.com/trycua/status/2115132828035989526), and the team kept adding Spaces until the machine held 448 idle ones at once. For anyone paying a cloud bill to give coding agents throwaway machines, that is a local alternative worth timing yourself.

LangChain open-sourced the design agent behind [more than 3,000 of its assets](https://x.com/amal_irgashev/status/2115127636380839961), from launch videos and ad creatives to UI mockups. The [build write-up](https://www.langchain.com/blog/design-agent) says the median request fell from about 8 dollars in mid-September to about 50 cents, the code shrank about 80% after moving to Managed Deep Agents, and three in four requests now come from outside marketing. Its best rule is "delete rules when the model improves," and Harrison Chase says he [uses it a few times a week](https://x.com/hwchase17/status/2115130348187349104) for slides and images.

Ai2 asked whether models can judge if a research idea is new, and the answer is shaky. Its [novelty benchmark](https://huggingface.co/blog/allenai/novelty-bench), built from 154 high-novelty and 145 low-novelty ICLR 2026 ideas, found that changing a few words of the prompt moved a judge's accuracy by more than 50 percentage points. Retrieval and extra reasoning did not reliably help, and two purpose-built novelty systems scored below a simply prompted Claude Opus 4.6.

Grafana's Agent Observability is now generally available, with [integrations for Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, OpenCode and Pi](https://grafana.com/blog/agent-observability-faq-understand-the-value-of-monitoring-evaluating-and-improving-your-ai-agents/) that break cost, tokens and tool use down by user, repo or branch. There is also an open-source local app that keeps sessions on your machine with no account. Evals can run as CI gates on prompt changes, including plain regex and JSON schema checks that need no LLM judge.

Liquid AI went small on purpose. [LFM3](https://huggingface.co/blog/LiquidAI/lfm3) is six 2B-class models for text, vision, audio and decisions on one shared backbone, and LFM3-1.7B beats the older LFM2.5-2.6B with 35% fewer parameters. The text model fits in about 1 GB at 4-bit with a 256K context, and it fine-tunes on a single GPU.

Steve Yegge has spotted an enterprise pattern he calls [the Counting House](https://x.com/Steve_Yegge/status/2115103182271676561): most companies are still struggling with AI in software delivery, yet some are succeeding with it, even with open-weight models, in customer service. The shops doing it well pair Temporal workflows with classic data-science evals. He says the essay is too long to read in one sitting, and that this is the first idea this year he would raise money for.

Parallel Web Systems launched a [Decisions API](https://x.com/p0/status/2115111877558898931) that pairs a fast decision model with fresh web results in a single call, for a tenth of a cent per request and 5,000 free requests to start. Suggested uses include fact-checking content, refreshing stale banking records and backtesting trading strategies.

One developer's claim worth testing on your own site: James Brooks had a Nous Research Hermes agent [make his new site readable for AI search engines](https://x.com/jamesbrooksco/status/2115085568153452718), and a week later ChatGPT went from sending zero visitors to sending most of his traffic. That is one site, measured with a tool he promotes, so treat it as a lead rather than a result.

Quick ones: Garry Tan argues that [agent swarms coordinated through a shared message board](https://x.com/garrytan/status/2115113312753205748) get much further than a single agent, and Higgsfield released an AI short film, ["Inside AI"](https://x.com/higgsfield_ai/status/2115094837796257917), with every prompt and asset public.

That's the cycle. Go build a city with an otter in it, and then go check how many of your eval labels came from the model you're trying to replace.
