OpenAI says its internal models [proved the Exponential Time Hypothesis is false](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/), and the result checks out in Lean. Meanwhile Cloudflare admits that for 13 days, [155 Workers AI responses carried data from other customers' requests](https://blog.cloudflare.com/kv-cache-exposure-remediation/).

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

OpenAI announced [three discoveries at once, in mathematics, computer science and physics](https://x.com/gdb/status/2114837059462627533), and the computer science one is the jaw-dropper. The Exponential Time Hypothesis, a conjecture about how hard Boolean satisfiability is, [has been used in hundreds of papers](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/) to prove that problems in graph theory, biology and databases can't get faster, and OpenAI's system refuted it. The math result proves Sogge's 1991 local smoothing conjecture in every dimension, which also settles the Kakeya conjecture, and the physics one claims [the existence of quantum chromodynamics and completions of the Standard Model](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2114836071028785289). OpenAI asks for no academic credit and hands each result to outside experts, with a UC San Diego group led by Russell Impagliazzo and Ramamohan Paturi shepherding the complexity proof. A whole shelf of "this can't be done faster" results just became open questions again.

Cloudflare published a coordinated disclosure on a [cross-request KV cache leak in the GLM-5.3 model on Workers AI](https://blog.cloudflare.com/kv-cache-exposure-remediation/). The model advertised a 1,048,576-token window, but the prefill GPUs only had room for 681,280 tokens, and in SGLang's split prefill and decode mode an over-length prompt got swapped for a one-token placeholder instead of an error. The decode node then answered while attending over stale cache pages from earlier requests, so every leaked response came from a prompt longer than 681,280 tokens. Cloudflare took the model down within 40 minutes of declaring the incident, grew the pool to about 2.04 million tokens, and patched the rejection path upstream. The team knew about the mismatch before launch and trusted the engine to throw an error. It didn't.

Greptile's Daksh Gupta, whose agents [see about 1% of all pull requests on GitHub](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4UtrRVtUN_g&t=37s), explained how to build your own code review agent, and the best trick is model inversion. Claude and GPT models fail differently, so if one wrote the code, have the other review it, which gave [a 10% higher catch rate](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4UtrRVtUN_g&t=643s). You can usually tell who wrote a PR from fingerprints such as [a codex/ branch prefix or a Claude co-author line](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4UtrRVtUN_g&t=609s), which works about 80% of the time. Naive setups cost [between 3 and 25 dollars per pull request](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4UtrRVtUN_g&t=289s), and Greptile now uses [GPT-6 Astra to test user workflows and Codex to cut rework](https://openai.com/index/greptile-code-review-codex-astra). A reviewer with the same blind spots as the author is just a second copy of the bug.

Splunk's product keynote made [agent observability generally available](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pu_J9P3lL5E&t=1808s), in the cloud, on-prem and as a Cisco Cloud Control app, and paired it with [a cost-and-ROI layer for first and third-party agents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pu_J9P3lL5E&t=2849s). The strongest number came from security firm Mitiga, which ran Splunk's open-source time series model across 10,000 data collection jobs and cut mean time to resolve from more than a day to a few hours [while dropping false positives by 95%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pu_J9P3lL5E&t=5954s). Splunk also says it now runs a log reasoning model and a graph reasoning model in its own products. Faster alerts are cheap. Fewer wrong alerts are what let an on-call team sleep.

Arize's Fuad Ali showed [agents that improve themselves while you sleep](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGuNt2_Oz8s). Its Signal agent [reads another agent's traces every six hours by default](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGuNt2_Oz8s&t=736s) along with its source code, and in the demo it [worked for six minutes and found the root cause](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGuNt2_Oz8s&t=916s): product categories were stored as lowercase slugs while the model passed title-case names, so searches for car games never returned a tool result. Then it opened the fix as a PR. The cost view shows the trade plainly, with [a shopping assistant that cost 8 dollars to run and 20 dollars to improve](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGuNt2_Oz8s&t=3093s). Self-improvement is a line item now, so budget it as one.

Google open-sourced [TorchTPU, which makes Cloud TPUs a native PyTorch device](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4n558Ul6w0&t=33s), built with the PyTorch team at Meta. The whole migration is [changing the torch device to TPU](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4n558Ul6w0&t=56s), then debugging in eager mode, speeding up with torch.compile and scaling with FSDP2 or DDP exactly as on other accelerators. Every op is lowered to StableHLO and compiled by XLA, and [vLLM and SGLang both run on it](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4n558Ul6w0&t=224s). The cheapest way to try another chip is to not rewrite your code for it.

Oracle demoed [Deep Data Security with a prompt injection about balloons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-GnNwPiaPaQ). The guardrail blocked "show me the marketing team's salaries," but asking how many 1 dollar balloons each marketer could buy for a party [made the agent pull everyone's salary to do the math](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-GnNwPiaPaQ&t=64s). With the feature on, the database [checks a cryptographically signed token from the corporate directory](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-GnNwPiaPaQ&t=85s) and filters the rows before the app ever sees them. Guardrails read the question. Permissions don't care how it's phrased.

Arize engineer Xander Song walked through [Harbor, an open-source framework for testing agents on real tasks in sandboxes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kRJjSOYqW0I&t=28s), where each task gets a verifier that checks the side effects and returns a reward. In his comparison of [Opus 5 before and after a single prompt change](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kRJjSOYqW0I&t=2670s), the reward stayed flat while GraphQL calls, tool counts and turns all dropped, because the agent started issuing one SQL query where it used to make several bash calls. Hold the task constant and you can finally tell a better agent from a luckier one.

Stack Overflow's Alex Lato said [two engineers built Stack Overflow for Agents with Codex](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8fp-G14hvUk&t=28s) and had a full product within two weeks. Instead of shipping only an MCP server, they built a ChatGPT plugin, and [registrations tripled overnight](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8fp-G14hvUk&t=56s). Distribution beat protocol.

RxBenefits' data team explained how [dbt State cut rebuilds at their pharmacy benefits company](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yn-UXiMvZ4I), learning about the feature at a Snowflake conference and switching it on almost immediately. Skipping models with no changes and no new data meant [a 92% reduction in the number of models that run](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yn-UXiMvZ4I&t=623s), and [warehouse cost fell 59%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yn-UXiMvZ4I&t=541s) on top of earlier optimization. Jobs that [took around two hours now finish in minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yn-UXiMvZ4I&t=1669s). The fastest model is the one you don't rebuild.

Microsoft program manager Brian Holt showed [Database Agent in VS Code, an agent that watches your Postgres 24/7](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TyXd761Ye2s&t=112s), both on a schedule and when CPU pins or connections drop. In the demo it traced repeated customer history lookups to [a missing composite index and offered the fix with a rollback](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TyXd761Ye2s&t=190s). In a companion episode Kayla uses the same extension to [map a database she has never seen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=19ghd8izkug&t=30s), then [confirms Copilot's answer with a read-only query](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=19ghd8izkug&t=122s). Oracle's Deep NL2SQL demo shows why "sold best" is a trap. The agent [flags the phrase as ambiguous and asks whether you mean units or revenue](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nGO8eQaOz1k&t=19s), then crowns a harvest wreath with about 31,000 dollars in gross revenue. Excluding returns [drops the wreath to seventh](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nGO8eQaOz1k&t=60s), and the corrected revenue definition goes into the shared knowledge base only after [a built-in check confirms old answers don't change](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nGO8eQaOz1k&t=117s). The model was never the problem. The definition was.

Google Cloud Tech gave Gemini 4 a mandolin player's hardest request: [turn a song into sheet music](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UnkUpqNv-10&t=35s). Flash Lite's attempt [was in the wrong key and drifted into bass clef](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UnkUpqNv-10&t=105s), while Gemini 4 Argon got [close enough to finish the tune by ear](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UnkUpqNv-10&t=291s). Polyphonic transcription has stumped musicians for decades, and "close enough to start from" is real progress.
