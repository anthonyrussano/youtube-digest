Mistral just handed out the weights to a 1-trillion-parameter model that [leads every open model on FrontierCode and Terminal-Bench](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-large-4-oss/). Meanwhile a game studio says it [spends 30 times its payroll on AI tokens](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/29/how-i-built-the-most-ai-pilled-company-in-the-world/), and treats an hour without shipping as its worst kind of incident.

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Mistral Large 4, nicknamed Le Chonk, went from public preview to [open weights](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-large-4-oss/) after three more weeks of reinforcement learning. It scores 48% on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index, tied with GLM-5.3, while using 47% fewer tokens per task, and on Cognition's private FrontierCode 2.0, which asks whether a maintainer would actually merge the change, it leads at 19.7%. In Surge's blind coding comparisons only Claude Opus 5.5 beat it. It also refuses 96.6% of malicious cyber requests while still doing defensive patch work, and it ships with three reasoning modes to trade cost against depth. A self-hostable model that sits one rung below the frontier changes the math for every team paying per token.

That math is exactly what Revolut ran. David Abitbol, who leads AI operations at the 80-million-customer bank, says engineers were [spending 1,000 to 10,000 dollars a month on AI with little to show for it](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ok6QdZHCcI8&t=169s), so in April the bank started self-hosting GLM 5.1 and saw [60% faster coding time and 70% faster lead time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ok6QdZHCcI8&t=198s) across 2,000 engineers. The blended cost is now [30 cents per million tokens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ok6QdZHCcI8&t=332s), and his tuning advice is blunt: [long context is a scam, serve 256K instead of a million, and keep coding cache hits above 90%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ok6QdZHCcI8&t=420s). Frontier models stay, but as the exception rather than the default.

On the other end of the spending curve sits Spawn, a multiplayer game where players prompt the world into shape. Founder Jacob Sansbury describes [a swarm of agents costing over 3 million dollars a month in tokens](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/29/how-i-built-the-most-ai-pilled-company-in-the-world/) that change live code and ship to production hourly, and about 60% of their work starts from a production error or a bug report the in-game AI files itself. Rollbacks take 30 seconds. The funniest detail is a failure: when they asked GPT-6 Astra to apply security fixes, it made every insecure feature secure by turning it off. Simon Willison [called it the sharpest edge of agentic engineering right now](https://x.com/simonw/status/2115821668350435645), and games are a safe place to find where that edge is.

Sonar CEO Tariq Shaukat explains why humans can't referee this pace. Async agents pushed [lines of code written up 2,450%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LQW_Wvqhp04&t=200s), and while [only 5% of developers run agentic software factories today, 34% expect to within a year](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LQW_Wvqhp04&t=234s). Worse, people [agree with the AI 93% of the time when it's right, and four out of five times when it's wrong](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LQW_Wvqhp04&t=385s), a pattern he calls cognitive surrender. His fix is guide rails rather than guard rails, plus independent verification, because a human in the loop who nods along is no loop at all.

Temporal's Shy Ruparel brought survey numbers to the same problem. [51.3% of teams get an AI prototype production-ready in hours or less](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mJ7q8CuVmbw&t=455s), but at companies over 5,000 people it takes months, and [22.2% lose progress and redo work after crashes and restarts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mJ7q8CuVmbw&t=553s). The live demo killed a worker mid-payment and the workflow resumed without reserving funds twice. Durable execution turns the 2 AM manual state repair into something the platform simply handles.

One agent-platform builder laid out [the seven must-haves of a software factory](https://x.com/BHolmesDev/status/2115862731610107965), and the list reads as a spec sheet: configuration as code, central conversation history, benchmarking, factory chat, artifacts, computer use and an agent debugger. The debugger is the one most teams skip, and it's the one you miss first.

Serval co-founder Jake Stauch is aiming that same factory idea at IT departments, where [ServiceNow has been the legacy giant](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EZcWEsMuLb4&t=91s). His bet from day one was that [code is the right way to express enterprise automation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EZcWEsMuLb4&t=615s), so agents write the workflows instead of admins clicking through forms. If he's right, the ticketing system becomes a code generator with an approval button.

GitHub's Universe recap adds one number the keynote coverage skipped. Copilot Fusion, which picks between single, cascade and critique patterns across several models, [scored 60 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index, two points behind Opus 5.5 max, at half the cost and 40% less time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xbCa0aa4tRM&t=110s). Copilot Projects, which fans agents out across several repositories at once, is [available to everyone starting today](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xbCa0aa4tRM&t=191s). Routing between models is quietly becoming the product.

LangChain shipped [Managed Deep Agents v0.10](https://www.langchain.com/blog/managed-deep-agents-browser-use-and-asynchronous-subagents) with a browser and async subagents. When a site asks for a login, the agent calls a handoff tool and gives you the window, so your password never lands in the model's messages or traces, and the handoff link expires after 15 minutes. DataDome saw agentic browser requests jump 4.7 times in September alone. Background subagents mean you can keep talking to the agent while a long job runs.

Google security engineers showed how to keep coding agents from leaking keys. A [pre-tool-use hook blocks the agent's git commit and sends the denial back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VzpbeVdS_ok&t=45s), so the agent moves the secret into an environment variable or a secret manager and tries again. The review skills they rely on are [open source under the name Mantis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VzpbeVdS_ok&t=237s), and they warn against blocking everything, since an unconfigured scanner just floods developers with false positives.

One security builder sorted [29 guardrails for agents that read strangers' text](https://x.com/sermakarevich/status/2115801259173151024) by one test: does it hold when the attacker adapts? Filters that inspect text fold, while defenses that remove a capability hold, and he tried seven of them on a real support inbox. If your agent can act, take away what it shouldn't do rather than hoping you can spot the bad prompt.

OpenAI rewrote its [Model Spec](https://openai.com/index/updated-model-spec) around five values: faithfulness to human intent, harm minimization, honesty and transparency, human agency, and care. The revision adds guidance on context, oversight and agency, and it retires much of the detailed style guidance. Instead of a longer rulebook, it bets on judgment for the cases nobody wrote down.

Decision models had a busy day. [A new paper argues](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2115810394614960516) that retrieval is the weakest step in RAG and that decision models can fix it; IBM's [Granite Switch 4.1.1](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-switch-4-1-1) shipped an improved Decision adapter plus speech recognition in 10 languages without retraining the base model, and Ollama added [a Decision model called Clef to its cloud lineup](https://x.com/ollama/status/2115838993074098261) alongside Mistral Large 4. Swapping adapters the way you ship software patches is how small models stay current.

Patrick at ServeTheHome found a Strix Halo box he prefers to the rest: ASRock Industrial's AI Box A395 with [128 GB of memory, 10 gigabit and 2.5 gigabit Ethernet and four display outputs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=68QinHpIV6Y&t=0s). It [shredded other systems on their benchmarks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=68QinHpIV6Y&t=365s) while running at [130 to 140 watts at the chip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=68QinHpIV6Y&t=724s), and with a hardware watchdog and an internal power supply he argues [an always-on box of this kind beats a beefier laptop for running agents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=68QinHpIV6Y&t=985s).

Fish Audio's Drama 3 is now [the top open-weights model on both Artificial Analysis speech arenas](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2115820963350929580), and first among all models on pronunciation robustness. Open voice just caught up with the closed leaders on the hardest words.

Grafana 13.3 brings [cross-dashboard variables, Notebooks and the biggest visual refresh since Grafana 7](https://grafana.com/blog/grafana-13-3-release-all-the-latest-features/). Define an environment or cluster selector once, globally or per folder, and every dashboard picks it up. Notebooks collect panels, queries and Grafana Assistant findings in one document, which is exactly what a post-incident review needs.

dbt Labs' Dave Fowler pitches dbt Charts as [the Markdown of dashboards](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YDwE3ety3og&t=1408s), with [dashboards defined in YAML](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YDwE3ety3og&t=1583s) so they get code review and source control. His claim for agents is practical: point your assistant at it and [your token costs go down](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YDwE3ety3og&t=3192s) while the charts come out prettier and governable.

Gergely Orosz sat down with Titus Winters on [how Google refactored 250 million lines of C++ before AI](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2115804551672725928), and what happened when Search couldn't ship on schedule because of regressions. It's the large-scale change playbook that agent fleets are now rediscovering.

TechWorld with Nana walks through [what happens in the second after you press enter on a URL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_x1GskHtJxo&t=0s), starting with [DNS as the internet's phonebook](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_x1GskHtJxo&t=53s). Every hop in that chain is something a cloud engineer keeps running, which makes it a tidy five-minute map of the job.

Which would you try first: self-hosting an open model the way Revolut does, a capability-removing guardrail for your agents, or a pre-commit hook that stops your agent from leaking keys? Tell me which one.

That's the cycle. Check your token bill, take away what your agents shouldn't touch, and see you tomorrow.
