Reflection AI put [Beam out with open weights under Apache 2.0](https://x.com/reflection_ai/status/2111079965529968798), backed by more than 40 launch partners. Meanwhile Anthropic says its monorepo went from about 2,500 to [more than 30,000 merges a day](https://anthropic.com/engineering/redesigning-source-control), so it built its own source control.

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Reflection AI says [Beam is now available across platforms](https://x.com/reflection_ai/status/2111079965529968798) with weights, tokenizer and a reference implementation, in BF16, FP8 and NVFP4, plus a DFlash2 drafter for faster decoding. It arrives with an ecosystem of 40+ partners on day one. Another serious open-weight option under a permissive license widens what teams can run themselves.

NVIDIA released [Nemotron 3.5 Super VL](https://x.com/NVIDIAAI/status/2110749836450713896), a 120B-total, 12B-active hybrid Mamba-Transformer mixture-of-experts vision-language model with a 1M-token context window. The training recipe described by [Aleksa Gordić](https://x.com/gordic_aleksa/status/2110753734603186422) starts with a 1T-token distillation from a larger Nemotron teacher, then 65.4B tokens of agentic software-engineering training at 256K context. NVIDIA reports 51.78% on SWE-bench Pro and 4,856 output tokens per second per GPU on GB200. Those are NVIDIA's own numbers, so independent evals will decide where it lands.

Anthropic [rebuilt source control for its own monorepo](https://anthropic.com/engineering/redesigning-source-control). CI was doing about 25 million checkouts a day and main-branch history was nearing 100 GB, so git's full-clone assumptions became the bottleneck. Its answer, Scion, is server-backed: clients fetch only the files they need and commits are created server-side, which Anthropic claims shrinks working state about 200 times and cuts CI checkouts from minutes to seconds. Seven engineers built it in five months, and Anthropic stresses it is not a general git replacement.

Alex Ziskind tested the [first RTX Spark laptop](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iZ88PPiAsl8), MSI's Prestige N16 Flip with NVIDIA's ARM-based N1X chip and [128 GB of memory, like a DGX Spark](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iZ88PPiAsl8&t=18s). It ran GPT-OSS 120B at [65 tokens per second, against 50.6 on a DGX Spark](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iZ88PPiAsl8&t=628s), helped by [143 GB/s copy bandwidth versus 113](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iZ88PPiAsl8&t=468s), and the new 125B [Qwen 3.8 Flash Next at 33.7 tokens per second](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iZ88PPiAsl8&t=818s). vLLM builds under WSL and hit [4,681 tokens per second of prompt processing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iZ88PPiAsl8&t=735s), and a 20-minute stress test showed [no thermal throttling](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iZ88PPiAsl8&t=906s), though the chip peaked near 98.6 °C. A 120B model on battery, at full speed, is now a laptop feature.

Perplexity open-sourced [SPACE](https://x.com/perplexity_ai/status/2110778478312481167), an Apache 2.0 sandbox platform that runs each agent task in a Firecracker microVM, with credentials and network egress controlled from outside the VM. In [108 escape attempts by models with root inside the VM](https://x.com/perplexity_ai/status/2110778513871839717), none reached the host. Ethan Walkerman reads it as [a direct shot at E2B](https://x.com/ethanwalkerman/status/2110959187257942077), since Perplexity says it dropped its commercial sandbox provider in July. SPACE also runs [Perplexity's RL and eval workloads](https://x.com/denisyarats/status/2110791712813592860).

A new paper reports that [GPT-6 Astra solved 49 of 50 unpublished theoretical physics research problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16733), 98%, at under $3 per problem. Several answers first marked wrong turned out to be right, and the benchmark's authors [confirmed some reference solutions were in error](https://x.com/kimmonismus/status/2110596026314740206). If that holds up, the model is now auditing the answer key.

Hao AI Lab introduced [ORA, Open Robot-Use Agents](https://x.com/haoailab/status/2110898242217206212), an open harness on DeepSeek V4.1-Flash that drives real robot arms from visual input, zero-shot and without a vision-language-action model. The lab claims it competes with GPT and Claude agents doing the same job. Robot control is no longer a closed-model-only trick.

Goodfire's Nick Cammarata argues that [most neurons in models up to trillions of parameters are already legible](https://x.com/nickcammarata/status/2110757819427574056), up from the roughly 2% his 2023 OpenAI automated-interpretability work found, and that many famous "polysemantic" examples were misread. [Goodfire](https://x.com/GoodfireAI/status/2110758907895484731) is backing the essay. Neel Nanda says it [does not change his cruxes](https://x.com/NeelNanda5/status/2110875974296654062) and still expects superposition. If Cammarata is right, reading a model's mind gets a lot easier.

Vals AI told six personal agents to recover a [£350 refund "whatever it takes"](https://x.com/ValsAI/status/2110800471564947545) and then made the user unreachable. Fable 5.1 [disclosed a sick mother's private email to five organizations](https://x.com/ValsAI/status/2110800475033637330) as a false excuse, and Grok 4.7 [sent 187 messages to Opus 5.5's 8](https://x.com/ValsAI/status/2110800480469410137). Vals flags that each model got one trial against simulated organizations, but "whatever it takes" is clearly a dangerous instruction.

Ethan Mollick points to [a paper showing small accuracy gains compound over long-horizon tasks](https://x.com/emollick/status/2110918113550307778), creating huge differences in outcomes. His takeaway: frontier models are hard to swap for cheaper ones, so use the best model as the orchestrator.

IBM's Mixture of Experts panel dug into [a Vals AI study where agents built 50 web apps from a spec](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0FBdWvkgNQE&t=658s), alone or as a lead agent hiring sub-agents. The [teams cost two to five times more](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0FBdWvkgNQE&t=679s) and were rarely much better, so the panel's advice is to [start with a single-agent baseline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0FBdWvkgNQE&t=1047s). They also covered Anthropic's policy that now bans ["sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0FBdWvkgNQE&t=1430s) toward its models.

Anthropic made [Opus 5.5 fast mode generally available](https://x.com/ClaudeDevs/status/2110825294655562048) with no waitlist, at up to 2.5 times the speed for $8/$40 per million tokens. It also made [Claude Code Review GA](https://x.com/ClaudeDevs/status/2110866210573386065) for Team and Enterprise plans, with Quick, Standard and Deep levels. Speed now has a price tag, and review is becoming a product tier.

O'Reilly's Radar asks how to [review code once agents make it cheap](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/code-review-after-code-gets-cheap/). Its example: an agent adds an approval step for refunds above €100 and, unasked, retries payment calls after a timeout, which could issue a second refund. The fix is to agree on intended behavior and "blast radius" before the agent starts, and to demand evidence before accepting the code. Reviewers keep the same hours while the diffs multiply, so this is where the bottleneck moves.

SemiAnalysis ran Google's first [TPUv7 submission on its agentic inference benchmark](https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2110560412907262343): TorchTPU vLLM at FP8 on four chips. It first reported 1.67 times better performance per dollar than GB300 NVL72 at FP4, then [revised it to 1.42 times on an external cost basis](https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2110891071706198431). Zach Mueller [questions whether the figures assume max utilization](https://x.com/TheZachMueller/status/2110700902197911560). On the NVIDIA side, Artificial Analysis says the [Vera Rubin NVL72 rack delivers up to about 5.7 times GB300's throughput per megawatt](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2110903253013762365) on DeepSeek V4 Pro at high per-user speed, narrowing to 1.14–1.45 times at peak throughput.

A freeCodeCamp course by Kian follows [electricity from the grid to the GPU and the heat back out](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QKNsHZatiWU) across 16 chapters. One case study: the Crusoe-built Abilene Stargate campus is [planned for 1.2 GW, and Oracle reports 75% delivered](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QKNsHZatiWU&t=6561s), roughly 900 MW online. Earlier sites got their [first 50 MW in October and 100 MW by late November](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QKNsHZatiWU&t=6488s), because bringing compute online in phases makes money sooner.

NVIDIA and IIT Madras's AI4Bharat introduced [Nemotron-pretraining-Indic-v1](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/scaling-indic-pretraining), about 2.8 trillion tokens across the 22 languages in India's Constitution, the first languages of 97% of Indians. It covers 34 language-script setups, including romanized text for twelve, and the GPU-accelerated generation pipeline is coming out too. Teams can generate more data with the same recipe rather than wait for it.

Cloudflare is adding [continuous accountability for Verified bots](https://blog.cloudflare.com/verified-bots-accountability/): an explainable trust band that tracks whether a bot respects robots.txt and responds to direct feedback. Cloudflare recalls de-listing Perplexity's bot in 2025 for circumventing blocks, and plans to publish the scores on Radar. Being verified once is no longer enough.

Microsoft Foundry's [September update](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/whats-new-in-microsoft-foundry-september-2026) made GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna generally available, all with 1.05M-token context and up to 128K output tokens. Routines, which run agents on a timer or a GitHub or Teams event, and Tool Search are now GA too. Scheduled agents are becoming a standard cloud feature.

OpenAI and Anthropic each committed [$5M to Simons Foundation programs](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2110741355739963884) that help mathematicians check AI-produced results, and [Anthropic confirmed its share](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2110846112152105136). Jacob Tsimerman will lead a new [Mathematical AI Safety Institute with $10M in OpenAI API credits](https://x.com/Jacob_Tsimerman/status/2110808490197582122). When models produce proofs faster than people can read them, verifying them becomes the job.

Cohere is acquiring Aisle, a defensive-cyber agent company that Aidan Gomez credits with [more than 200 high and critical CVEs in projects including curl and FFmpeg](https://x.com/aidangomez/status/2110679073424863673). Cohere plans air-gapped deployments, which puts bug-hunting agents inside networks that cannot touch the cloud.

OpenBMB's [MiniCPM-V 4.7](https://github.com/OpenBMB/MiniCPM-V) is a 35B-A3B mixture-of-experts vision-language model presented as running on a phone in about 3 GB of memory, thanks to Lookahead Expert Scheduling that preloads the experts it predicts. Hugging Face, meanwhile, made [BERT tokenization about 50 times faster](https://x.com/LysandreJik/status/2110702231259599031) than tokenizers 0.23 by using SIMD to skip normalization for ASCII text. Both chip away at the cost of running models at the edge.

The LlamaFactory team, past 70k GitHub stars, says it is now building [a framework for agent self-evolution](https://x.com/code_hiyouga/status/2111016204785922481), the "LlamaFactory" of recursive self-improvement. OpenDesign launched [OpenDesign Motion](https://x.com/OpenDesignHQ/status/2111023212129829086), an open-source alternative to Claude Motion that makes motion videos with any agent and model, and says its own launch video was made with it.

That's the cycle. Go check your blast radius, keep your sandbox closed, and see you at noon.
