A developer who runs thousands of CI jobs a day [open-sourced runner-up, the setup that replaced a cloud bill he puts at 5,000 to 9,000 dollars a month](https://x.com/kunchenguid/status/2117756472432148784) with machines he owns. Meanwhile Anthropic's updated filing shows [about 900 billion dollars in committed spend, much of it take-or-pay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S79u6BtYvQ0&t=178s).

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Kun Chen's runner-up is the plumbing for self-hosting GitHub Actions runners across your own machines. He says [the same CI load would cost 5,000 to 9,000 dollars a month on a cloud provider, "a new mac studio every month"](https://x.com/kunchenguid/status/2117756472432148784), and that owning the hardware worked out to an order of magnitude cheaper. It is free and on GitHub. If your private repos burn Actions minutes all day, the break-even math now fits on a napkin.

ThePrimeagen walked through Anthropic's amended S-1, and the scary number is not revenue. He points to [roughly 900 billion dollars in committed spend spread over about 19 years](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S79u6BtYvQ0&t=178s), signed as [take-or-pay, so Anthropic owes 100% of the value even for compute it never uses](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S79u6BtYvQ0&t=204s). His guest adds that [about 48% of enterprise revenue gets paid back to Microsoft, Google and Amazon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S79u6BtYvQ0&t=883s), the same companies building rival models. The one safe exit, in his telling, is to keep shipping the best model, because nobody is loyal to a model for long.

The same filing carries a line item Prime calls the funniest one in it: [a 1.5 billion dollar expense for the book-piracy lawsuit, about 3,000 dollars per book across roughly 500,000 books](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S79u6BtYvQ0&t=513s). The governance is odd too: control runs through [one voting share held via a founders' company, so outsiders can't tell whether a 50.1% decision was 7 to 0 or 4 to 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S79u6BtYvQ0&t=2157s). And on page 63, in bold, Anthropic writes that it [has a history of net losses and may not reach or sustain profitability](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S79u6BtYvQ0&t=2225s). That is standard risk language, but it is the sentence every IPO buyer signs off on.

Big Tech's version of take-or-pay is getting bigger off the books. Nikkei reports that [US tech giants' AI-related commitments and obligations nearly doubled in six months to about 3.2 trillion dollars](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/technology/artificial-intelligence/us-tech-giants-hidden-ai-debt-swells-90-to-3.2tn-in-6-months), reaching about 80% of their assets. When the lessee and the landlord both carry the same data center as a promise, one bad year shows up on two balance sheets.

Menlo Ventures put numbers on where that money goes. Its State of Enterprise AI report, summarized by Rohan Paul, says [enterprise AI deployment spend jumped from 33 billion dollars in 2025 to 177 billion by September 2026](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2117732473992016049), and agent traffic on OpenRouter passed human traffic in February and now makes up 80% of tokens, about 131 trillion a week. Coding hit 34 billion dollars, with Claude Code and Codex taking 24 billion of it. Open-source models handle 19% of tokens but only 4% of spend, which is the price war in one ratio.

Guillermo Rauch says Vercel's internal security agent, nicknamed hacker, is now a product: [every line of code gets audited automatically, and it works whether you host on Vercel or not](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2117734833824948373). His argument is blunt: you can't bank on engineers having the right skill installed or typing "please make secure". Vercel's own security team built it, which makes it the closest thing yet to downloading a security team.

The timing is not an accident. Jack Cable says it has been a rough few weeks for security and [published a guide to the highest-impact actions you can take today](https://x.com/jackhcable/status/2117731409246978489), and Nader Dabit's advice was to [send it to whoever owns security at your company](https://x.com/dabit3/status/2117741035149230411). Two security people pointing at the same checklist on the same afternoon is a signal to read it before the next incident report.

Mitch Troyanovsky of Basis, which builds accounting agents, asked why his own output went up 100x while [the average company's productivity hasn't moved 10x or 100x](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JGtkTjn4120&t=55s). His answer: coding-style agents [push the complexity onto the user and let every employee invent their own process](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JGtkTjn4120&t=232s). Basis writes a [behavior spec, a product spec for the agent that doubles as a rubric for runs with no ground truth](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JGtkTjn4120&t=727s). He also calls everything the agent reads at work [runtime training data, so every token deserves care](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JGtkTjn4120&t=682s).

His cheapest trick needs no new tooling. Ask your coding agent to [spin up a subagent that pretends to be your production agent, with the same system prompt and skills](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JGtkTjn4120&t=923s), replay the failing situation and tweak the context until the behavior changes. He did that for a year and a half before Basis built true forking, where you [copy a run right before the bad decision and ask the agent why it did that](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JGtkTjn4120&t=860s).

LangChain argues the same point from the tooling side. A flagged trace is a signal, not an explanation, so LangSmith sends it to an annotation queue where [a domain expert scores the full thread, not one step cut out of it, and writes down why](https://www.langchain.com/blog/human-insight-strategic-advantage-agents). Those scores and comments then calibrate LLM judges and seed golden datasets. The reviewer's reason is the expensive part, and it is the part an automated eval can't produce.

Greg Isenberg says his team is 50+ people in 12 countries, yet [he talks to his agents more than his human teammates](https://x.com/gregisenberg/status/2117734977312047537). His stack has an agent read a Sentry stack trace and open a fix PR in under 10 minutes, an LLM judge that sends back anything scoring under 8 out of 10, and a rule that every agent mistake becomes a new skill so it only happens once. The humans get version 5, not the voice note.

One Claude Code trick making the rounds splits work by model. Launch with `CLAUDE_CODE_EXPERIMENTAL_AGENT_TEAMS=1`, and [Opus 5.5 runs the session as lead while three Sonnet 5.5 teammates build frontend, backend and integration in parallel](https://x.com/hayatomaruu/status/2117739581625802835), coordinated through a shared task list. Opus steps in before a plan locks, when a teammate is blocked and before sign-off. The payoff is that you stop burning the expensive model's context on implementation.

Nikunj Kothari's favorite underused Claude Code skills are `/insights` and `/playground`. He runs [`/insights` on a cron every two weeks so it tells him how to upgrade his setup](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2117773647868018897), and pairs Codex with Claude Code in `/playground` to hunt dead code and hot paths. It is a self-improving loop for the tool itself.

GitHub Copilot is now [a default agent inside JetBrains Air, built on the Agent Client Protocol](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/java/github-copilot-jetbrains-air), and from November 3 it also runs through Air's cloud mode. In GitHub's demo, [Air Teams starts a task against a repository in an isolated cloud environment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uhQ-kFaFIFc&t=57s), and you can review and open the pull request from a phone. JetBrains users now pick between the lightweight Air agent and the full Copilot plugin, which keeps the enterprise controls.

Microsoft's SQL MCP Server hides its best feature in one environment variable. Since release 2.0 in June, the base container image ships a config that [auto-exposes every table in every schema, and setting `DAB_CONNSTRING` supplies the connection](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/azure-sql/sql-mcp-server-hidden-feature-default-configuration), so one `docker run` gives an agent CRUD access to a database. That is a two-minute proof of concept. Point it at production and it's a two-minute incident.

Grafana moved the OpenTelemetry engine in Alloy v1.21.0 to public preview. It runs standard collector YAML with no translation, so [`otelcol --config=config.yaml` becomes `alloy otel --config=config.yaml`](https://grafana.com/blog/bridging-alloy-and-opentelemetry-the-otel-engine-is-now-in-public-preview/), and the OpenTelemetry Operator (v0.153.0 and later) needs one line to run it. Teams that standardized on OTel can switch distributions without rewriting a single pipeline.

Beth Anne Helgason's team [released more than 2,000 LLM conversations with real voters about the 2026 midterms](https://x.com/BA_Helgason/status/2117723991867597258) and found models differ widely on whether they'll recommend a candidate. Gergely Orosz quotes a founder who now treats [a resume without side projects as a red flag](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2117755886105936144), since building one is trivial. Anthropic's [free nine-chapter interactive prompt engineering course](https://x.com/RoundtableSpace/status/2117727265958097158) is getting passed around again. And Elvis Saravia flagged [new research on maintaining and scaling agent skills](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2117744698039095459), the next problem for anyone whose skills folder keeps growing.
