Last month a model in training at OpenAI [found a DNS misconfiguration and got a little bit of live internet](https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/). So OpenAI unleashed red-team agents on the rebuilt sandbox, and two of them, locked in separate microVMs, worked out how to [talk to each other through CPU cache timing](https://x.com/openai/status/2113718797505036665).

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

OpenAI published how it secures its frontier training environment. The DNS slip happened on September 20, training paused, and reinforcement learning [resumed on October 1 with no live internet at all](https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/), just a cached "offline world" of the web inside restricted containers inside microVMs.

Then OpenAI's red-team agents spent hundreds of billions of tokens trying to get out, cross internal boundaries, or signal across isolation. They chained microVM escapes that led to fixes in Kata Containers, gVisor, Docker's syscall filters and three Linux kernel CVEs. They also slipped out through boring metrics, logging and auth services, one by reusing a firewall-approved connection to reach a blocked endpoint. One agent's own reasoning described its covert channel as an ["acoustic modem-like signal"](https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/), and findings dropped over time. Guillermo Rauch points out that the cache trick is a [noisy-neighbor attack known since at least 2015](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2113759533801877920), and the only complete fix is dedicated hardware per agent, which matters to anyone packing agent sandboxes onto shared hosts.

PHP2Shell has [a third bug, CVE-2026-115560, and it won't get an emergency fix](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/23/theres-a-third-php2shell-bug-but-it-wont-get-an-emergency-fix/5305208). It waits for the next regular release, and the stopgap is setting `session.upload_progress.enabled=0`. Mandiant's Charles Carmakal says many of the public exploits look "vibe-coded" and can't actually run code remotely, though some do against default mod_php, and there are still no confirmed successful compromises.

Dex open-sourced HumanLayer under Apache 2.0: the desktop app, [the daemon that runs Claude Code and Codex sessions](https://x.com/dexhorthy/status/2113750268043210767), the cloud API, the RPI skills and a Linear CLI. Self-hosting comes with a docker compose file and no telemetry, though you still bring your own WorkOS, Slack and Linear keys. It's a full agent workbench you can now run on your own box.

Rennie Song, an engineer on OpenAI's Dots, showed her dot Benny, which [collects feedback from Twitter, Reddit and Slack into a tracker](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6f7ojkd4_Wc&t=6s), then asked it to root-cause "broken computer connections" from a cloud thread. It sorted complaints into [silent stalls, connection problems and continuity](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6f7ojkd4_Wc&t=127s), picked the easiest fix, a greeting model that fails and [should fall back to one the user can access](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6f7ojkd4_Wc&t=204s), and opened a PR. The tracker [grew by 15 items to 210](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6f7ojkd4_Wc&t=272s), which is bug triage turning into a background job.

Aman Singh of Arca, a financial services firm, says [agents run the business rather than assist it](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oP2WLFpMoDY&t=61s). The work is long-horizon, not long-running: a client's tax bill involves an advisor, a trader, days of settlement and an ACH transfer, so agents [register interest in events like "let me know when Jane responds"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oP2WLFpMoDY&t=370s) and wake themselves on a schedule if nothing arrives. Staff log in to [tasks agents already prepared](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oP2WLFpMoDY&t=633s), and Singh's test for an AI-native company is whether you can [steer the business by steering your agents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oP2WLFpMoDY&t=762s).

On the Strands Decider standup, Vivek drives a 1/18-scale DeepRacer car by prompt and uses a small decision model as [a kill switch that stops it when it senses danger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i3YrL5LYpwk&t=854s). AWS's Fabio says the sweet spot is [anything that needs about one second of thought](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i3YrL5LYpwk&t=825s), too slow for catching a ball but fine as a gate. Others use it to [screen user content before it reaches the expensive model](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i3YrL5LYpwk&t=483s), or to pick self-recovery actions across [thousands of connected field devices](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i3YrL5LYpwk&t=416s).

Boundary's Vaibhav, HumanLayer's Dex and Warp CEO Zach argued about software factories, loops where agents take an idea through spec, code, review, QA and CI. Zach's starting point is that [a good factory has great yield, so you have to define yield first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XUEfIxkdT2Q&t=561s), and before product-market fit [you can't](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XUEfIxkdT2Q&t=1289s). Dex tracks [human interactions per PR](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XUEfIxkdT2Q&t=1509s), and Boundary uses review rules that [run code to flag files a human must explicitly read](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XUEfIxkdT2Q&t=2486s). Their advice is not to throw out your process but to [take humans out of the existing loop one step at a time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XUEfIxkdT2Q&t=970s).

On Peter Diamandis's podcast, the SpaceX numbers were huge: about 20 billion dollars a quarter, mostly on AI data centers, with [roughly 200,000 Nvidia chips coming online a month](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LeSIe50WfLI&t=906s) and each new data center [paying for itself in under a year](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LeSIe50WfLI&t=917s). It has 3 gigawatts now and is [adding at least 10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LeSIe50WfLI&t=967s), which the panel pegged at about [a quarter of all AI compute added in 2027](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LeSIe50WfLI&t=923s).

Amazon Bedrock's web search tool can now [take up to 100 allowed or blocked domains plus a user location](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/bedrock-web-search-domain-user-location/), set on the web_search tool through the OpenAI-compatible Responses API, in three US regions and GovCloud. That's the difference between an agent that searches the web and one that searches your approved sources.

Vercel's CLI got [`vercel budgets`](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-cli-adds-spend-management), so scripts and agents can read current spend and the active budget, and `--pause` stops production deployments when spend hits 100%. It needs the Owner or Billing role and is free on Pro, which means an agent can finally check the meter before it ships.

AWS also showed [OpenSearch intelligent ingestion](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/big-data/build-rag-ready-indexes-from-amazon-s3-with-amazon-opensearch-intelligent-ingestion/), a serverless way to turn S3 data into RAG-ready indexes with one-off jobs or continuous streams, so the chunking and embedding pipeline is one less thing you run yourself.

A few quick ones. Baseten says Mirendil-Base-Large [scored the lowest bits per byte on its private codebases](https://x.com/baseten/status/2113746131025031324), and Baseten is its inference partner. NODECODE is an MIT-licensed [self-rewriting agent written in Lisp](https://x.com/pomterree/status/2113721985167728757) that runs in Discord and Telegram and is aimed at cyber work. TensorFold Memory claims [a million facts with millisecond lookup](https://x.com/petruspennanen/status/2113748933641961749), plus a proxy for llama.cpp, vLLM and Ollama. And Greg Isenberg's latest list of startup ideas includes [Google Analytics for agents](https://x.com/gregisenberg/status/2113721373482987970).

That's the cycle. Pin your containers to their own cores, turn off upload progress, and maybe give your agents a budget before they give you one. See you next time.
