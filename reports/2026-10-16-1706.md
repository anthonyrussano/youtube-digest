Anthropic's sharpest Claude Code trick yet: [let Opus 5.5 write the plan as a script and swarm Haiku 5.5 in parallel to execute it](https://x.com/mirku21/status/2111200540772221418). Meanwhile an OpenAI engineer showed [an agent that had already investigated a 3 a.m. page](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GBOZIRK1ujw&t=70s) before he got out of bed.

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

The Claude Code pattern is simple: [Opus 5.5 writes the entire plan as a script, and a swarm of cheaper agents carries it out](https://x.com/callanxai/status/2111195719927521372). Because the script holds the loop, Opus only sees the final diff, so [huge sweeps run with almost no Opus tokens burned](https://x.com/mirku21/status/2111200540772221418). The breathless "goldmine" framing is the sharers', not Anthropic's, but the idea stands on its own: put the expensive model on planning and let the script do the looping.

The Claude Code team also explained [how it does code review](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/16/code-review/). Addy Osmani, Morgan Westlee Lunt and Qing Wang call "did a human read the code?" an imprecise proxy for "what could go wrong with this change, and which check, automated or human, is the right one to catch it?" Review now runs continuously, like spellcheck while you write, and most changes are reviewed and fixed by Claude at least once before a human looks. Human attention goes to the higher-level questions.

OpenAI engineer Ali showed how he [puts his "dot" on call](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GBOZIRK1ujw), a persistent cloud agent with its own computer, access to his laptop and [its own identity in Slack](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GBOZIRK1ujw&t=25s). One prompt told it to [monitor the channel, triage every incoming page and open a thread to investigate](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GBOZIRK1ujw&t=47s). When a page hit at 3 a.m., the dot had [already found the problem and opened a Codex thread](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GBOZIRK1ujw&t=85s) he could steer from his phone, running on his laptop. He says the same setup can [triage a feedback channel or open PRs straight from Slack](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GBOZIRK1ujw&t=141s).

Jinjing Liang shared [a CI tip that she says cut about 600 hours of runs a month](https://x.com/JinjingLiang/status/2111211135542362334): use concurrency keys with `cancel-in-progress: true`, and stop running checks on draft PRs. Both are one-line changes an agent can make for you. Wasted CI minutes are the cheapest win in the pipeline.

Adib Hanna released [Muxin, one free, open-source app for all his computers](https://x.com/adibhanna/status/2111180686958981302): Macs, Linux servers, Windows PCs and cloud machines. He runs AI coding agents on all of them from one window, and his agents can drive it too. Fleet management for agents is turning into a desktop app.

Reflection engineer @nayshins introduced [Mirror, an open-source coding agent optimized for Beam](https://x.com/nayshins/status/2111189152117186956), after many iterations since joining the company. It installs with one command. Every model lab now wants its own coding harness.

AWS's sales operations team showed [how it keeps a fleet of sales agents honest with Amazon Bedrock AgentCore](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/building-trust-in-ai-agents-a-production-ready-evaluation-framework-with-amazon-bedrock-agentcore/). Before launch, one agent reported a customer's 410.08 dollars of revenue as "410.08 Million," invented an annual recurring revenue figure and presented its own math as sourced fact, all while monitoring stayed green. In one test, a data change, a tool change and a prompt update that landed apart dropped the pass rate from 78 percent to 38 percent. Their lesson: agents fail softly, so evaluation has to run continuously, not as a final QA step.

Aravind Srinivas says Perplexity [open-sourced two more Decision Models](https://x.com/AravSrinivas/status/2111181956444827695): pplx-decider-flash-v1.2 at 4B parameters and 1 cent per million tokens, and pplx-decider-v1.2 at 27B parameters and 2 cents, which he says ranks second overall on the HF Decision Index "for now." Cheap open routers make it easier to send each request to the right model.

E2B founder @mlejva published an essay titled ["Sandboxes are dead"](https://x.com/ethanwalkerman/status/2111176138051792913), striking from a company that has spun up more than 2 billion sandboxes and sees over 10 million monthly SDK downloads. The argument turns on the unit economics behind running agent code in isolation. When the sandbox leader questions sandboxes, agent infrastructure teams should read closely.

Anthropic faced pressure from two directions. New York City Comptroller Mark Levine is [urging Dario Amodei to delay Anthropic's IPO](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2111213177191166378), and Politico reports that Anthropic has [written its own bill to regulate frontier AI](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2111222136362897502) and is pitching it to members of Congress. The lab is now shaping the rules as well as the models.

AWS made [CloudWatch log centralization available in AWS GovCloud (US) and 13 more commercial Regions](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/amazon-cloudwatch-centralization-region-expansion). It copies logs from many accounts and Regions into one destination account through AWS Organizations, scoped to the whole org, specific OUs or selected accounts. The first copy is free and extra copies cost 5 cents per GB, so one pane of logs no longer needs custom plumbing.

In his latest BG2 interview, Jensen Huang [put NVIDIA's content at more than 40 billion dollars per gigawatt](https://x.com/ShanuMathew93/status/2111182292886380734), with frontier labs at about 25 to 30 percent of its business and baseline computing at roughly a third, growing 30 to 40 percent a year. Those are the numbers to check the AI buildout's revenue math against.

That's the cycle. Go add `cancel-in-progress` to your workflows, let the script hold the loop, and have a good weekend.
