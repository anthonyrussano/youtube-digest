A piece of software [became an AI agent's personal reverse engineer](https://x.com/0xCodila/status/2109623431763292649), and 2,576 people bookmarked the post. Meanwhile [a robot arm in Texas was driven live from New York](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sKHiuE7J-Y&t=3s) through a web browser.

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Google launched a [single, always-on Gemini agent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WuqwGPtZwvY&t=126s) at its [Gemini at Work 2026 keynote](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WuqwGPtZwvY). It follows a user across Docs, laptop, phone and the command line with one shared history, and because it runs in the cloud, a multi-hour task keeps going after the laptop goes to sleep. A ["dream mode"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WuqwGPtZwvY&t=248s) replays past sessions overnight to learn how the user works. Google also says agents will get [their own identity, an audit trail and hard real-time spend caps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WuqwGPtZwvY&t=982s), so the AI stops borrowing the user's credentials.

OpenAI put its proactive ChatGPT "dot" on [iOS and Android](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p5KxBTUncoE). It has [a name, an avatar and a computer of its own](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p5KxBTUncoE&t=0s). In the demo it [started a Codex prototype in the middle of a voice call](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p5KxBTUncoE&t=61s), then [drafted a Slack reply for the user to approve](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p5KxBTUncoE&t=121s). The assistant now does some of the work, and the user signs off on it. OpenAI also previewed [GPT-6 in ChatGPT with Intelligent UI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IsL4dVezs18).

A startup is chasing the same idea on the desktop. Aditya Vellanki introduced [Sentient OS](https://x.com/aditya_vellanki/status/2109699200682524921), on-device AI that he says "understands your whole life overnight" and works with its own cursor, backed by Y Combinator among others. People saved the post 271 times and liked it 259 times.

OpenAI is also bringing other companies' apps into the chat. [Plugin extensions for ChatGPT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LiNhqbetrbU) give a product its own sidebar, panel and @mentions, and they can [tell ChatGPT exactly what the user has selected](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LiNhqbetrbU&t=61s) in a design editor. It is all [built on MCP](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LiNhqbetrbU&t=121s), the same plumbing behind OpenAI's own Meetings sidebar.

OpenAI also says [code review is no longer the bottleneck](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IZXv6a78NDw). Codex now gets a review inbox for GitHub pull requests, and [cloud reviews run automatically](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IZXv6a78NDw&t=0s) and sync their comments back. Each PR gets [a private chat that knows the team's review guidelines](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IZXv6a78NDw&t=60s), plus an optional [local review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IZXv6a78NDw&t=120s). GitLab support is next, in preview.

In a two-hour live stream, GitHub's Wes Bos, Burke Holland and Pierce Boggan [had Copilot agents build a fake foldable-phone OS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ql_-cPc_ZYQ), starting with a [home screen and calculator](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ql_-cPc_ZYQ&t=367s) and [running several agents in parallel, each in its own worktree](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ql_-cPc_ZYQ&t=2562s). The hosts say the share of agent-written code they keep has jumped from about 50% to more than 90%, and the session cost 2,642 AI credits. Their tip is to get a ["rubber duck" second opinion from a model of a different family](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ql_-cPc_ZYQ&t=3653s), and to still read the code.

OpenAI's DevDay talk [*Stop Overpaying for Intelligence*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nmlHbSB8kM) argued that teams should [measure cost per task, not cost per token](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nmlHbSB8kM&t=74s), because a cheap model that needs human cleanup can cost more. OpenAI says prompt caching makes cached input up to 90% cheaper, and programmatic tool calling cut Clio's prompt tokens by 38%. One customer [went from 24% to 90% cache reuse](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nmlHbSB8kM&t=1041s) by keeping the start of the prompt stable. The talk recommends picking models from an [accuracy-versus-cost Pareto curve](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nmlHbSB8kM&t=316s).

GitHub's [Project HydraFusion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIHnmqYU614&t=0s) routes Copilot work across cloud models, and GitHub claims it cuts costs by up to 67%. Copilot will soon [auto-discover local models](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIHnmqYU614) from Ollama and Foundry Local and [send simple tasks to them at no AI-credit cost](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIHnmqYU614&t=124s), even offline. GitHub's suggested split is to [plan with Astra and implement locally](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIHnmqYU614&t=62s).

That push to run models locally collides with memory prices. ServeTheHome reviewed the [GMKtec EVO-X3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-nZiqOh-vEw), a Ryzen AI Max+ 395 box with up to 128 GB of unified memory that sells for about $3,599–3,799. That is cheap by comparison, because [a 128 GB DGX Spark runs about $7,000](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-nZiqOh-vEw&t=120s). It [usually draws under 100 W while running LLMs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-nZiqOh-vEw&t=853s), and the reviewer's [case for local AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-nZiqOh-vEw&t=1035s) is that work under NDA shouldn't go to the cloud.

Alex Ziskind and Wendell of Level1Techs [gave two AI supercomputers a real job](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9nKwDzsaAII): an 8× RTX Pro 6000 rig and an NVIDIA DGX Station running "Turnstone," a harness that [hands work to personas such as engineer, manager and scribe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9nKwDzsaAII&t=242s). The DGX Station [finished one job in 21 minutes versus 37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9nKwDzsaAII&t=1029s) on the other machine. The plan is a [smart orchestrator directing several cheaper local workers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9nKwDzsaAII&t=605s), so one desk can produce the output of a team.

Firecrawl [fine-tuned Leia](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SHoFr4D9cjM), a 421M-parameter open model, on a MacBook. It took [2,980 search results labeled by Opus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SHoFr4D9cjM&t=186s) and about 98 minutes on an M5 MacBook Pro, peaking at 21 GB of memory. Stock Leia [agreed with Claude only 44% of the time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SHoFr4D9cjM&t=124s), and [after fine-tuning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SHoFr4D9cjM&t=369s) it beat Jev head-to-head on this task while running much faster. Firecrawl also built ["FireIQ"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qhUU4vMKfm8&t=0s), a vidIQ clone made in a few hours with agents, which [scrapes YouTube pages to markdown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qhUU4vMKfm8&t=123s) and uses an [agent-written skill to score titles](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qhUU4vMKfm8&t=184s).

Google's [Gemma 4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fL9OBJO3Gu8&t=0s) open models run on everything from phones to workstation GPUs. One demo, ["PenguinAgent"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fL9OBJO3Gu8&t=61s), runs the 26B model offline for field research, and ["CodeBuddy"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fL9OBJO3Gu8&t=123s) reads a photo of handwritten Python and debugs it with no internet connection. Its sibling [EmbeddingGemma 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=anPsS6huQk0&t=0s) maps text, images, video and audio into one space with at most 740M parameters, using [768-dimension vectors that shrink to 128](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=anPsS6huQk0&t=61s), which is small enough for [private RAG on a phone](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=anPsS6huQk0&t=121s).

Neki Replicator [moves Postgres databases tens of terabytes in size](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fw2Jy6of7MI) at about 0.5–2 TB per hour per shard. It works around a known problem: [logical replication holds one snapshot for 20–40 hours](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fw2Jy6of7MI&t=124s) and bloats the WAL. Neki instead copies in [batches with snapshots of about 15 minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fw2Jy6of7MI&t=427s). The [cutover usually takes about five seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fw2Jy6of7MI&t=854s), is capped at 30, and reverts automatically if something goes wrong.

For smaller fires, AWS published a checklist for [slow PostgreSQL on RDS and Aurora](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URV5hiGHUE4). It starts with [CloudWatch metrics such as DiskQueueDepth](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URV5hiGHUE4&t=6s), then moves to [one-second Enhanced Monitoring and Top SQL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URV5hiGHUE4&t=138s), and finally checks [dead tuples, autovacuum and lock waits](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URV5hiGHUE4&t=201s). The rule is to scale up only after tuning. AWS also introduced [Lambda Web Functions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RDjKqsGVqwU).

Elastic built an [incident-investigator agent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HQqb_TmrMCc) that read live logs, metrics and traces and [found a latency regression in the frontend proxy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HQqb_TmrMCc&t=60s), showing every tool call along the way. It is [registered in Gemini Enterprise over A2A](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HQqb_TmrMCc&t=120s), and a [Cloud Run proxy keeps the API key server-side](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HQqb_TmrMCc&t=180s). Harness showed [deployments driven from the IDE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jX-EGkYIhsI): the agent [generated a canary-to-Kubernetes pipeline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jX-EGkYIhsI&t=9s), [diagnosed a failed deploy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jX-EGkYIhsI&t=69s) and [summarized trends across the last 20 deployments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jX-EGkYIhsI&t=132s).

Google Cloud showed [voice agents that use a real Chrome browser](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xDL5DFMo1-Y), [clicking by x/y coordinates over the DevTools Protocol](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xDL5DFMo1-Y&t=60s) from a fresh screenshot after every action. Slow actions [run in the background so the voice never goes silent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xDL5DFMo1-Y&t=184s). Anything irreversible [needs a human to confirm](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xDL5DFMo1-Y&t=246s), and the server enforces that with an action token rather than trusting the prompt.

Google Cloud's guide to [scaling agentic coding across a team](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TiH2E2mPZIk) comes down to three files: AGENTS.md, [DESIGN.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TiH2E2mPZIk&t=186s) and SKILL.md. In a benchmark the talk cites, having an AGENTS.md meant [less time, fewer tokens and smaller diffs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TiH2E2mPZIk&t=373s), and files of about 12 lines worked best. Personal preferences belong [at user scope, not project scope](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TiH2E2mPZIk&t=625s). Looker follows the same pattern with [five tools for agents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JvASK8qX4hg&t=0s). One warning: its [CLI acts with the exact permissions of its API key](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JvASK8qX4hg&t=122s), and enabling a tool on the [managed MCP server](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JvASK8qX4hg&t=243s) applies it to the whole instance.

Boundary and HumanLayer argued that [coding agents need a memory pipeline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mAKUL12h1n8). Theirs mines session transcripts for [user corrections and the agent's own fixes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mAKUL12h1n8&t=180s), and it caught one [BAML fact that tripped up two people across three sessions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mAKUL12h1n8&t=546s). They still don't know [how it compares with Cognition's approach](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mAKUL12h1n8&t=2729s).

On X, the most-saved study guide was Suraj Sharma's [six-month plan to become an AI engineer "in the Agentic Era"](https://x.com/suraj_sharma14/status/2109615202371252261), which starts with token math and context budgets. It was bookmarked 1,411 times but liked only 821, so far more people kept it than praised it. The REA post from the opening, by codila, [claims it lets an agent reverse engineer any app down to the binary](https://x.com/0xCodila/status/2109623431763292649) and rebuild paid features for free. It drew 2,576 saves to 1,512 likes, but the claims are unverified and the free-features pitch raises obvious licensing questions.

Other posts readers kept were reading lists. Daily Dose of Data Science listed [11 ways to evaluate an LLM](https://x.com/DailyDoseOfDS_/status/2109577335305596975), noting that no single metric tells you whether a system is good (302 saves, 231 likes). Leonard Rodman listed [ten open-source repos that turn AI into a researcher](https://x.com/RodmanAi/status/2109582143655731439), led by GPT Researcher (349 saves, 181 likes), and the 0xMarioNawfal account listed [six repos for shipping agents to production](https://x.com/RoundtableSpace/status/2109815130720547079), including awesome-mcp-servers. Kushal Gangil found ["AI Engineering from Scratch"](https://x.com/techyoutbe/status/2109884437160960457), a free course he describes as 503 lessons and about 320 hours. It was saved 13 times and liked only 4, the widest gap in the set.

A few more saves came from launches and research. Mori's article on using [Grok Bot and Opus 5.5 as a 24/7 engineering team](https://x.com/0xjmori/status/2109648700897116554), with agents that fix GitHub issues and prepare pull requests overnight, got 73 saves to 45 likes. Andriy Burkov noted that LLMs now rely on reinforcement learning and a ["mid-training" phase on expert data](https://x.com/burkov/status/2109840044986908812) to handle code and multi-step reasoning. Ruben Hassid's tip that [Google is giving away AI courses with certificates](https://x.com/rubenhassid/status/2109608474523140596) collected 48 saves.

Even OpenAI's community surprised its organizers. The [Codex Ambassador program](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=enPbh_A5F7E) was planned for 15–20 people and now has [more than 150 ambassadors in 80+ countries](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=enPbh_A5F7E&t=61s), [including one who ran a meetup aboard a moving metro in Kochi](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=enPbh_A5F7E&t=122s). The [main requirements to join](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=enPbh_A5F7E&t=243s) are curiosity and generosity with your time. At [Oracle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ju-JyxDgV7g), business users skip the report request altogether: Codex plans the work, calls MCP services and returns an analysis, a report or even an app.

Jeff Geerling says [Qualcomm made a Pi 5 killer, and good luck getting one](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KUUuxkICOLw). The $209 Radxa Dragon Q8B is about twice as fast as a Pi 5 on multi-core work, but its [OS images are confusing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KUUuxkICOLw&t=63s) and [uploads cap near 200 Mbps on a 2.5G port](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KUUuxkICOLw&t=246s). His order [took three months to arrive](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KUUuxkICOLw&t=491s).

Michael Klements found that a [$40 travel router](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QiTA53pBQjM), the GL.iNet Mango 2, draws under 5 W over USB-C and can [fail over between Wi-Fi, Ethernet, a phone or a cellular modem](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QiTA53pBQjM&t=185s). From a fake hotel network he [reached his home Jellyfin, Immich and Nextcloud over Tailscale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QiTA53pBQjM&t=247s). The [VPN benchmarks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QiTA53pBQjM&t=368s) had a clear winner: WireGuard reached about 120 Mbps, while OpenVPN managed only 14–18.

ExplainingComputers made the case for [encrypting your data](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2FNhbXc2Yzk), noting that full-disk encryption also keeps browser session cookies from being stolen. Windows Device Encryption [needs a TPM and a Microsoft account to hold the recovery key](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2FNhbXc2Yzk&t=247s). On Linux, [turn encryption on at install time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2FNhbXc2Yzk&t=490s), and for portable drives, [VeraCrypt works on every platform](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2FNhbXc2Yzk&t=794s).

On *Machine Learning Street Talk*, Akarsh Kumar argued that [evolution is "anything but random"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=615B5nyMFPk). By [keeping partial solutions, it turns exponential search into roughly linear search](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=615B5nyMFPk&t=187s). His ASAL work [searches a whole space of simulated worlds for the interesting ones](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=615B5nyMFPk&t=1844s), and newer work [pits programs against each other in an open-ended arms race](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=615B5nyMFPk&t=2584s).

Julia Turc traced [70 years of rebranding intelligence](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jh9LF6Lc3Vs), starting from an [executive order that renames AI "superintelligence"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jh9LF6Lc3Vs&t=0s) for federal agencies. McCarthy [coined "artificial intelligence" in 1955](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jh9LF6Lc3Vs&t=186s) partly to avoid the word cybernetics, and [backpropagation in 1986 revived connectionism](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jh9LF6Lc3Vs&t=625s), later rebranded as deep learning. Each new name, she argues, has marked a fresh start.

A knitter used Gemini to [hide the message "Look what you can do" inside a knitting pattern](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pijSNA-hWkg&t=0s) using carrier matrices. DeepMind's ["Gemini Audio at Night"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lTQHImoeuEY&t=1s) added TTS with emotion and pacing, plus live translation into about 100 languages. And that robot arm in Texas runs on a Gemini-written script that turns the joint positions streamed from New York into motor commands.

That's the cycle. Go encrypt a drive, check your cache hit rate, and maybe bookmark this one.
