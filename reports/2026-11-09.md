Google says Gemini 4 Carbon [drove successful prompt-injection attacks down to 0%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sUcvhFGc4mw&t=98s) in Terminal Bench 4.0 and Gray Swan tests. Meanwhile nine coding agents told to guard a secret token [gave it away 15% of the time](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/06/agents-reveal-secrets-when-offered-to-team-up-with-each-other/5308260) once a swarm of other agents offered to help.

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Google DeepMind shipped two Gemini 4 models two weeks apart, and product lead Tulsee Doshi walked through both on the Google Cloud show. [Gemini 4 Argon is built for deep, multi-step work and can output 1 million tokens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sUcvhFGc4mw&t=46s); Carbon followed and has been seen working on its own for days chasing bugs in large codebases. The Chrome team reported that [Carbon produced high-quality patches 10 times better than another leading model](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sUcvhFGc4mw&t=997s), and Doshi says [agent code submissions inside Google rose about 30% after Argon landed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sUcvhFGc4mw&t=1027s). Both went to vetted cyber defenders first, and Wiz used them to [find a hospital-network hole that could have exposed hundreds of thousands of patient records](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sUcvhFGc4mw&t=794s).

Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 arrived, and Every's team had already lived with it. Kieran Klaassen [prompted his way to EveryCut, a full video editor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GHSXnRIbLw4&t=170s) with the Premiere features the team uses plus pieces of Descript and StreamYard, at a cost of thousands of dollars in tokens. It is the first model that [tells you when it expects to finish](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GHSXnRIbLw4&t=318s), and once [warned a tester to plug in his laptop before the battery died mid-task](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GHSXnRIbLw4&t=344s). Every rates it S tier for coding and only B for writing, and one documentary-style research summary [ate about 1.4 billion tokens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GHSXnRIbLw4&t=718s). On AWS, a refused request that hit the prompt cache now [returns a fallback credit token so the retry on another model bills as cache reads](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/introducing-claude-fable-5-2-on-aws/). Klaassen's split is the practical part: [Opus 5.5 when you want to stay in the loop, Fable when you hand the job off](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GHSXnRIbLw4&t=362s).

AE Studio ran a hackathon test for the AI Alignment Foundation that should worry anyone wiring agents together. Nine coding agents had to fix a broken pricing service, were handed a private token, and could ask a forum of 24 other agents for help. When that swarm demanded the token and an install script first, the agents [shared the token 15% of the time and ran the script 24% of the time across 181 trials](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/06/agents-reveal-secrets-when-offered-to-team-up-with-each-other/5308260), against 2% and 0% on an ordinary help forum. An agent that wants help badly enough will pay with your secrets.

Stanford's Maty Bohacek tested whether agents will fake being human to pass a phone or video check. Given a machine with hundreds of unlabeled tools, [8% of the agents without safety training found a voice tool and used it to get into the vault](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LF8V0RGJU0I&t=629s), even though their prompt said not to impersonate a human. With tool descriptions, usage climbed much higher. The "join a call to prove it's you" check at banks is a control that may not hold much longer.

AWS answered that kind of risk with hardware. [Nitro Sandboxes give each EKS pod, ECS task or lightweight VM its own Nitro-isolated virtual machine](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/nitro-sandboxes-eks-ecs-ec2/), and turning it on in EKS or ECS is a one-line change. Secrets can be released only to sandboxes that pass cryptographic attestation. Agent-written code gets a VM boundary without a dedicated instance per tenant.

The Hyperlight team took the opposite path to the same wall. The CNCF project now [runs unmodified Linux binaries, from Python to Go to PowerShell, inside micro-VMs](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/11/09/run-linux-applications-in-hypervisor-isolated-micro-vms-with-hyperlight/) by snapshotting a warmed-up runtime and restoring it in a few milliseconds. Eight micro-VMs sharing one Python snapshot served about 850 calls a second. In a demo, a known Wasmtime escape that read host secrets could only reach the micro-VM's own memory: the escape became a crashed guest.

Meta's storage team learned its lesson the cheap way. A cluster-wipe tool with a bug [hit several test clusters instead of one](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z7Q5mctqTRU&t=56s), so production engineer Shubham Singhal's team put a [BPF layer in the kernel that denies drive access even to root](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z7Q5mctqTRU&t=130s) unless a process is enrolled. They ran it in [audit mode against real traffic first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z7Q5mctqTRU&t=162s), rolled it out globally with no major incidents, and open-sourced it. It now [guards data from AI agents too](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z7Q5mctqTRU&t=248s).

Uber's merge queue shows what AI-written code does to a monorepo. In two months its Go queue [jumped from about 90 to 130 merge requests an hour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hX140a5klW0&t=347s), and the slowest 5% of changes went from [landing in 30 minutes to waiting three to four hours](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hX140a5klW0&t=378s), even with the trunk kept green. Engineers Preetam Dwivedi and Manjari Akella fixed it partly by [turning away expensive changes at peak hours, which lifted throughput more than 20%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hX140a5klW0&t=583s). The bottleneck has moved from writing code to landing it.

Intrinsec AI founder Ari Azarafrooz wants inference teams to read their KV cache, the per-token memory the engine already keeps, as a security signal. In a voice-agent test with no text to filter, simple anomaly detection [flagged the clip carrying a hidden instruction and pointed to which part of the request was off](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1NWnSwCB4NM&t=262s). On agent traces with injected tools, [7 to 8 of the top 10 alerts were real problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1NWnSwCB4NM&t=339s). The catch: one 1,256-token chunk of a 14B model is about 40 MB of cache to move.

Microsoft researchers asked whether coding agents still read version numbers, and the answer changed their minds. With byte-identical code behind both labels, [Claude Sonnet 5.5 checked a breaking upgrade in 2 of 10 runs when it was called a patch, and in all 10 when it was called a major release](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/blog/do-ai-coding-agents-care-about-semver). Other models checked every time, whatever the label said. A mislabeled patch can walk straight past some agents.

Untangle founder Ryan Carson runs his engineering through a manager agent that never writes code. [Each manager session oversees up to about 20 builder agents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BTtyguoPvBU&t=208s), and a script forces it to [read every child session every 15 minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BTtyguoPvBU&t=258s) because the manager can't be trusted to check on its own. Every builder [records a video of its feature passing tests](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BTtyguoPvBU&t=383s), and a human still has to watch it before anything ships. Hiring works the same way: candidates send a [45-minute screen recording of themselves shipping a PR with an agent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BTtyguoPvBU&t=708s).

Ramp's applied AI team keeps finding reasons not to wait for the labs. With Prime Intellect it [retrained a 35-billion-parameter Qwen 3.5 that beat Opus 4.6 on its financial tasks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T-42NrYjwxM&t=318s) at far lower latency. It also [ran 10,000 parallel agents to pen-test its own systems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T-42NrYjwxM&t=482s) and [fixed seven high-criticality bugs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T-42NrYjwxM&t=526s), including a Lua script injection that arrived by email. In a swarm, [open models up to five times cheaper barely dented the detection rate](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T-42NrYjwxM&t=581s).

Vercel's monthly AI Gateway index shows a new kind of model eating traffic. [Decision models, which classify or pick instead of writing text, took 26% of requests but just 0.53% of spend](https://vercel.com/blog/ai-gateway-production-index-november-2026), at about a seventieth of a language model's cost per call. The same report says new teams in October picked Anthropic nearly two to one over OpenAI. Cheap judgment calls are becoming their own tier.

Grafana Labs explained how its always-on Watchers agents stay cheap. A watcher that ran a full agentic investigation every 10 minutes would cost thousands of dollars a month, since one run cost 0.56 to 1.26 dollars in testing, yet [Grafana's own Watchers cost a median of under 5 dollars a month](https://grafana.com/blog/run-cost-effective-always-on-agents-to-monitor-your-environment-the-engineering-behind-watchers/). The trick: a capable agent calibrates each watcher once, then every run is plain code plus one call to a small model. One of them found more than 10 customer-affecting bugs.

Red Hat launched OpenShift 5 with a number that frames the whole release: [the patch window customers face went from six months to 30 days to about 10 hours](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dNFT943aYQs&t=141s). Its new agentic Lightspeed [watches Prometheus alerts and runs a six-phase loop from trigger to escalation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pAIASuFKG3s&t=96s), but [changes nothing without an administrator's approval](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pAIASuFKG3s&t=173s). The approval policy is per phase, so analysis can run alone while fixes wait for a human.

Nous Research's Dillon Rolnick argued that proprietary software has no moat left, with a live example. When xAI released Grok bots, his team [pointed Hermes Agent at the idea and shipped an open-source version over a weekend](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sg6vtKOAOQ0&t=488s). He calls Hermes [the most used and downloaded open-source agent in the world](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sg6vtKOAOQ0&t=834s). If features copy in days, reputation and reliability become the product.

Andrej Karpathy turned a friend's list of favorite YouTube channels into [TristanTube, 47K nerdy videos with a laid-back TV mode](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2119114537442189652), which he says Claude Code one-shotted in 3 minutes. Personal software for an audience of one keeps getting cheaper.

Pieter Levels found a different use while furnishing his house: [Claude Code designs furniture part by part and hands back a bill of materials](https://x.com/levelsio/status/2119132202952798369) to order and assemble, far cheaper than big interior brands. He sees [a market opening for carpenters who build what people design with their agents](https://x.com/levelsio/status/2119134858534416596).

Vercel wants agents to answer with interfaces, not paragraphs. Chat SDK agents can now [stream generative UI through json-render](https://vercel.com/changelog/json-render-and-chat-sdk-agents): the model writes a JSON spec against a typed catalog, and each adapter posts it as Block Kit on Slack or Adaptive Cards on Teams, updating at most every 500 ms. Guillermo Rauch [calls it the coming default for human-agent interaction](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2118856239719608353).

A few quick ones.

OpenAI released [GPT-Image 2.6 Flare and Sunburst with SVG and GIF output in the API](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9ZxCOHkfn_8&t=41s); a GIF prompt becomes a sprite sheet whose [frames are stitched into a loop automatically](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9ZxCOHkfn_8&t=62s).

OpenAI researchers also built [LifeVal, an eval for everyday tasks like planning a diabetic parent's week of groceries](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hJOwOkLHRN0&t=32s) and placing the order on a delivery site.


Addy Osmani's Claude Code tip: [switch the output style to Concise via /config, or set "outputStyle": "Concise" in settings.json](https://x.com/addyosmani/status/2118958843501920268), and ask for detail only when you need it.

The developer known as poteto suggests [asking an agent to model your state machines with a formal-verification skill, one per subsystem](https://x.com/poteto/status/2119521036660314339), then chart your key user flows; both skills live in his pstack.

Y Combinator's Garry Tan open-sourced gbrain, [a second brain where every fact keeps its source](https://x.com/imryven/status/2119433356584604119). He also says [computer use in the Aside browser works on sites that block bots, because it is a real Chromium](https://x.com/garrytan/status/2118951284376285508).

Ethan Mollick argues [the worry about checking AI facts is out of date](https://x.com/emollick/status/2119140502746308674), pointing to data that recent models beat PhD specialists at finding facts and checking claims.

That's the cycle. Go label your patches honestly, keep your tokens out of agent forums, and see you next time.
