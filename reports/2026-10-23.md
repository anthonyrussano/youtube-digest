Anthropic says Claude wrote [a 630,000-line proof for cJSON's 391-line parser entry point](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formal-verification) to show that no input under 64 MB can crash it. And a San Francisco lab [gave a human patient a cocktail of AI-designed viruses](https://www.theregister.com/science/2026/10/22/human-receives-ai-designed-virus-for-the-first-time/5304874), built to kill the bacteria behind an antibiotic-resistant infection.

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Anthropic's Frontier Red Team, working with Theorem, says Claude [proved that 239 entry points across 123 OSS-Fuzz libraries cannot crash](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formal-verification) on any input under 64 MB. The proofs cover the compiled RISC-V binaries, not the source, and add up to about 180 million lines of Rocq; a typical proof runs about 600,000 lines for a binary with a median of 18,000 instructions. On the way, Claude found and patched real bugs, including an out-of-bounds write that only fired on about 20 MB inputs with one rare layout, and it rediscovered CVE-2026-56132 in libexpat. For scale, the post notes that seL4 took two person-years to build and 20 to verify.

The part most coverage skipped is who decides what "correct" means. Theorem's [OSS Verify program](https://github.com/theorem-labs/oss-verify) proves theorems about a project's own fuzz targets, so [the fuzz target is the spec](https://github.com/theorem-labs/oss-verify/blob/main/docs/what-gets-proven.md): a harness that does `if (decompress(compress(x)) != x) abort();` turns a no-crash proof into a round-trip proof for every input. Its guide warns against `assert()`, because Release builds define NDEBUG and the check silently vanishes, and against returning -1 for a failed property, because -1 counts as a pass. Maintainers enroll by pointing a coding agent at the repo's AGENTS.md, and findings go privately to the security contact with no disclosure deadline.

The same Anthropic post has two more striking results. A Python interpreter written in Rust and verified with Verus [matched CPython on 99.95% of 1.7 million random programs](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formal-verification), and of seven bugs deliberately injected, five survived random tests while all seven broke the proof. Opus 5.5 also proves half of 36 small verification tasks with about 40% fewer tokens than Opus 5, and agents sharing one proof workspace needed 17% fewer tokens than agents working alone.

Shreya Shankar told dbt Labs' Tristan Handy that [data agents still analyze data badly](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k5YL1NmhwFY). In her benchmark, agents [pull whole tables into pandas and join them there](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k5YL1NmhwFY&t=1463s) instead of pushing the work down to the database. The benchmark took eight months to build, and in the updated paper [the best agent reaches 61% pass@1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k5YL1NmhwFY&t=1629s); the leaderboard's 80s come from per-query prompt overfitting. She also found that AI SQL features [send one HTTP request per row to the model](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k5YL1NmhwFY&t=2900s), which is why she is building a custom inference engine for them.

Her side quest is the funniest experiment of the day. Shankar put a hidden note to AI agents on her website, and [agent-written emails dropped from about five a day to about three a week](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k5YL1NmhwFY&t=136s). Only one of them actually used her planted phrase. It is a tiny, real measurement of how many inbox "humans" are agents reading the page.

Firecrawl tested how agents handle poisoned web pages. In its demo, [hidden instructions on a fake pricing page](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YzLR8X3_5gw&t=76s) didn't fool a Sonnet-class model, but another model fell for them after just two runs, and a CSS-hidden trap in a GitHub issue got two models to [read a .env key](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YzLR8X3_5gw&t=113s), though none sent it out. It also cites Zscaler research where docs for a Python library [told models to pay 3 dollars to Stripe for a fake API key, and 4 of 26 models did](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YzLR8X3_5gw&t=167s). The pitch, promotional but concrete, is a scrape option that [runs a classifier and returns a 403](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YzLR8X3_5gw&t=190s) before the page ever reaches the agent.

WorkOS product manager Isabelle Williams showed how to [let AI agents sign up for your app safely](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uTrY8slys0w). Her demo app publishes an [Auth MD file, an open standard written for agents rather than people](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uTrY8slys0w&t=147s), that explains how to get access; an agent can try things anonymously and gets a [claim token so the human can later take over its work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uTrY8slys0w&t=245s). The key design rule: never hand the agent the user's own token. Instead the app issues an agent token whose [scopes are the overlap of the user's rights and a per-agent ceiling](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uTrY8slys0w&t=860s), so it can be revoked without touching the user's session.

Security researcher Donato Capitella weighed in on [the US DeepSeek ban and model backdoors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MPj-puRyPTM). His point: a hosted API can [quietly route your request to a malicious model](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MPj-puRyPTM&t=202s) that slips vulnerabilities into code or appends malware to bash tool calls, while open weights in SafeTensors mostly retire the old pickle risk. Behavioral backdoors with a trigger are ["trivial" to train](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MPj-puRyPTM&t=472s), though he has seen no evidence of one in DeepSeek. His fix is boring and right: [use models from official labs or known quantizers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MPj-puRyPTM&t=824s), run them in containers, and give agents keys that can only push to forks or branches.

Researchers at Binghamton University showed what that kind of backdoor can do in a robot. Their MuTRAP attack [ties different malicious plans to different trigger words](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/22/backdoored-robot-tries-its-hand-at-finger-chopping-and-cat-cooking/5304870) during domain fine-tuning, so one model plotted "grab knife, cut hand" for one trigger and putting a cat on a stove for another (in a plastic-fruit demo and a simulation). They tested it across seven planners from GPT-2 to Qwen2.5-7B and propose PROTEA, an LLM judge that checks task plans, as the defense.

Stephanie Jarmak of Omni Analytics, a former astronomer, gave a talk on [not trusting your agents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A3FL_zTQKU0). On the same 63 Terminal Bench tasks, [swapping only the harness flipped which model won](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A3FL_zTQKU0&t=196s): Claude Opus 5 led GPT-6 Astra 36 to 31 in OpenHands, then trailed 19 to 38 in Pi. And one grep-happy agent [pulled 295 files when it needed six](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A3FL_zTQKU0&t=552s). Her scariest example came from researchers who seeded security issues for an agent: a tamper hash check [passed everything and caught none of them](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A3FL_zTQKU0&t=739s), while an independent record of the traffic caught them all. And a judge with a 1% false-alarm rate [can create 10,000 human reviews](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A3FL_zTQKU0&t=680s).

Evan Conrad of San Francisco Compute argued that [AI startups will disappear without compute markets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wk2bUUGFC_o). The deposit to start a lab [went from 10 million dollars to 100 million to a billion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wk2bUUGFC_o&t=836s), because lenders can't underwrite a two-person team promising superintelligence. In his telling, [AI hits the credit wall before it hits the power wall](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wk2bUUGFC_o&t=501s). His company started as a music-model lab that nearly went bankrupt on its own cluster and survived by subleasing it, which made it the first GPU broker by accident.

Microsoft and Hugging Face turned database grading into RL rewards with [ThinkingBox-Training](https://huggingface.co/blog/microsoft/thinkingbox-training). After 50 GRPO steps, a 27B Qwen model's pass@1 rose from 51.70% to 60.78%, and the number of tasks it solved on all 20 attempts went from 38 to 107. In one hotel-booking task it went from changing bookings without approval in 15 of 20 attempts to keeping the state intact in 20 of 20. The lesson is consistency: Kimi-K3 solved 93.89% of tasks at least once, but only 13.41% on every attempt.

AWS made [Continuum for code vulnerabilities generally available](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/aws-continuum-for-code-vulnerabilities-is-now-generally-available). It builds a context graph of your code, validates exploits in a sandbox, and opens pull requests that come with the proof. A Learn mode watches before an Enforce mode starts blocking, the kind of gradual rollout security teams actually adopt.

Andrew Curran reported that [Quest Diagnostics was hacked and patient data exposed](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2113437710065311970); the lab serves roughly half of US hospitals and physicians, and the investigation is ongoing. The same day Matt Shumer wrote [a plain-language security explainer meant to be sent to family](https://x.com/mattshumer_/status/2113363050745217364), and Gergely Orosz predicted [secure software will get a lot more expensive](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2113357337926779093), mostly because AI-native engineers will cost more than today's. Vercel's Guillermo Rauch called for the industry to [kill static credentials and move to OIDC](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2113399539227136084), so thieves could only steal "self-destructing keys." And Pieter Levels resurfaced his advice to [pull your VPS off the public internet](https://x.com/levelsio/status/2113587042185576853) and behind Tailscale and a Cloudflare Tunnel, because SSH key login does nothing against exploits in the services you expose.

Ethan Mollick shared a study where [Opus 4.8 beat human experts as peer reviewers](https://x.com/emollick/status/2113483068174692831) on 124 Harvard social-science papers, from statistics to formal models to qualitative work. His takeaway is that academics need to adapt how science is reviewed, and fast. He also shared [a useful prompt for Codex with computer use](https://x.com/emollick/status/2113361252173091114).

@poteto is building [a companion skill that draws statecharts and checks them against your formal-verification specs](https://x.com/poteto/status/2113398433977659654), rendered with Steph Ango's Pele. It pairs with the same proof-minded trend as the lead story: the agent draws the behavior, then checks it against the spec.

Joshua Warren [plugged an AMD Radeon RX 7600 into a MacBook Pro over Thunderbolt](https://x.com/JoshuaSWarren/status/2113424762617610335), got the driver and Vulkan up under Omarchy Linux, and ran Qwen2.5-7B on it with llama.cpp. Apple Silicon has long shut out eGPUs, and he says Intel and NVIDIA cards are next.

A developer writing a container runtime for fun explained [why Docker is weird](https://codeandcake.dev/posts/2026-10-22-docker-is-weird). Go is multi-threaded and can't really fork, so runc runs a C module before Go's runtime starts and then re-executes /proc/self/exe to fake a fork. Rootless runtimes, meanwhile, have to rebuild networking in userspace.

Linus Torvalds says [AI is fine for competent kernel developers](https://www.theregister.com/columnists/2026/10/23/torvalds-ai-is-fine-for-competent-devs-but-spare-us-the-drive-by-patches/5304357), and maintainers at the Prague summit decided to deprecate the "Assisted-by" tag, so patches no longer have to disclose AI use. What he wants to stop is the clueless "drive-by" patch from people who never meant to learn the code. Greg Kroah-Hartman adds that AI bug reports flipped from obvious slop to real, good reports almost overnight, which now buries maintainers in valid work.

A few quick ones. Ramp explained [why it built its own agent harness](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2113435080278360133), which Elvis Saravia says more hardcore teams are realizing they must own. Rauch also pitched [tmp.now deploys](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2113357674989650307), throwaway hosting so agents can ship just-in-time frontends with no signup. Aravind Srinivas says [the Perplexity Decision Model now runs fully locally on a Mac](https://x.com/AravSrinivas/status/2113420151232352444). Santiago Valdarrama pointed to [a model specialized for posters and marketing art](https://x.com/svpino/status/2113351794302832824) and [an agent platform that drafts proposals for freelancers](https://x.com/svpino/status/2113362839666876437). A CNCF walkthrough built [isolated multi-tenant GPU Kubernetes on a single 4-core, 8 GB machine](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/10/23/multi-tenant-gpu-kubernetes-on-simulated-hardware-with-kamaji-and-hami/) with Kamaji control planes and HAMi GPU slicing on mock A100 and L4 cards, so a pod needing 4 GB no longer blocks a whole 80 GB card. Jeff Geerling took apart [Ubiquiti's 99-dollar PoE air-quality sensor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqBD8M_uYnE&t=229s), which uses a real CO2 sensor but won't hand its data to anything outside Ubiquiti's system. And Pedro Domingos offered ["yet more evidence" that RL is "basically useless"](https://x.com/pmddomingos/status/2113376989826654494), on a day when the ThinkingBox numbers argued the opposite.

That's the cycle. Check your fuzz harness for assert(), keep your agent's keys on a short leash, and see you tomorrow.
