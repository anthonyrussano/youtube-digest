OpenAI now tucks [500 dollars of API credits a month into ChatGPT Pro 500](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ujmyzWSj8IU&t=127s), so the subscription you already pay for can fund the app you keep meaning to build. Meanwhile Google and XREAL started taking orders for [the first Android XR glasses, with Gemini reading everything in view](https://x.com/testingcatalog/status/2115571049794253233), laptop windows included.

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

ChatGPT Pro and Business plans now come with monthly API credits that work across the whole OpenAI platform: [200 dollars on Pro 200 and 500 dollars on Pro 500](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ujmyzWSj8IU&t=127s). You link an API organization under Billing in the ChatGPT app and you're done. In the demo, the OpenAI Developers plugin in Codex [sets up an API key for one Mac app](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ujmyzWSj8IU&t=75s), wires in the Decisions API to judge a draft message, and calls GPT-6.1 Terra to suggest the edit, all billed to the plan.

Kun Chen argues that once agents write code nobody reviews line by line, CI is the bottleneck, and he has three fixes. His cross-platform project [skips most mac and windows tests in CI and lets CD catch the rare platform-only bug](https://x.com/kunchenguid/status/2115565245293039814), which made CI 15x faster. Modular code means CI runs only on the modules a change touches, and test selection with a nightly loop that audits past runs got him 20x faster CI that almost never misses a real problem.

AWS made the Amazon Bedrock AgentCore CLI generally available as [@aws/agentcore 1.0.0](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/amazon-bedrock-agentcore-cli-version-1-0-available/), rebuilt for faster startup and a consistent command structure. You can now work with harnesses, runtimes, memory, identity, gateways and payments without adding them to a project first, invoke resources by ID or ARN, and drop into an interactive shell. Global flags cover region, JSON output and debug, and `agentcore migrate` moves existing projects over.

Keycard's Kamil Potrec explains why handing an agent your personal access token is like leaving your laptop unlocked at the office. The MCP server on the other end [can't tell whether Alice or her agent sent the invoice](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=58ulJViGFgU&t=210s). His answer is to keep credentials out of the agent's reach entirely: a gateway that injects them per request gives you [instant revocation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=58ulJViGFgU&t=841s) and can enforce rules like ["you can read my email, but you cannot send my email"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=58ulJViGFgU&t=863s). The cost is an extra hop and one very attractive target.

XREAL AURA is the first pair of Android XR glasses, built with Google, and Gemini works across [everything you can see, laptop windows included](https://x.com/testingcatalog/status/2115571049794253233). Orders opened today in the US, Canada and Korea, with Japan following on November 2, and prices start at 1,099 dollars.

The money side had a rougher month. Peter Diamandis and his co-hosts walked through a sell-off in which [CoreWeave and Nebius fell 30%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WXELsIk4o0g&t=979s), triggered by CoreWeave's 51 billion dollars of debt and the worry that 2027 will bring stranded, under-earning compute. On the same episode Emad Mostaque pointed to a line in Anthropic's S-1: the company [banned 7% of its user accounts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WXELsIk4o0g&t=3407s), which he reads as the cost of fighting distillation.

CNCF's Volcano scheduler reached [v1.16 with a NamespaceQueue and a Slurm-inspired fairshare plugin](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/10/28/volcano-v1-16-released-namespacequeue-fairshare-and-a-broader-hypernode-ecosystem/). A new Unschedulable Job Cache remembers why a Job failed to place and skips repeat checks, with a watchdog that retries after 5 minutes; it is off by default and switched on with `--set custom.scheduler_feature_gates="UnschedulableJobCache=true"`. The release also adds Kubernetes 1.36 support, topology anti-affinity across supernodes and DRA scoring.

LangChain gave its marketing team a coding agent and treated it as a knowledge worker. The [Paid Media Agent](https://www.langchain.com/blog/paid-media-agent) helped take paid media from 0 to 20% of pipeline in six months, and cost per lead fell 30% from June to August while spend rose about 60%. The biggest win was boring: moving calculations out of the model and into code made one reporting job about 40x cheaper and went from 18 minutes to 85 seconds. It runs on Deep Agents in a LangSmith sandbox, reports in Slack every Monday, and is now open source.

Supabase launches about 5 million databases a month, and Latent Space asked [why agents keep choosing it](https://latent.space/p/supabase/). Swyx's tracker found frontier models picking it first for app backends almost every time. New pieces include an alpha runtime that runs Supabase locally without Docker, declarative schemas kept in Git, which the team calls the Terraform moment for the database, and Supabase Compute in private alpha for running containers next to your data.

Runpod's Chris Vela says most teams serving open models are guessing at their vLLM config. Kimi K3, he notes, [won't run on CUDA 12.8 and needs 13.0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P_0_Th_ZzIg&t=427s), and in his benchmarks raising max batch tokens [barely moved time to first token but cut inter-token latency](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P_0_Th_ZzIg&t=651s). He also reminds people that [a B300 node costs about 40,000 dollars a month](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P_0_Th_ZzIg&t=879s), while DeepSeek on four H200s does fine for most jobs. His Benchpod template is due at the end of the month.

OpenAI's Ted Sanders cleared up the model names. The order is [Luna, then Terra, then Sol, then Astra](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/28/ted-sanders/), and size explains most of the difference between them. A "6.1" comes after 5.6, shares a lineage with the other 6.1 models, and wasn't a big enough jump to earn a 7.

Hermes agent users who want memory now have [24 plugins to pick from in the official catalog](https://x.com/HermesWatcher/status/2115548343648821731), including Hindsight, Honcho, mem0, OpenViking, Supermemory and Cognee. They fall into three groups: local and private stores, notes and wiki setups such as the official Memory Wiki, and cloud services you can share across machines.

A leak exposed [more than 41,000 Homeland Security employees and contractors](https://www.wired.com/story/massive-leak-exposes-41000-homeland-security-workers-including-undercover-ice-agents/) across seven agencies, including undercover ICE agents, with records last updated August 19. DDoSecrets says the data came from DHS's Okta login system. Okta says its platform is secure and shows no sign of compromise.

Jitendra Malik's Berkeley course Robots That Learn is [free online as 15 lectures, about 15 hours](https://x.com/0x_meden/status/2115559534349488606), and 14 of them have fewer than 1,000 views. Malik is now a VP at Amazon's Frontier AI and Robotics lab. Lecture 9 covers RMA, and lecture 11 shows two robot arms tying a shoelace.

Google's Sprout team took two weeks to build a houseplant that drives itself to the window. It's a [TurtleBot 4 base, a Pixel 11 for a face and a real Monstera](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ID6vEy94j7c&t=19s), using Gemini Flash for vision, Gemini Live for its voice and Nano Banana to sketch the casing. A sensor in the pot tells it when it's thirsty, and it [tells plant jokes on request](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ID6vEy94j7c&t=106s).

That's the cycle. Go water something, before it learns to drive off and find someone who will.
