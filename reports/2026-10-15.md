Anthropic told investors [Q3 revenue hit $16.8bn](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/15/anthropic-tells-investors/), up nearly 50% from $11.5bn in Q2. Meanwhile a study of [18 frontier models found they all got less reliable as the context window got bigger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BwXremQ-i6I&t=36s).

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Anthropic's investor day leaked through people with knowledge of the matter, and Simon Willison pulled the numbers that matter. [Q1 was about $4.8bn, Q2 $11.5bn, and Q3 $16.8bn](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/15/anthropic-tells-investors/). In Q3 the company said it made [$1.70 in revenue for every $1 spent on computing power](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/15/anthropic-tells-investors/), with adjusted operating margins of 11% excluding stock-based pay. Distillation — rivals training on Anthropic outputs — was [5% to 7% of revenue before they cut it back "materially" in Q3](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/15/anthropic-tells-investors/). If that share held on Q2 alone, other labs spent over half a billion dollars that quarter just to distill Claude.

Modal just crossed into decacorn territory. Latent Space reports a [$16B Series D on about $1B ARR](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-modals-1b-arr-16b-series-d), with usage and valuation each roughly tripling in five months. Sandboxes alone are a [$300M run-rate product](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-modals-1b-arr-16b-series-d); the company has spun up close to [3 billion sandboxes](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-modals-1b-arr-16b-series-d) and says they scale to a million concurrent. That is the infra layer agents lean on when they need short-lived machines by the million.

IBM's Martin Keen walks through why stuffing more tokens into an agent often makes it worse. A recent study of [18 frontier models](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BwXremQ-i6I&t=36s) found they all got less reliable as the window grew — researchers call it [context rot](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BwXremQ-i6I&t=46s). The window may hold a million tokens, but [attention across them is finite](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BwXremQ-i6I&t=262s). Keen names four failure modes: [poisoning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BwXremQ-i6I&t=275s) (a wrong note becomes "truth"), [distraction](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BwXremQ-i6I&t=320s) (long history steers the next step), [confusion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BwXremQ-i6I&t=363s) (too many tools), and [clash](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BwXremQ-i6I&t=415s) (the window disagrees with itself). One benchmark detail sticks: a model [failed with 46 tools and passed the same task with 19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BwXremQ-i6I&t=397s). The fixes — validate, compact, curate, prune — all throw something away. Agents get better at long jobs [by holding less](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BwXremQ-i6I&t=594s).

Raid Owl got hands-on with Nvidia's [RTX Spark](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X7YAOBDHU_Q), the Windows-on-ARM cousin of the DGX Spark. The N1X chip shares Grace Blackwell specs with the GB10; the big differences are [Windows instead of Linux and no ConnectX-7](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X7YAOBDHU_Q&t=134s). Configs run from 24 GB up to [128 GB of unified memory](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X7YAOBDHU_Q&t=19s). On the review unit, Qwen 3.8 27B ran at about [40 tokens per second](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X7YAOBDHU_Q&t=170s), Llama 3.1 8B hit nearly [3,400 tokens per second of prefill](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X7YAOBDHU_Q&t=181s), and Flux 2 Klein 4B in NVFP4 produced about [36 images per minute](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X7YAOBDHU_Q&t=190s). Top configs land in the same [$6k to $7k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X7YAOBDHU_Q&t=482s) band as a loaded M5 Max MacBook — the buyer who needs CUDA and Windows on the go finally has a box.

Cloudflare and OpenAI published early results from a [research pilot on AI discovery](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-openai-discovery-agentic-web/). Using signals from about [1% of Cloudflare's network](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-openai-discovery-agentic-web/), OpenAI indexed [hundreds of millions of previously unknown pages](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-openai-discovery-agentic-web/) for ChatGPT Search. The point is not ranking tricks; it is helping agents find the obscure manual, menu, or passport page that never had many inbound links. As GPT-6 and Intelligent UI make ChatGPT more visual, that discovery loop is what feeds it fresh sources.

LangChain answered the questions teams keep asking about [agent evals](https://www.langchain.com/blog/common-questions-about-agent-evals). Start with one behavior, build a dataset of [10 to 20 real traces](https://www.langchain.com/blog/common-questions-about-agent-evals/), run a baseline experiment, and turn every new failure into another example. Use code evaluators where you can, LLM judges where you cannot, and human review to check both. For multi-step agents, score full threads and trajectories, not just the final answer. Harbor can run longer offline suites like Terminal-Bench and log them into LangSmith experiments.

Microsoft's Agent Framework for Python [ports Semantic Kernel's vector-store work forward](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/agent-framework/vector-stores-in-python-agent-framework-familiar-foundations-streamlined-for-agents) rather than starting over. You still define typed records with keys, data fields and embeddings, then upsert and search. The new piece is [context providers](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/agent-framework/vector-stores-in-python-agent-framework-familiar-foundations-streamlined-for-agents): expose a collection as an agent tool, or keep conversation history searchable in a store instead of stuffing it into the model window. The APIs are still experimental, but the pattern is clear — RAG as a first-class agent tool, not a bolted-on database SDK.

Canonical shipped [Ubuntu 26.10 "Stonking Stingray"](https://ubuntu.com/blog/canonical-releases-ubuntu-26-10-stonking-stingray). The headline AI feature is [Myna](https://ubuntu.com/blog/canonical-releases-ubuntu-26-10-stonking-stingray), an opt-in on-device speech-to-text agent built on local models, aimed at accessibility first and intended to land by default in 28.04 LTS. The release also finishes the [Rust uutils coreutils](https://ubuntu.com/blog/canonical-releases-ubuntu-26-10-stonking-stingray) rollout, adds post-quantum package signing, deepens Microsoft Entra ID MFA and passwordless sign-in, ships GNOME 51, and switches the desktop to dbus-broker. Interim Ubuntu releases are carrying real product again, not just polish.

Gary Marcus sat down at Bloomberg's New Economy Forum to argue that [current LLMs are not delivering industrial-revolution productivity](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jIZjuM4YYzU&t=35s). He jokes they may soon be called ["large liability modules"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jIZjuM4YYzU&t=81s) because they do not really follow instructions — more like an intern who breaks into the testing service to ace an exam. His older line still stands: [autocomplete on steroids](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jIZjuM4YYzU&t=172s), now with a separate verification trick for math and code. Useful as a tool, he says, but not AGI on its own, and not a five-year cure for cancer.

Pieter Levels says [data brokers are buying SaaS datasets for $100K to $2M](https://x.com/levelsio/status/2110671750727946638) as those companies look for cash while revenues slip, then reselling the data to AI labs for training. It is a secondary market for the customer records sitting inside struggling software businesses.

Aakash Gupta puts a price on the homework-help wipeout. [Chegg was still worth about $3 billion the month ChatGPT launched; today it is worth about $80 million](https://x.com/aakashgupta/status/2110667498354098357). Over the same stretch, he notes, four MIT friends built a coding app on top of the same models that ate Chegg's business.

Skywork open-sourced [Matrix-Game 4.0](https://x.com/Skywork_ai/status/2110615377088745817), pitched as a real-time playable world from a video model on a single consumer GPU: [480p at 60 FPS with about 112 ms end-to-end response](https://x.com/Skywork_ai/status/2110615377088745817). Instead of watching a generated clip, you steer inside it. Treat the "world's first" claim as marketing until independent benches land, but the open weights and consumer-GPU target are the real news.

Local inference keeps finding headroom. One report has [Qwen3.8-27B-Uncensored hitting 367.9 tokens per second of aggregate throughput on a local setup using about 20 GB of memory](https://x.com/Oluwaphilemon1/status/2110559462436073794) by stacking several inference optimizations, not by swapping the model alone.

Topview open-sourced its [3D Builder](https://x.com/TopviewAIhq/status/2110615678805774657): describe a scene in ChatGPT, Claude or another agent, or drop a reference image, and get an editable 3D set with locked composition before you roll the generative dice. It is a pre-production step between a prompt and a finished shot.

Dan Shipper is ready to call it: [the newsletter is dead](https://x.com/danshipper/status/2110707025826996280), even though Every still runs one with 120k+ subscribers. Persistent agents and always-on bots, he says, mean he barely opens the inbox. Nader Dabit sees the labor market flipping the other way — [FDE-adjacent jobs up about 1,000% over the past year versus roughly flat growth for general tech](https://x.com/dabit3/status/2110562060727681414). Agents are eating distribution channels and creating hands-on implementation roles at the same time.

That's the cycle. Go prune your agent's context, check your sandbox bill, and see you at noon.
