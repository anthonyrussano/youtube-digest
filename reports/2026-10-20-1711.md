The private keys of [eight eMudhra intermediate certificates were compromised](https://blog.cloudflare.com/emudhra-certificate-compromise/), so anyone holding them could forge a certificate for any domain. Meanwhile [two servers with identical B200 GPUs differ by about 50% in output](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3axY1wz2OvA&t=230s) purely because of who runs them.

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Cloudflare says it learned on October 19 at about 17:00 UTC that [private keys for eight eMudhra intermediate certificates were compromised](https://blog.cloudflare.com/emudhra-certificate-compromise/), certificates trusted by browsers, operating systems and Cloudflare itself. Browsers fixed it with a quick revocation switch, but everything else needs work: Cloudflare's advice is to "look beneath the SDK," because an API client may hand certificate checks to an HTTP library, the language runtime or the OS, and a passing browser test proves nothing about a background service. Ubuntu [removed the two cross-signing roots, emSign Root TLS CA G3 and Comodo AAA Certificate Services, from its ca-certificates package](https://ubuntu.com//blog/emudhra-ca-compromise-fixes-available), so `sudo apt install --only-upgrade ca-certificates` is the minimum, then rebuild images and restart services. And when certificate errors show up after patching, don't fix them by turning verification off.

Oliver Kennon of SemiAnalysis argued that [a GPU falls to the level of the operator](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3axY1wz2OvA&t=30s). In his example, [two stacks on B200s serving the same model at roughly 8,000 tokens in and 1,000 out](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3axY1wz2OvA&t=212s) deliver about 200 versus about 300 output tokens per second, [the same chip with about 50% more output](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3axY1wz2OvA&t=251s), all from software, settings and batching choices. He also says [their security testing found monitoring access that exposed another customer's logs and metrics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3axY1wz2OvA&t=315s) on a very fast cluster. The point for buyers: price per GPU-hour is an input, and the real number is cost per acceptable token.

According to [a WSJ report on Anthropic's draft prospectus](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2112641878147649911), the company plans to spend about three-quarters of a trillion dollars on compute over the next seven to ten years, including 111 billion dollars on Google Cloud contracts and 110 billion with Amazon Web Services. That is a frontier lab committing to compute at the scale of national budgets, before it has even listed.

Alexey Fateev argues that [DeepSeek's own DeepInfer docs show one cluster serving 155 trillion tokens a day](https://x.com/superalesha/status/2112653788574339075) at 38 dollars of revenue per GPU-hour at off-peak prices, against an API price of 30 cents per million input tokens. His framing is deliberately provocative, so treat the math as a claim, but published serving numbers like these are rare and give everyone a benchmark for what inference really costs.

Simon Willison built [Battle Drones, a Command & Conquer style RTS that runs in the browser](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/20/battledrones/), over 12 major versions and 321 commits by coding agents, mostly Claude Code, in TypeScript he barely reviewed. It only became fun after he told Opus 5.5 "This game isn't fun enough yet. Make it a LOT more fun," which kicked off many hours of subagent work overnight. It even has [two-device multiplayer over sound](https://x.com/simonw/status/2112630778068881543) and a mode where two LLMs play each other, which undercuts his own earlier belief that agents can make things that look like games but aren't fun.

i14 and AWS trained [Laplace 3B, a linear attention model whose memory per conversation stays at 29.5 MB no matter how long it gets](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/beyond-the-kv-cache-training-and-serving-i14s-laplace-3b-linear-attention-model-on-aws-trainium2-with-native-pytorch/), on Trainium2 with native PyTorch. One trn2.48xlarge trained it at about 120,000 tokens per second and served 11,544 output tokens per second across 1,280 concurrent sequences, and rewriting how the Muon optimizer communicates cut per-rank traffic 24 times. The catch AWS spells out: linear attention compresses history instead of keeping every token, so test retrieval quality on your own workload.

Oliver Gracer of Parcel Perform, whose platform handles [a few billion parcels and at times more than 1,000 updates a second](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=58ENTbfZD64&t=179s), told AWS's podcast how his team fine-tuned Amazon Nova. They brought [about 10,000 samples, and the loss flattened after roughly 20% of them](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=58ENTbfZD64&t=1953s). AWS's Karan Pandarkar put [the fine-tuning bill at tens to a few hundred dollars](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=58ENTbfZD64&t=919s), tiny next to the cost of a model that gets things wrong, and asked to pick, Gracer chose [a smaller model with fine-tuning over a bigger one with prompting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=58ENTbfZD64&t=3416s).

Amazon Web Services walked through [taming an AWS WAF bill](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BkzMdh_9EPY), where [request inspection is typically 60 to 70% of the cost](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BkzMdh_9EPY&t=51s). One customer [cut request volume 40% with a single rate-based rule](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BkzMdh_9EPY&t=229s), static assets and health checks often make up [20 to 30% of inspected traffic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BkzMdh_9EPY&t=240s), and [rules left in count mode still incur inspection charges](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BkzMdh_9EPY&t=249s). The setup can even [have Lambda add a throttling rule when requests pass three times the baseline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BkzMdh_9EPY&t=284s).

OpenAI showed [a shopping agent that restyles a room from one photo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8n-SXxRIrm8&t=13s) in a demo for a fictitious home goods store. It [swaps in a cheaper chair to keep the set under 250 dollars](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8n-SXxRIrm8&t=39s), regenerates the picture, and [fixes a pickup order to home delivery mid-checkout](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8n-SXxRIrm8&t=86s). It is a template for retailers who want the agent inside their own app rather than in ChatGPT.

Vercel made [WebSocket support in Vercel Functions generally available](https://vercel.com/changelog/websocket-support-is-now-generally-available). Since the beta began in late June its CDN has served 300 million WebSocket connections and now handles 15 million a day, with CPU billed only while messages are processed. Realtime AI chat no longer needs a separate always-on server.

Ollama now offers [Clef from Cloudflare, a 27B multimodal decision model you can run locally or in its cloud](https://x.com/ollama/status/2112635366159352101), compatible with the Jev and System One APIs. AI researcher Elvis argues [decision models like these make good LLM judges for evals](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2112632133357346889). Fast yes-or-no models are quietly becoming the grading layer for everything else.

Scenario's Emm and team shipped [an open-source Blender AI plugin with more than 500 models in one tab](https://x.com/emmanuel_2m/status/2112673359775310084) for images, video, 3D, audio, textures and whole worlds, usable by people or agents. Blender becomes a front end for generative pipelines instead of a place you import their output.

That's the cycle. Go update your ca-certificates, check who runs your GPUs, and see you in the morning.
