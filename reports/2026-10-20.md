Anthropic's new open-source scanner has already sent maintainers [77 critical and 803 high findings, rated by the model, including a curl fix](https://x.com/ClaudeDevs/status/2112299602510749992). And the mysterious seeds behind NIST's elliptic curves turned out to come from [an old, widely available random number generator seeded with a timestamp](https://anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins).

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Anthropic launched [OSS Scanner](https://x.com/ClaudeDevs/status/2112299602510749992), a program that gives critical open-source projects free, periodic vulnerability scans with proofs of concept and patches attached. The first scans have already reported 77 critical and 803 high findings, with the severity rated by the model itself, and one of them became a fix in curl. Maintainers of the code everyone runs now get an attacker-grade bug hunter for free, along with the job of checking what it finds.

An Anthropic employee asked Claude where the seeds for NIST's elliptic curves came from, and it [cracked the 25-year-old question in about 30 minutes](https://anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins). The answer was not a hidden hash or a backdoor: the NSA's curves appear to have been generated with an old, widely available random number generator seeded with a timestamp. It is not a cryptographic breakthrough, but it explains the simple process everyone overlooked because the curves came from the NSA.

Inception released [Mercury 3](https://x.com/StefanoErmon/status/2112222911218291051), which it calls the most capable diffusion LLM to date, and claims it beats GPT-6 Luna and Haiku 5.5 on quality while running at more than 1,000 tokens per second. It is [40 times larger than Mercury 1](https://x.com/volokuleshov/status/2112224841998991396) and is available [on Baseten with a 1M-token context](https://x.com/baseten/status/2112285669360046338). If those claims hold, models that write all their tokens in parallel are no longer just a speed demo.

DeepSeek open-sourced [DeepInfer](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer), the in-house inference framework that [serves DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at scale](https://x.com/deepseek_ai/status/2112476496283775135), under the Apache 2.0 license. It ships batch-invariant inference and deterministic sampling, so the same input gives the same output no matter which other requests share the batch, and it runs on NVIDIA SM100 GPUs and Huawei Ascend 950 NPUs. The catch is that the KV cache system, prefill/decode disaggregation and load balancing are not in this release, so it is a production engine rather than a full service, and DeepSeek says it was originally built mostly on vLLM.

Meta published a technical report on [Muse Glimmer, a 30B open-weight agentic model](https://x.com/AIatMeta/status/2112265822274302066) built to run at 4-bit on a single consumer GPU, while its bigger sibling Muse Wonder [debuted at number 6 in Code Arena WebDev with 1,726 points](https://x.com/arena/status/2112215825944117361). Meta also says it trained Muse on [Prometheus, a gigawatt-scale cluster with hundreds of thousands of GPUs](https://x.com/AIatMeta/status/2112245968750145561), hitting 85%+ pretraining goodput and about 47K GPU-hours between interruptions. At that scale, one slow host can stall the whole run, so reliability is the real benchmark.

Lincoln Murr, head of AI product at Coinbase, explained [why AI agents will need their own bank accounts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f_HwzoYrZ7s). Coinbase's x402 standard, built on the long-unused HTTP 402 "payment required" code, had a goal of 25,000 agent transactions by year end and [was seeing one every 17 seconds by then](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f_HwzoYrZ7s&t=202s), and it now handles [hundreds of millions of transactions every 30 days](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f_HwzoYrZ7s&t=211s). His bet is that publishers will [charge agents a ten-thousandth of a cent per read](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f_HwzoYrZ7s&t=324s), and he says [AWS and Cloudflare, with about 40% of internet traffic between them, already support x402 gating](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f_HwzoYrZ7s&t=350s). His most practical trick: instead of waiting on the brokerage team, Coinbase [writes a skill that drops paper-trading code onto the user's own agent machine](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f_HwzoYrZ7s&t=816s).

Al Chen of Cisco explained [how his team gets AI software into production at some of the largest banks in the world](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bz1HFxQA1o8&t=88s), cutting [the wait from a year to a couple of weeks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bz1HFxQA1o8&t=101s). Scanners only check that a package was bumped, so his team [has an agent write an exploit against the old version and test whether the new one still falls for it](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bz1HFxQA1o8&t=372s), running the trials in parallel sandboxes. His sharpest warning is that an LLM judge's reasoning [can unredact customer PII](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bz1HFxQA1o8&t=678s) even when the UI looks clean, so they persist only the score unless a check clears it, or [run a second judge on redacted input](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bz1HFxQA1o8&t=752s). Zero leakage, he says, [costs another LLM call on the same request](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bz1HFxQA1o8&t=770s).

An audit of Terminal Bench 4 found that [60 of 65 tasks had at least one issue, 44 could be passed by cheating and 41 graded on unstated requirements](https://x.com/chem_safety/status/2112212879734947937). On an older version of the benchmark, Tencent Hunyuan's Terminal-RSI method [reportedly lifted Qwen3.8-27B from 51.7% to 86.52% on Terminal-Bench 2.1](https://x.com/Oluwaphilemon1/status/2112383672611402111) by turning the model's own verified successes into training data. Put together, the lesson is to read terminal-agent scores with care before picking a model by them.

The [vLLM v0.32.0 release](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2111814711956168760) packs 814 commits, a new /v1/systemone structured-decision endpoint and low-precision KV cache across hardware, including 4-bit KV on ROCm. Multi-API workers deliver 43 to 85% more requests per second, and shipping [precompiled Python bytecode in images cuts startup by 14 to 18%](https://x.com/nilsmatteson/status/2111985646286045482). For anyone self-hosting models, that is free throughput from an upgrade.

Cloudflare and VoidZero, working with Max Schwenk of Owner.com, introduced [tsrs, a Rust port of the TypeScript compiler](https://blog.cloudflare.com/typescript-rust-port/) built in collaboration with the TypeScript team rather than as a fork. Early benchmarks claim it is up to 8 times faster and uses about 70% less memory than TypeScript 7.0 with the same number of type-check workers, and some of its optimizations were already upstreamed into the TypeScript 7.1 beta. Cloudflare picked Rust over the official Go port for WebAssembly, since there is no garbage collector to ship along, which points toward full type checking right in the browser.

SemiAnalysis's InferenceX benchmark has a Google TPUv7 submission [running Kimi K3 with a megakernel on the TorchTPU vLLM stack](https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2111938013852762351), and says NVIDIA's first GB300 FP4 counter-submission is still [1.64 times worse in performance per dollar across most of the latency curve](https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2112258243372146742). Vera Rubin NVL72 is claimed to be [up to 7.3 times better than GB300 on 1M-context agentic work](https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2112379877621919877). The inference price war is now being fought one kernel at a time.

Satya Nadella says Microsoft is [expanding Project Perception](https://x.com/satyanadella/status/2112168320946634932), which treats AI models as insider risks and puts the security controls outside the model, to continuously assess agent risk, adapt permissions and contain threats. It is the same rule good ops teams use for people: don't trust the actor, constrain the access.

The US Department of Homeland Security ordered federal civilian agencies to [stop buying and remove DeepSeek products, including third-party tools built on DeepSeek](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-21447.pdf). The order excludes national-security systems, allows exception requests within 60 days and requires interim monitoring and logging. Anyone selling AI tooling to government now has to know exactly which model sits under the hood.

A Google DeepMind Gemini agent [found and formally verified acceleration rules for 11 unresolved Turing machines in BB(6)](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2112226024419365210), the frontier of the Busy Beaver problem. Every rule had to pass a Lean proof, so the result does not rest on trusting the model. It picked its own problems, which is rare for AI math work.

Physicist E. O. Kahya used Claude Opus 5.5 to [audit his own 2007 quantum-gravity calculations](https://github.com/eokahya/claude-audits-quantum-gravity), orchestrating an 18-task, 9-stage pipeline of SymPy and mpmath checks at about 30 digits of precision. It found arithmetic slips, a sign-convention issue and 23 typos, while the main physical result survived. The expert supplied the structure; the model did a year of checking.

A new paper compresses whole document collections into [16 memory tokens for a frozen LLM](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2111879742454665582) and beats feeding the full context on FactKG, 84.33% against 80.43%, while using 108 times fewer source tokens. Memory that small could make long-running agents far cheaper to keep informed.

A solo developer released [PandaCode](https://x.com/aicodeking/status/2112371824696901818), an open-source agent desktop app of just 12 MB, written in Rust with no bundled Chromium, that idles at about 180 MB of memory with around 30 agents. It works with Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode and others in one window and can run Mac VMs and Debian desktops for agents. The author warns to expect bugs, but it shows how light an agent cockpit can be.

Cameron Wolfe highlighted a paper arguing that [data selection should depend on how much compute you have](https://x.com/cwolferesearch/status/2112373934419042804): with lots of compute, training on lower-quality fresh data can beat repeating the best data. Filtering a dataset once and forever, it turns out, leaves performance on the table.

Hugging Face's Thom Wolf estimates that labs have shared [close to a billion dollars of training compute on the Hub](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2111828314851598745), free to download, up from under 40 million dollars at the end of 2023. The open-model commons is now written in GPU-hours.

Anthropic created the [Anthropic Public Benefit Fund](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2111881963657412903), starting with 5% of its shares and adding up to 2% more for each 50% rise in company value, so that roughly 20% of its equity is committed to the public good. The extra shares are subject to the board's discretion, and [@willcb reads the whole thing as a creative capital structure](https://x.com/willcb/status/2112200491535089760). Even so, a fifth of a frontier lab's equity is now pointed at something other than its shareholders.

Aleksander Madry is [returning to OpenAI](https://x.com/aleks_madry/status/2112231448103239998) to work with Jakub Pachocki on the safety and pacing of AI progress, after a few months away. A safety researcher coming back says a lot about where the lab thinks the hard problems now are.

That's the cycle. Write down your threat model, upgrade your vLLM, and see you at lunch.
