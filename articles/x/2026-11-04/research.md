### Video: "Building the AI Agent Is Easy. Proving It's Safe Isn't" (Jonathan Osborne, NatWest; AI Engineer, YouTube 5UgnatzpZ9E, 18 min)
Transcript (local Whisper fallback) plus four slide frames checked by eye (t=300, 490, 600, 705, 900).
- NatWest: second-largest UK retail bank, about 19 million retail customers (t=49). Its assistant Cora serves about 100,000 conversations a day (t=61), mostly scripted until now; about a third of journeys move to AI-generated answers backed by RAG and agents that act (payments, address change) (t=84).
- "The scary part isn't the model. It's the blast radius." (t=110)
- Four failure modes a script doesn't have: confidently wrong, prompt injection, right but unhelpful, non-deterministic (t=226-258).
- Routing funnel (slide at t=300, "Deterministic rules bracket the model. Fewer than half of all turns ever reach it."): all turns 100%; contextual filter removes button presses (slide 80% remain; transcript "about 20%" removed, t=311); stage 1 pre-classification rules, no ML; stage 2 relevancy and rephrasing (LLM, gated); stage 3 ML classifier ("an MLP, not an LLM"; transcript t=338 "a neural network over embeddings"); stage 4 post-classification rules. Why: access control, auditability (every decision carries a code), resilience (rules survive an ML outage), governance (versioned config, no redeploy). Slide footer: "We did not point a language model at 19 million customers".
- Quality as a vector: safety, task, experience (t=414). "A router that's 94 percent accurate but 100 percent confident the 6 percent of the time it's wrong is way more dangerous than one that's 85 percent confident and knows it." (t=445)
- Human baseline slide (t=490): June baseline, 111 conversations, 37 journeys, four brands, three independent scorers. 97% happy path, 76% edge cases, 43% topic changes. "A single 83% pass rate hid a 54-point spread." Pairwise human agreement: bias and fairness 95%, overall verdict 78%, final response quality 66%, intent understanding 64%. "If your humans agree 64% of the time, 'match the human' is not a target." (The Whisper transcript says "80% pass rate" once and "83%" later; the slide and the video description both say 83%, used here.)
- Judge slide (t=600), complaints agent live since February, about 14,000 cases scored by both a human and an LLM judge (t=581): LLM judge mean ~0.97, spread ~0.04; human mean ~0.83, spread ~0.26; error vs human ~0.2 "looked close"; correlation 0.04 "was not close at all". Same outputs, same 0.89 pass mark: 0.08 headroom under one judge, 0.02 under another (only the judge model changed, t=640). "Your judge model is part of your threshold configuration. Choose a judge from a different model family." (t=654)
- Scoring machinery (slide t=705): live conversation -> sampled (5% plus all handoffs, fails, long, angry) -> scored (LLM-as-judge on each dimension) -> Comet Opik (traces, scores, experiments). No internet from the pods; PII never leaves. Live traces as it happens, offline scoring every 4 hours, regression gate every merge request. "Continuous evaluation is production infrastructure, not a notebook you run once."
- Going live (slide t=900): shadow (classify all real traffic, Watson answers) -> smallest brand -> larger (percentage ramp) -> largest. Gate to advance: error rate below 1%, p95 latency below 500 ms, zero circuit-breaker trips. Alerts: groundedness below 0.90, handoff rate above baseline, drift or confidence shift. Roll back three levels: global (all to Watson), per backend (RAG or agent), per brand.

### Video: "Can we trust AI agents in the enterprise? ft. Derek Carr" (Red Hat, Technically Speaking, YouTube v489tFAlrHY, 23 min)
- Carr (Red Hat senior distinguished engineer, Kubernetes background) worked with NVIDIA on the OpenShell agent sandbox (t=187).
- "Sometimes that code is written live on production systems and run without ever being reviewed" (t=151).
- Kubernetes network policy was never as deny-by-default as agents need, and it was fixed at deploy time (t=580); OpenShell describes the credentials an agent needs, the endpoints it may use them on, and lets policy evolve at runtime: deny something novel, or be notified and approve it (t=593-679).
- Split the agent: the Slack-facing gateway stays in a trusted compute zone; the harness hands the untrusted tool step to a heavily confined zone (t=716-746). Agents may "consume a sandbox on demand just when doing the untrusted action" (t=801).
- Shared multi-user agents "de-duplicate the token expense" of everyone prompting their own agent in isolation (t=863). OpenClaw Enterprise adds a control plane with multi-tenancy and audit (t=1030).
- Outside facts used only in the Article diagram/one clause, each from primary docs: NVIDIA OpenShell docs (deny-by-default egress per host, port and calling binary; policy advisor: agent proposes a narrow rule, human review by default, rule loads on a running sandbox) https://docs.nvidia.com/openshell/how-it-works/policies/advisor ; Red Hat blog: secrets never stored in the sandbox, proxy injects credentials at the network boundary https://www.redhat.com/en/blog/bringing-claude-self-hosted-sandboxes-to-openshell-on-red-hat-ai . Devin's docs list "Devin Outposts on NVIDIA OpenShell" among its platforms https://docs.devin.ai/cloud/outposts/overview .
