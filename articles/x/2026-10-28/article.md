Chainguard's Patrick Smyth put Mandiant's numbers on one slide: in 2018 and 2019, attackers needed [63 days after a patch to exploit a bug, and by 2025 the average exploit arrived 7 days before the patch existed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xZX3pmaB8Hk&t=171s). Meanwhile researchers who studied leaked code from a Chinese firewall vendor found its reset packets carry [the same seed key, 13, as two of the Great Firewall's own injectors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MaKRLgXzWp0&t=816s).

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Patrick Smyth of Chainguard told AI Engineer New York that the 30-day patch window [may as well be a million years](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xZX3pmaB8Hk&t=254s) now. His list of the year's supply chain attacks includes an Axios compromise that was [live for only two hours and still got 600,000 downloads](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xZX3pmaB8Hk&t=382s), and he says attackers now aim at the side doors: CI pipelines, developer laptops and the new agent layer. His to-do list starts with [cool-down periods for new packages, faster CVE fixes and signing every artifact](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xZX3pmaB8Hk&t=555s) with Sigstore and SLSA. Chainguard's own targets are [one day for known exploited bugs, seven for criticals and 14 for everything else](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xZX3pmaB8Hk&t=997s), and his scan of public agent skills found [about 5% actively malicious and another 30 to 40% with serious security problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xZX3pmaB8Hk&t=1188s). If your laptop installs packages that CI would hold back for a week, that is the gap he means.

Felix Rieseberg, a hiring manager at Anthropic, has [stopped giving coding interviews](https://felixrieseberg.com/hunger-beats-tenure-advice-for-junior-engineers/) this month. A small React challenge now tells him as little as an assembly quiz, he writes, because "Claude is now better at both," and newer engineers are flying past people who are probably better coders. His proof: two relatively new engineers prototyped the graphical version of Claude Code over one weekend, and it shipped about eight days later. His advice to engineers without status is to pick one radical idea tied to business goals and build it over a weekend. Nader Dabit's reaction was blunt: [the skill set he spent 13 years mastering is now obsolete](https://x.com/dabit3/status/2115264402547245398), and writing code was always a means to an end.

Ilya Razenshteyn, who until two months ago worked on Codex at OpenAI, interviewed about two dozen engineers and researchers for a [pragmatic guide to agentic coding](https://ilyaraz.substack.com/p/pragmatic-guide-to-agentic-coding). Almost all of them use stock Claude Code or Codex, nobody uses agent swarms or software factories, and the common loop is plan, review the plan, implement, review the PR, merge. Skipping code review only works for demos, strictly testable code such as GPU kernels, and research code that just has to be directionally correct. The bigger danger, he says, is the team: "slop infestation" of PRs and docs, and "overbuilding" nearly identical tools nobody uses. He recommends one team's rule that every LLM-written design doc gets a human-written one-pager.

Amazon rewrote its software engineer role guideline and [published the memo](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-software-engineer-role-guideline-ai). It names no tools and judges engineers on customer and business outcomes, admits that token counts and lines of code are deeply flawed metrics ("tokenmaxxing is at odds with frugality"), and tells managers to study the 10th and 90th percentiles of AI adoption. The line worth framing: "If your work does not benefit from current AI tooling, that is a finding, not a failing." Gergely Orosz [reads it more bluntly](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2115331737714622800): engineers who don't want to be close to customers will struggle to have a future there.

Aaron Levie agrees that enterprise automation is still in [the autocomplete era](https://x.com/levie/status/2115305369799110905). The vast majority of enterprise workflows haven't had agents applied in any meaningful way, he says, and agents probably cover less than 1% of the economy so far. He expects computer use, long-running agents and agent swarms to open up work in finance, life sciences and healthcare.

Artificial Analysis moved its Intelligence Index to closed benchmarks in version 5, and [open-weights models fell further behind](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2115265171233120627). The best open contender, Kimi K3.1 Pro (weights not yet released), scores 46, 12 points behind the leader, compared with a 3-point gap in v4.3. Among released weights, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM-5.3 top out at 32, and Claude Haiku 5.5 beats both. Ethan Mollick [suggests a grain of salt](https://x.com/emollick/status/2115277286123704654) but says it matches the general feeling.

A USENIX Security team from William & Mary and IBM Research let agents loose on a live 24-hour capture-the-flag event with 17 new challenges. Among the humans, [trust in AI dropped after the event](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xPxlzzMB9O8&t=283s), and less experienced players fell into ["answer shopping"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xPxlzzMB9O8&t=514s). Then they ran 12 agent teams, Claude Code, the NYU and Cybench agents and a proprietary one, each on Sonnet 4.5, Opus 4.1 and Haiku 3.5. The top human team still won with about 5,400 points, but a proprietary agent on Sonnet 4.5 took [second with 4,900 points in only 4 hours](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xPxlzzMB9O8&t=614s), for less than 100 dollars per team against prizes of 1,000 and 500. Haiku teams stalled between 300 and 1,100, and [an expert paired with an agent beat the best human team](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xPxlzzMB9O8&t=760s).

Anna Ablove of the University of Michigan dug through the September 2025 Geedge Networks leak: [more than 100,000 documents, about 572 GB and over 100 git repositories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MaKRLgXzWp0&t=66s), the first public look at the source of a commercial deep packet inspection system. Geedge's founder Fang Binxing is known as [the father of the Great Firewall](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MaKRLgXzWp0&t=129s). One log tracks 29 VPN tools, [21 of them at the top risk level](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MaKRLgXzWp0&t=452s), including ExpressVPN, NordVPN, Psiphon and Tor, and a Myanmar support ticket marks [Signal blocking as urgent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MaKRLgXzWp0&t=516s). The team built the gateway in Docker and matched its reset-packet fingerprint to live Great Firewall injectors, calling it ["source code actively running inside a national firewall"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MaKRLgXzWp0&t=862s).

Arjun Gupta's Revelio team checked VoIP blocking in 71 countries and found [19 that censor calls, across 5 apps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fo_nWRxkzT4&t=952s). STUN travels in plaintext, so blocking Messenger calls can be as crude as matching "turn.facebook," and [changing one letter to "turn.mazebook" got calls through](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fo_nWRxkzT4&t=524s). In Myanmar about 4,600 addresses answered a plain probe, but [only 336 answered once it carried a Messenger fingerprint](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fo_nWRxkzT4&t=653s), the UAE answered WhatsApp with [more than 1.5 million spoofed ICMP replies](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fo_nWRxkzT4&t=743s), and in China Viber, Signal and Telegram calls are [blocked more than 90% of the time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fo_nWRxkzT4&t=1053s).

Meta and Yandex Android apps were quietly [listening on localhost](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yYBdEWY1L0g&t=198s), so the Meta Pixel and Yandex Metrica scripts on ordinary websites could hand them your browsing IDs. Meta Pixel sits on [more than 5.8 million sites and Metrica on nearly 3 million](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yYBdEWY1L0g&t=317s), Yandex had been doing this [since February 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yYBdEWY1L0g&t=363s), and over 75% of cases fired on page load, before any consent banner. Both companies stopped [the day the disclosure went live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yYBdEWY1L0g&t=565s), and most browsers now ask before a page can reach your local network.

Tile's anti-theft mode [hides tags from its own anti-stalking scan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IpBw1G46mNc&t=144s), and the scan itself means [six manual scans while walking three city blocks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IpBw1G46mNc&t=655s). The researchers found location reports sent unencrypted, so [Tile can see millions of users' locations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IpBw1G46mNc&t=236s), plus IDs that repeat every 30 days next to a static MAC address. They recommend [dropping anti-theft mode altogether](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IpBw1G46mNc&t=825s).

Regex backreferences have their own denial of service. A Stony Brook and Purdue team built a new automaton model and a detector, RepGuard, that found [48 vulnerable regexes in the Snort 2.9 ruleset](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=70x8DFsmm20&t=1020s), 15 of which other ReDoS detectors miss. The nastiest of their four attacks trips PCRE's match limit, and then [Snort simply lets the packet through](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=70x8DFsmm20&t=1159s).

Purdue and Cisco found that membership inference on fine-tuned LLMs gets better when you stop listening to the loudest tokens. Across [more than 10 million token-loss differences from 20,000 documents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bc7UIBBETvI&t=190s), domain-specific tokens dominate but say little about membership, so their WBC attack slides windows of 2 to 40 tokens and lets them vote. It reaches [an AUC of 0.839 against 0.754 for the best baseline across 11 datasets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bc7UIBBETvI&t=1097s), and 2.8 times the true-positive rate at a 1% false-positive rate. Their closing line: ["the strongest signal is not always the most informative signal."](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bc7UIBBETvI&t=1188s)

Robert C. Martin, Uncle Bob, released [ub-mode](https://github.com/unclebob/ub-mode), a Clean Code skill for coding agents built on three stages: make it work with TDD, make it right, then practice with small ordered commits. It enforces the rules with his own tools: functions must stay under a CRAP score of 10, duplication under 0.8 similarity, and a mutation tester kills surviving mutants. It drops into Claude Code, Cursor or Grok skill folders and runs as /ub-mode.

[Coolify](https://github.com/coollabsio/coolify) is the open-source answer to Vercel, Heroku and Netlify on your own hardware. It deploys static sites, full apps and databases, offers 360-plus one-click services, needs only SSH access to a VPS, bare metal or a Raspberry Pi, and is Apache-2.0 with over 65,000 GitHub stars.

And for the Kubernetes crowd, [seven practical deep dives](https://x.com/techopsexamples/status/2115300949577384088) cover etcd, the RBAC decision flow, namespaces, air-gapped clusters, liveness probes, HPA versus Cluster Autoscaler and multi-cluster GitOps.
