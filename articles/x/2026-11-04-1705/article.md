Anthropic's biologists [pointed Claude at a mystery cell biologists have chased for decades](https://x.com/NewsfromScience/status/2118075833516503246) and came back with the first 3D structure of a vault from a virus that infects bacteria. Meanwhile researchers showed a speaker and a phone are enough to [slip an inaudible jailbreak into a voice assistant](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hULdofRBYn0&t=417s).

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Anthropic's in-house biology team used Claude to find [the first direct evidence that bacteria and viruses build vaults](https://x.com/NewsfromScience/status/2118075833516503246), the barrel-shaped particles found by the thousands in animal and plant cells whose job is still unknown. Anthropic's write-up says the phage vault is [built from 96 copies of one protein, against 78 in human vaults](https://www.anthropic.com/research/investigating-vault-particles-with-claude), and that Claude ran the cryo-EM processing software itself, with a first full model ready in 34 hours. Structure work like that often takes weeks or months by hand. The function is still a mystery, but the lab bench now has an agent at it.

A Claude Code pattern is spreading fast: [run Sonnet as the main session and keep Opus on call as an advisor](https://x.com/xsmryze/status/2118087892354449537), so the expensive model only weighs in before a plan locks, after the same error shows up twice and before a task is called done. The launch line is `claude --model sonnet --advisor opus`, and Anthropic's docs confirm [the flag exists even though `claude --help` doesn't list it](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/advisor). The viral version also pitches a routing product called JEV, which the docs don't mention. The advisor part is the free win: Opus judgment on the big calls without paying Opus rates on every turn.

A team from German universities tested passkey logins on real websites and found that [none of the 103 sites they could fully analyze followed the spec](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V4CDbcnOU8c&t=579s), with 18 critical and 53 high-severity flaws. The worst trick is simple: an attacker registers a key in their own account but [swaps in the victim's key ID, and a backend that overwrites the row hands over the account](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V4CDbcnOU8c&t=672s). Passkeys fix phishing and password reuse. They don't fix a server that skips the checks.

Microsoft shrank its MAI-Code-1.1-Flash coding model [from 276 GB at 16-bit precision to a 53 GB mixed-precision checkpoint](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry-on-windows/coding-ai-on-windows-pcs-quality-efficiency), and says it runs at a median 51 tokens per second with a 120,000-token input on an NVIDIA RTX Spark PC with 128 GB of memory. In its demo Copilot audits a whole codebase for dead code, unused dependencies and stale feature flags with the model running locally. Unmetered coding agents, with no per-token bill, are moving onto the desk.

The Sirens' Whisper attack hides a spoken jailbreak in a near-ultrasonic signal that people barely hear, and the [microphone's own quirks demodulate it back into speech](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hULdofRBYn0&t=431s) for the speech-driven model. At its best it got the targeted models to [stop refusing 94% of the time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hULdofRBYn0&t=785s), and it kept working with [background noise around 50 to 60 dB](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hULdofRBYn0&t=851s). Detecting the ultrasound in software is [highly device dependent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hULdofRBYn0&t=1029s), because some phones filter it out before the software ever sees it. The researchers' demo rig is [a speaker and an iPhone one meter apart](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hULdofRBYn0&t=944s). Your smart speaker's safety boundary now includes the air in the room.

Jon Saad-Falcon and colleagues built [Joules-to-GDP, a way to measure how much economic value AI produces per unit of energy](https://x.com/JonSaadFalcon/status/2118079242340814896). Their findings cut against instinct: Gemma 4 31B delivered 56% of GLM 5.2's value with 23% of the energy, and moving one model from 1 to 8 H100s used 3.2 times the energy to finish only 22% faster. The biggest model on the most chips is often the worst deal per watt.

Layer 3 Intel spotted [residential proxy traffic exiting through the White House network 236 times in 30 days](https://layer3intel.com/blog/residential-proxies-white-house-network), from 6 addresses and through 28 different proxy services. About half of it happened between 5 p.m. and midnight Eastern, which the researchers say points to a person's infected device rather than guest Wi-Fi. Anyone can rent that exit for a few dollars, which is exactly the problem.

A team working with Cisco showed that poisoning fine-tuning data can teach a model [a secret language for hidden instructions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=79ji7uky_wo&t=146s), so an innocent-looking paragraph about fungi carries a command. They mixed only [140 poison examples into 1,230 clean ones](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=79ji7uky_wo&t=875s), and the hidden channel [still worked over 70% of the time after the text was paraphrased](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=79ji7uky_wo&t=989s). That makes vetting third-party fine-tuning data a security job, not a data-cleaning one.

Microsoft's Intelligent Terminal 0.3 adds [a sidebar that shows live status for every agent session](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/commandline/intelligent-terminal-v0-3-a-new-way-to-see-and-manage-your-tabs-and-agents), with a dot on any tab waiting for you. It also detects installed agent CLIs and adds them to the new-tab menu, and a headless mode keeps chosen tabs running after the window closes. The terminal is turning into a control room for agents.

A jailbreak detector called MTK stops looking at one snapshot of a prompt and instead [tracks how it moves through every layer of the model](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0VQ11EtvpQE&t=331s), where wrapped attacks drift between harmless and harmful regions. It [scored an AUC of about 0.94 on Llama 2 and Llama 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0VQ11EtvpQE&t=563s) across 10 attacks, best in 31 of 40 model-and-attack pairs, and it [needs no jailbreak samples to train](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0VQ11EtvpQE&t=426s). Defenders can build it from examples they already have.

A University of Hong Kong team built XGuardian, which catches aimbots in shooters from replay files on the server, and on [about 3,000 real Counter-Strike 2 matches it hit 88% accuracy with a 5% false-positive rate](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_ChlZnCdDIo&t=808s). It still [caught over 81% of cheaters trying to mimic normal players, in under 10 seconds per match on a CPU](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_ChlZnCdDIo&t=1022s). It also points reviewers to the exact suspicious frames, so a ban comes with evidence.

Amazon ECS Managed Instances can now [write container logs as standard Docker json-file files that one managed daemon per instance reads](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/amazon-ecs-managed-instances-json-file-log-driver/), with no host bind mounts and no root. Each container gets its own log directory named after its ID, at no extra cost. Bring-your-own observability agents got a lot less fiddly on ECS.

Hong Kong Polytechnic University researchers showed federated learning clients don't all have to pay for privacy: when [only two of 10 clients defend together](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2Y49WeowGG0&t=513s), membership-inference attacks drop to 0.52 AUC, close to a coin flip, for about 1% utility loss. The defenders split classes between them and add noise that cancels out when updates are combined. Hospitals in one network could protect patients without crippling the shared model.

A founder who works with data vendors and post-training labs says synthetic users that behave like real people [stretched an expert dataset from 50 to 100 tasks to 500 or more](https://x.com/kale_panoho/status/2118080210994405651) for training environments. The scarce skill, in that telling, is designing evals that capture how experts actually judge subjective work like a doctor's call. The data moat is moving from raw records to the graders around them.

fal now serves [GPT Image 2.6 with markup editing](https://x.com/fal/status/2118127874926407952), so circles, arrows and notes drawn on an image steer the edit, plus animated GIF output and five reasoning levels from low to max. Image models are getting the red pen designers already use.
