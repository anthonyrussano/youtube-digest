When Cursor started pushing a new model in its interface, Jellyfish watched tens of thousands of engineers and found [merged pull requests per engineer didn't budge](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ve2bAarPA2I&t=240s). On the same day, Anthropic [gave Agent Skills, the folder format behind hundreds of millions of installs, to the Linux Foundation](https://www.anthropic.com/news/donating-agent-skills-to-the-agentic-ai-foundation).

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Nicholas Arcolano of Jellyfish studied about 50,000 engineers at 447 companies and found that which AI tool someone uses explains [under 1% of their pull request throughput](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ve2bAarPA2I&t=385s), while how much they use it explains 9% and the person themselves about three quarters. The lever hiding in plain sight is skills: as of September [almost half of engineer-weeks had essentially no skill use](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ve2bAarPA2I&t=542s), yet engineers running 30 or more skills a week merge about 30% more. And the top 10% of token burners get [roughly double the output for about ten times the tokens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ve2bAarPA2I&t=658s), so spending more is not the same as working better.

Those skills now have a neutral home. Anthropic donated the standard to the [Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/22/donating-agent-skills/), which already hosts MCP, and says more than 70 platforms including ChatGPT, Cursor and GitHub Copilot support it, with nearly 280 million skill installs. Simon Willison called this move last December, and found his old prediction by asking Claude through his own Datasette plugin. Since a skill is just a folder of instructions and scripts, that kind of reach is exactly why the roadmap promises security guidance for people who install them.

Steven Tey's Hallmark is a good example of how fast skills spread: the design skill passed 30,000 stars and 60,000 installs, and [version 2 just landed](https://x.com/nutlope/status/2113316017510191578) for Claude Code, Codex and Opencode with a single npx command. Read what a skill runs before you add it, because it gets the same access as your agent.

Google's Gemini team shipped a preview [command-line tool built for agents](https://x.com/LyalinDotCom/status/2113287431382601765) that can generate images, video, speech and music, analyze media and manage agents, with the pitch of no more guessing model strings. It turns a sprawling API into something a coding agent can call without reading docs first.

CrowdStrike's George Kurtz says attackers who land on a machine now [pivot to the next system in 29 minutes on average, and as fast as 27 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PST8yesYOSo&t=474s), because they iterate at the speed of inference. His summary is blunt: [it's not malware anymore, it's prompts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PST8yesYOSo&t=491s), each one leaving a unique signature, which is why he calls the agent the new apex predator for defenders to watch.

Google Cloud's AI harness crash course splits agent engineering into context, tools, runtime security and evaluation, and builds a workflow with [parallel checks and a retry limit that stops and asks a human](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2C_CefSQEAQ&t=295s). Its security lesson is one line worth taping to a monitor: [simply telling the model to be careful isn't enough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2C_CefSQEAQ&t=404s), so the sandbox has to do the work.

Vanguard's Gareth Yoder ran a cash ledger migration for a system taking [up to 30 million calls a day](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uA4ah-dVHvU&t=98s), and the turning point was exposing the legacy mainframe code to AI tools through an MCP server. Questions that took a mainframe engineer seven days to answer [now come back in 15 minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uA4ah-dVHvU&t=156s), and 90% of the team's engineers use the tools daily. His first lesson for leaders is simply to make the legacy code base queryable.

Google's Knowledge Catalog tackles the agent that [guesses what "premium customer" means](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qeWzgLGSkls&t=12s) by mining agent interaction traces into definitions that data stewards [review as a diff](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qeWzgLGSkls&t=196s). Google claims nearly four times the accuracy and up to 75% fewer tokens, which says a lot about how much of an agent's work is reconstructing context nobody wrote down.

In Google's Build It episode, a fitness app's AI planner went from 15 seconds to [about 2 by mixing a prebuilt exercise library with generation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1TdBFTUbUrU&t=272s). The design rule they kept coming back to is worth stealing for any agent's memory: [never store data you can figure out from what you already have](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1TdBFTUbUrU&t=428s).

Jerry Liu's LlamaIndex team introduced [OCRia-1, a vision model just for document parsing](https://x.com/jerryjliu0/status/2113291928292790725), scoring 87.4 on ParseBench and 92.9 on olmOCR-bench's tables. Tables are where most document pipelines quietly break, so a dedicated parser is a big deal for anyone feeding PDFs to agents.

V7 says the GPT-5.6 models cut [tool call errors in its context graph benchmark from 2.7% to 0.2%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=20IRGgQwPkc&t=31s) and hallucinations by 38%. Fewer broken tool calls matter more than benchmark points when a workflow chains hundreds of them.

OpenAI says Uber is rolling ChatGPT Work out to more than 30,000 employees after a 1,000-person pilot, and notes that [some of its heaviest token users aren't engineers at all](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l16Gw7oVLNQ&t=21s). That is a hint about where the next wave of agent demand comes from.

Elvis Saravia highlighted a study asking [whether agent memory frameworks still help as models get better](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2113272925558305191). If stronger models make memory layers less useful, a lot of agent plumbing could turn into overhead.

Ethan Mollick marked his book's launch by noting the length of tasks AI can handle went [from 4 minutes in 2024 to 17 hours to 17 weeks](https://x.com/emollick/status/2113323645284344215). That curve is the reason every item in this edition worries about long-running agents.

Teknium had Hermes Agent build [a page of lesser-known dithering and component libraries in 20 minutes](https://x.com/Teknium/status/2113265212484636929), and Nous opened a [Hermes Labs plugins repository](https://x.com/Teknium/status/2113278395945779386) for extending the agent. It's a small demo of agents doing design research, not just code.

Cua opened [early access to Cua Spaces on Hetzner and AWS](https://x.com/trycua/status/2113315281787617537), running the same code written for E2B and Daytona on your own cloud. By Cua's own math, 256 reinforcement learning sandboxes for eight hours a day for a month cost about 10,200 dollars on E2B versus 700 on its service.

Louis-François Bouchard spent [around 500 hours building a benchmark](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_dTF3NpPzGo&t=858s) for whether a model writes like you, and found a cheap GLM model [matched the top model at a fiftieth of the cost](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_dTF3NpPzGo&t=961s). His advice is to stop chasing the perfect one-shot post and pick the model that's least annoying to edit, then feed it a prompt as long as you can stand.

Sarah Chieng of Cerebras and Cova Lee of OpenAI explained the system behind 200 million views, and the surprising rule is to [spend half your time on the title and header](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CFbnVpSsDFQ&t=485s). They also hunt outliers with a tool that flags videos doing [up to 20 times a channel's normal views](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CFbnVpSsDFQ&t=2993s).

freeCodeCamp's hour-long course builds keyword and vector search inside Postgres, with a [BM25 index](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kT7nXO32L-Q&t=490s) and results combined through [reciprocal rank fusion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kT7nXO32L-Q&t=2624s), weighted 70% semantic and 30% keyword. One database doing both kinds of search means one less service to run.

AWS SageMaker added [training session APIs](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/training-session-apis-on-sagemaker-ai) where your code calls forward_backward, optim_step and sample against a live session and pays per token processed. It turns fine-tuning into a loop you script instead of a job you submit.

CloudFormation can now [force a stuck update rollback to finish in one operation](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/aws-cloudformation-cdk/) from the CLI, SDKs, CDK or an AI agent, in every region at no extra cost. Anyone who has babysat UPDATE_ROLLBACK_FAILED knows how big that is.

AWS's Graviton token race ran vLLM on three generations of its Arm chips and showed Graviton 5 [serving tokens for about 1.16 dollars per million](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kTzhZq7di4g&t=166s), all on CPUs. CPU inference won't beat a GPU on speed, but for small models it's a cheap option to price out.

A separate AWS walkthrough of cross-account S3 replication with Object Lock has a trap that bites everyone: the default [AWS-managed aws/s3 key can't be used across accounts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o0rASDZgwXk&t=382s), so you need a customer-managed key. And remember Object Lock [can't be turned off once it's on](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o0rASDZgwXk&t=124s).

That DevOps Guy breaks down Linux permissions from the [ten-character permission string](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uLXxxPYwLH0&t=160s) to why [chmod 755 means what it means](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uLXxxPYwLH0&t=1168s). Running apps and containers as unprivileged users starts with reading those ten characters.

ServeTheHome's Qotom firewall mini PC packs ten network ports, but each pair of 10-gigabit ports [tops out around 12 Gbps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=12FmVwZhEWY&t=750s), and it [ships a 19-volt adapter for a case labeled 12 volts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=12FmVwZhEWY&t=909s). He also warns that mixed performance and efficiency cores add jitter for networking.

Quad9, the nonprofit DNS resolver, [will leave France on November 24](https://quad9.net/news/blog/au-revoir-france/) after a court sided with beIN, even though the daily penalty was cut from 580,000 euros to 1,000. A sports piracy case just knocked a public resolver out of an entire country.

The White House science advisor Michael Kratios told Tyler Cowen the plan's centerpiece is the [Genesis Mission, applying AI to science](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rmzUxe1_CpM&t=306s), and that frontier labs share models with the government [for at least 30 days before release](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rmzUxe1_CpM&t=2214s). Hugging Face's Clem Delangue argued that labs' biology guardrails [block legitimate scientists while bad actors get around them](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2113264393756545449).

Kevin Weil announced [Warp Sciences, an AI and robotics company for medicines](https://x.com/kevinweil/status/2113294657673466060), with a 215 million dollar Series A led by Lightspeed. Money for AI drug discovery is now arriving at robotics-lab scale.

That's the cycle. Use more skills, not more models, read before you install, and see you next edition.
