---
title: "Agents ship 60% of Vercel deploys, DeepSeek Flash cuts tokens 44%, and 1,000 Claudes tackle an OS"
dek: "A Sunday afternoon packed with agent plumbing: verification, evals, retries and the token bill."
---

At the start of the year, 3% of deployments on Vercel came from coding agents. [Now it is 60%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WuorvLfAFmA&t=23s). And DeepSeek's newest Flash model [finished the same coding tasks with 44% fewer generated tokens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A1vscZzkUg4&t=303s).

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Guillermo Rauch [laid out what agents have done to Vercel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WuorvLfAFmA): it took about 10 years to reach a billion deployments, and then it [added another 1.5 billion in 10 months](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WuorvLfAFmA&t=32s). He says [80% of the tokens on Vercel's AI network are now open models](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WuorvLfAFmA&t=3302s), and that [swapping one model for another is one line of code](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WuorvLfAFmA&t=3470s). Vercel's AI Gateway now [shows every agent session in one view](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2111871697842495810), across personal and coding agents, apps and API keys, which Rauch pitches as the metadata you need to measure return on the spend. When robots ship most of the code, the deploy platform becomes the place where you can see what they cost.

DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash 1017 update is lean. Donato Capitella ran it on AMD Strix and Gorgon Halo machines, and on the first eight tasks of Terminal Bench Mini it [took 42% less time per task](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A1vscZzkUg4&t=328s). On one Gorgon Halo box he saw [over 400 tokens per second of prefill and 14 to 15 tokens per second of generation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A1vscZzkUg4&t=423s), with speculative decoding pushing that to 17 or 18. Parallel Web Systems calls it [the highest-performing open model it has measured](https://x.com/p0/status/2111871426856894878), and the first open model in the top 10 of its Search Capability Leaderboard. For a half-trillion-parameter model running on desktop hardware, fewer tokens means a faster model.

Anthropic's CJ Avilla and Harrison Stall demoed [Flows, a new kind of multi-agent orchestration in Claude Managed Agents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kiClTcJUnhc&t=426s), where Claude spins agents up and down depending on how hard the task is. As a stress test, they asked Claude to [build a Unix-compatible operating system with a Python interpreter, using up to 1,000 agents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kiClTcJUnhc&t=2831s), and gave it a GitHub repo. Last time they checked, it had about 1,000 commits and was still going. They also warn that agents reading the open web can pick up text like ["ignore previous instructions"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kiClTcJUnhc&t=4660s), and point to Claude Code's auto mode, which checks each tool call before it runs.

On Peter Diamandis's podcast, the hosts dug into [OpenAI's release of 722 math solutions in a single day on October 6](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5YVqEpbgaU), and claim it amounted to [81% of the most important math results of the past three years](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5YVqEpbgaU&t=6s), a big claim worth checking. Co-host Alex says the drop was [one agent per problem with at most three hours each](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5YVqEpbgaU&t=3244s), and that only about 15 to 20 percent of the posed problems were solved that way, with no swarm at all. The panel's bet is that the same bulk-solve playbook [moves next to virtual cells and then whole virtual organisms](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5YVqEpbgaU&t=3600s).

Vercel's playbook for internal agents comes with [a repo you can copy](https://x.com/undefinedKi/status/2111917044715204681): start with the work your team hates, then run one durable workflow (research, qualify, write, ask a human) that retries and resumes when a step fails. The research agent is capped at 20 tool calls per lead, every email draft lands in Slack with Approve and Reject buttons, and the agent spent six weeks in shadow mode while the team's best SDR checked every output. The writeup says one of these agents now does the work of a 10-person team.

WorkOS engineers Nick Nisi and Zack Proser built a whole workshop around one line from Lauren Tan: [if an agent can't verify its own work, nothing else matters](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q4WC4TowkBo&t=457s). Their answer is app-specific ["proof" commands that drive a real browser with Playwright](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q4WC4TowkBo&t=1803s) and check, for example, that a waitlist email was actually saved. The easiest way to roll it out to everyone, they say, is to [put an agents.md in the repo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q4WC4TowkBo&t=3306s) that tells every agent to run those checks on every change.

Laurie Voss of Arize told the story of Claude Opus 4.5 [scoring 42% on CoreBench](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SHMCGrNJJGc&t=5854s), a benchmark for reproducing scientific papers. A researcher read the transcripts and found Claude was often right in a different way, like giving four digits of precision when the test wanted six; once Anthropic fixed the tests, [the score went to 95%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SHMCGrNJJGc&t=5895s). In another case, [Claude "failed" a flight-change test by upgrading the passenger to first class](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SHMCGrNJJGc&t=770s), where seat changes are allowed. His rule of thumb: [to halve your error bars, quadruple the number of examples](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SHMCGrNJJGc&t=6165s).

Langfuse's Annabell Schäfer and Doneyli De Jesus opened their evals workshop with the failure every agent builder fears: a support agent that [told a customer it had issued a refund but never called the refund tool](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=obfPzbapMIM&t=161s). Latency was fine and nothing looked wrong, and the team would only find out weeks later when the customer came back. That is why they [rerun evals automatically on every prompt change](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=obfPzbapMIM&t=2100s).

Eugene Cheah of Featherless AI says his token-cost advice [won't fit you if you spend less than 10K a month](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ti-BmGU1_mQ&t=154s); below that, find a strong use case first. Above it, [measure with separate token keys](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ti-BmGU1_mQ&t=1061s) per team and workload, and remember his rule that [every 2x of speed costs about 3x, and it compounds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ti-BmGU1_mQ&t=1807s). Ethan Mollick adds the demand side: [developers will end up a small part of total token usage](https://x.com/emollick/status/2111884340955062274), as internal stats at OpenAI and Anthropic show, while harder-to-verify, long-running tasks benefit most from smarter models.

Anthropic interpretability researcher Emmanuel Ameisen described a model that kept [doing a math problem, getting 12, then insisting the answer was 36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JHo-aiePjoM&t=36s). Looking inside showed why: [it had been accidentally trained on similar questions marked 36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JHo-aiePjoM&t=2264s), so it knew the right answer and was also rewarded for the wrong one. Asked for the capital of the state that contains Dallas, [you can watch "Texas" light up inside the model](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JHo-aiePjoM&t=807s) before it says Austin. Models, as he puts it, are grown rather than built.

Airflow 3.3 can now [reconnect to a Spark job instead of resubmitting it](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M4kuOP57vTk&t=350s) after a worker dies, by storing the job's ID in a task state store. It also has an [LLM retry policy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M4kuOP57vTk&t=440s): handed a job with a broken Java class path, it [failed the task at once instead of burning three retries](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M4kuOP57vTk&t=673s), while a quota error got a wait-and-retry. Durable, smarter retries are exactly what long-running agents need too.

An Astronomer engineer and Airflow committer walked through Airflow incidents where the first guess was wrong. One "broken DAG" was really [a new default of Python 3.14 that dbt didn't support yet](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aH8atjxRGig&t=305s). A cluster [ran 16 pods while about 496 tasks sat in the queue](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aH8atjxRGig&t=913s), and a mystery out-of-memory error came down to [each extra task adding about 400 MB](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aH8atjxRGig&t=1065s) after dev concurrency was set to 15 against 14 in production.

Datadog now runs [60 teams on Airflow, about three times as many as last year](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mLzoJDMC03A&t=150s), each in its own Kubernetes namespace. To keep one team's deferred tasks from running under another team's cloud identity, it built and upstreamed [trigger queues](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mLzoJDMC03A&t=699s), which let each triggerer [subscribe to one or more task queues](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mLzoJDMC03A&t=737s) just like workers do.

Pendo cut the time to stand up an ML model [from about three months to about three weeks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DW_qqXGDhTI&t=205s), and built a triage agent that [calls Claude Sonnet through Vertex AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DW_qqXGDhTI&t=1040s) and matches failing models against a journal of past fixes. On Airflow 2 it took a hand-rolled tool loop; [on Airflow 3 the same thing is about six lines](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DW_qqXGDhTI&t=1202s).

Sri Vidya's RAG pipeline in Airflow [won't promote an index to production unless faithfulness clears 0.85](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=msgNRIEDBnU&t=310s), plus context precision and recall gates. When she slipped an AWS example key into a document, the run [hit a hard block for a security incident](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=msgNRIEDBnU&t=1021s) and pinged Slack; quality failures can be overridden by a human, security ones cannot.

An Astronomer architect built an AI endurance coach on Airflow, and his best lesson is a warning: when a job [hit the max output tokens, the LLM returned an empty result and the task still succeeded](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pkXN00_f0Ec&t=1392s), marking data as processed that never was. Check LLM outputs like any other untrusted input.

At Frame.io, Bao found that the cloud bill [can run four to five times higher than the storage actually alive](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bj2DbwHv9Hw&t=261s), because replication, backups and soft-deleted files all still cost money. Once the numbers were trusted, he says, [he could take an actual vacation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bj2DbwHv9Hw&t=554s).

S2 co-founder Shekhar pitches ["S3 for streams"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pc2Rw9bTYZ0) for agents: [trigger.dev runs agent sessions on it](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pc2Rw9bTYZ0&t=724s), one stream per direction, and S2 says it [already handles millions of durable streams a day](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pc2Rw9bTYZ0&t=794s), where Kafka-style systems top out around thousands.

Oracle's Nacho Martinez argues that agent memory belongs in a database, and that [the worktree workaround was solved by databases 30 years ago](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KtBivPzmOM8&t=910s). His anti-money-laundering demo [splits memory into types like procedural and semantic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KtBivPzmOM8&t=1050s) and loads only what the current step needs.

Wisedocs found a medical claim file that was [an eight-gigabyte PDF](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W4pnzOURwHQ&t=1222s), which no context window will swallow. On its claims benchmark, [the best accuracy went from 20% to about 75% in a few months](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W4pnzOURwHQ&t=1548s), though completeness is still unsolved.

At a YC paper club, Stanford researchers showed [Rust from the Dalek Lite cryptography library checked with the Verus verifier](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X7oI4Umt3DU&t=1366s), and explained how they brought the stress-testing methods from [the ACAS-X aircraft collision avoidance system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X7oI4Umt3DU&t=1828s) to language models.

IBM's explainer on the memory wall has one number to remember: [chip compute grew about 6,000 times in two decades](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4b6KpFN7Rio&t=0s), but memory bandwidth grew far slower, a gap of [roughly 7x](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4b6KpFN7Rio&t=155s). That's why LLM decode is memory-bound and batching helps.

On the local hardware front, two AMD Radeon AI Pro 9700 cards ran Qwen3.8 Flash Next at [about 107 tokens per second at 64K context with a custom vLLM build, against about 30 for llama.cpp](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIoTh48iRBU&t=124s), and Qwen3.8 27B hit [up to 196 tokens per second with speculative decoding](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIoTh48iRBU&t=796s). Nvidia's new RTX Spark N1X laptop chip in the ASUS ProArt P16 has [128 GB of unified memory with up to 96 GB for the GPU](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=riq34gfL9hg&t=231s), and ran a small model at [about 103.6 tokens per second with Windows ML](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=riq34gfL9hg&t=731s).

At Apollo's Redding Ridge, which manages about 50 billion dollars in structured credit, building a portfolio [used to take a PM two to three days in Excel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BOWYujhIQuo&t=280s); with Rowspace's agents [it takes minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BOWYujhIQuo&t=294s), and the PM refines the last 10% by hand.

Justin Poehnelt moved some GitHub Actions runners onto a Mac Studio and [shared what makes self-hosted runners fast](https://x.com/JPoehnelt/status/2111873389774475570): preinstall tools in a container image, keep pnpm's store on the same fast disk so installs hardlink, split light checks, heavy builds and Docker jobs into separate pools, and merge tiny jobs. His last tip is to give an agent a scheduled prompt to monitor and improve the runners.

For homelabbers, [Incus Commander](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IAnnHdmefo8) is an open-source, self-hosted control center for Incus containers and VMs, with SSO and backups, and a move between hosts [doesn't delete the original until the copy succeeds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IAnnHdmefo8&t=908s). And [PikaOS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=exMAn_7gSIU&t=74s), a rolling distro built on Debian Sid, installed the right proprietary Nvidia driver for an old GT 1030 on the first try.

Two small ones. Logitech's MX Keypad [shows GitHub Copilot sessions, reasoning level and AI credits right on the keys](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EQeJj0hoPS8&t=64s). And if Amazon Connect's agent panel drops calls, AWS says to [make sure outbound TCP 443 is open](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FZSuJnJPDYA&t=124s) for its signaling server.

swyx's ["Highest Loop"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fva8hFosqyQ) keynote frames it all as loops stacked on loops, and notes that the AI Engineer World's Fair [aimed for 6,000 attendees and passed it](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fva8hFosqyQ&t=321s).

That's the Sunday cycle. Go write a proof command for your agent, and see you this evening.
