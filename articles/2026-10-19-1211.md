Unsloth made a [1-bit DeepSeek V4.1 Flash that shrinks the model from 508 GB to 250 GB](https://x.com/UnslothAI/status/2112212605930778830) and says it keeps about 88.2% of its accuracy, enough to run on a machine with 128 GB of RAM. And Grafana says its AI agent now starts with [504 tokens of tool descriptions instead of about 54,500](https://grafana.com/blog/six-improvements-that-make-grafana-assistant-better-at-helping-your-workflows/).

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Grafana Labs published six months of lessons from building Grafana Assistant, and the [dashboard error rate fell from 2.47% to 0.65% in about 10 weeks](https://grafana.com/blog/six-improvements-that-make-grafana-assistant-better-at-helping-your-workflows/) while the slowest operations dropped from 63 to 28.8 seconds. The trick was mostly the plumbing: the agent loads detailed tool manuals only when it needs them, its gcx command line tool gives errors that explain how to recover, and graders check queries and tool calls instead of just the final answer. Grafana's test set grew from 78 scenarios to nearly 200, because every failure found in production becomes a new test.

Jediah Katz, the [longest-tenured engineer on Cursor's agent harness, now at SpaceXAI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Y4sbZgBdnc), asked whether the harness is dead. His answer is half yes: studies from UC Berkeley and Apple with EPFL found that on newer models [the choice of harness barely moved coding scores](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Y4sbZgBdnc&t=305s), because models have [absorbed skills like exploring a codebase and running tests](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Y4sbZgBdnc&t=353s). But those same studies found [big differences in cost per task](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Y4sbZgBdnc&t=804s), and his team now logs every guardrail "shim" so that [a bot opens a PR to delete any shim that stops firing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Y4sbZgBdnc&t=1002s). He predicts that within six months [90% of agent work will run off the laptop](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Y4sbZgBdnc&t=1035s).

Cua launched the [Spaces SDK](https://x.com/trycua/status/2112228154651881479), which puts Claude Code, Codex or any other agent on a computer you pick. Each bot gets its own desktop, on [your laptop, an office Mac mini, your own AWS, Google Cloud or Azure account, or sandboxes like E2B, Daytona and Modal](https://x.com/trycua/status/2112228156958822650), and [about ten lines of code start one](https://x.com/trycua/status/2112228158699434443). Changing one setting moves the job to another of your machines, so the always-on agent no longer has to live on someone else's cloud.

Cloudflare previewed [Clairic, the first of a new class of extraction models](https://blog.cloudflare.com/clairic-extraction-model/), on Workers AI, with open weights promised soon. It answers in a single forward pass, generates no output tokens, charges only for input and typically responds in under a second. Every number comes back with an 80% range and every field with a confidence score, so pulling an invoice total out of a PDF stops being a job for a chatty LLM.

AWS engineers added [bi-directional KV cache transfer to vLLM](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/improve-ttft-for-multi-turn-agentic-workloads-with-bi-directional-kv-transfer-in-vllm/) for multi-turn agent chats. Normally the prefill server has to recompute the whole growing conversation each turn; sending the cache back the other way cut time to first token by up to 1.6x for Qwen3-32B and up to 3.3x for GLM-5-FP8 in AWS's benchmark. Agents that loop through dozens of tool calls are exactly the workload that pays this tax.

Exa's Ryan Ahern showed [Exa Snapshot, a search engine you can run as of a past date](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L4dUFimVDKU&t=335s). Exa saves every version of the pages it crawls, an [index of around 100 billion documents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L4dUFimVDKU&t=384s), so a backtest can [stop just before an acquisition was announced](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L4dUFimVDKU&t=659s) instead of leaking the answer from today's news. In a [companion talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vS2IA9pq0Q4) he argued that agents [run far more searches per task than people do](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vS2IA9pq0Q4&t=130s), which is why search quality now shows up on your inference bill.

Microsoft's Pamela Fox ran a [model swap workshop with GPT-5.5, Kimi K2.6, DeepSeek V4 Flash and Mistral Large 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGjk3Uk0MxM&t=190s), arguing that [any team should be able to point its app at a different model](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGjk3Uk0MxM&t=169s). On a shopping word problem, GPT-5.5 [did the whole calculation in one tool call](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGjk3Uk0MxM&t=1342s) while DeepSeek [took four to reach the same answer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGjk3Uk0MxM&t=1375s), and Mistral [picked a 2.30-dollar plant instead of the cheapest one at 1.58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGjk3Uk0MxM&t=918s) in an image test. Her point: grade the path, not only the final answer.

Apify's Jan Curn, whose marketplace holds [about 80,000 tools and paid creators almost 2 million dollars last month](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-AOxpwVeIWw&t=36s), asked why agents still don't pay for things. Despite more than a dozen payment protocols, he says [x402 saw only about 1 million dollars of volume in 30 days](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-AOxpwVeIWw&t=437s), because [agent payments are really competing with plain API keys](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-AOxpwVeIWw&t=603s). He also claims a 100-dollar Claude plan can buy [about 14,000 dollars of tokens if maxed out](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-AOxpwVeIWw&t=710s), so agents have little reason to buy a service instead of building it.

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch said the company [open sourced a fast HTML artifact builder powered by libfx](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2112247046082363821), which makes the agent durable. He says building the plumbing that let v0 survive server crashes, flaky LLMs and function limits was a huge pain, and now it is free and open source. The demo is a [mini v0 that reaches 200+ models through Vercel's AI Gateway](https://x.com/vercel_dev/status/2112245199808434614) and outputs pure HTML. Durable agents that survive a crash are now something you can fork.

CERN and Mistral signed a statement of intent to build a [Large Physics Model trained on billions of simulated proton-proton collisions](https://home.cern/cern-and-mistral-join-forces-to-accelerate-ai-for-science/) at the Large Hadron Collider. CERN already stores about an exabyte of LHC data, and collisions will grow tenfold when the High-Luminosity LHC starts around 2030. One model could replace a whole chain of specialized reconstruction algorithms.

Navvye Anand of Bindwell announced [Atomwell, a foundation model for predicting experimental outcomes](https://x.com/navvye/status/2112219726474564019), trained from 800M to 9B parameters on about 80 million lab measurements. They report clean scaling laws in both model size and data, which would make molecular discovery another field where bigger simply works.

Alan Baade, with Fei-Fei Li and others, argued that [the tokenizer is a new scaling axis](https://x.com/BaadeAlan/status/2112242254169747577) for image and video diffusion. Their method keeps working where DINO and LeJEPA collapse and gives joint tokenizer-plus-diffusion scaling laws that they call compute-optimal. If it holds, how you chop up pixels becomes as tunable as model size.

The Information's Stephanie Palazzolo reported [a new Anthropic model, Google's Gemini 4 Carbon and a possible OpenAI model called Terra](https://x.com/steph_palazzolo/status/2112183963452059734) are on the way, then [corrected the timing to the next few weeks rather than October](https://x.com/steph_palazzolo/status/2112205820440326307). Treat it as a scoop, not a launch date.

fal Research and Black Forest Labs released [FLUX 3 Max, a video model that makes a 10-second 720p clip in about 12 seconds](https://x.com/fal/status/2112251022047412492). fal says it is tied for first in image-to-video quality on third-party benchmarks and is 75% off for three weeks, a vendor claim worth testing yourself.

Sergii Makarevych published a guide to [writing agent skills and testing them with evals](https://x.com/sermakarevich/status/2112198653469241856), pointing out that millions of skills exist, hardly any were ever tested and most were "vibe-written." A skill without an eval is just a prompt you hope works.

Long Lake's Prathik Naidu explained why his firm [buys service companies instead of selling them AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J7n3WJnFTto). In a typical service business, [out of every 100 dollars of work you keep about 20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J7n3WJnFTto&t=675s), and AI can bend that labor curve. Long Lake has [raised 7.5 billion dollars, works with 40 companies, and announced a 6.3 billion dollar deal with Amex GBT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J7n3WJnFTto&t=736s).

GitHub showed [Copilot in IntelliJ taking an accessibility issue to a working fix](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=knXsDMuKKyw), with a choice of [three harnesses: the classic local agent, the SDK agent that shares Copilot CLI's engine, or GitHub's cloud agent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=knXsDMuKKyw&t=165s). A separate guide shows how to [open a Copilot app session in VS Code with its plan, conversation and worktree intact](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/how-to-continue-a-github-copilot-app-session-in-vs-code/), so handing work from the agent to yourself no longer means re-explaining the task.

A side-by-side of [Cursor, Claude Code, Codex CLI and Google's Antigravity CLI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xSjF0ownP70) says the first question is [where you want the AI to live: terminal, editor or a multi-agent platform](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xSjF0ownP70&t=105s). The presenter says that in his use [Claude Code runs out of tokens fast on big projects](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xSjF0ownP70&t=417s), while Codex rides a ChatGPT plan further.

Amazon GuardDuty added [more than 50 optional Runtime Monitoring rules](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/guardduty-runtime-optional-detection-rules/) for EC2, EKS and ECS, catching things like edits to sudoers files or disabled audit logging. They span 13 finding types mapped to MITRE ATT&CK, and a dry-run mode lets you see the noise before alerts go live.

OpenAI's new [ChatGPT Voice, powered by GPT Live, listens and talks at the same time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mEulEkuxCnM&t=22s), so you can interrupt it mid-sentence. The UK demo shows it [translating a guitar sale into Spanish in real time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mEulEkuxCnM&t=104s) and emailing a list of vegan restaurants during the chat.

Thomson Reuters says lawyers juggle too many systems, so it wants them [working in one place, ChatGPT Enterprise](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ExVDpMeaWp0&t=28s), backed by the trust it built with CoCounsel Legal, with results that are [verifiable and cited](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ExVDpMeaWp0&t=40s). In law, an answer without a source is a liability.

Alba spent over 10 years as a radio frequency engineer on 4G and 5G networks, then [became a DevOps cloud engineer for mobile operators](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vnxkaP8clJE&t=40s). After two AWS certifications she read real job ads, saw [CI/CD, Kubernetes, Docker, Terraform and Ansible, and realized certificates weren't enough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vnxkaP8clJE&t=96s). She studied at 5 AM before work and [asked her manager for a role that didn't exist yet](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vnxkaP8clJE&t=131s).

That's the cycle. Trim your tool list, version your search, and see you tonight.
