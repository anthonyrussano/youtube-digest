Nvidia just reversed its CEO exit in eleven days. Meanwhile, Anthropic went public near two trillion dollars, and an open-weights model in 20 GB of laptop memory beat models four times its size.

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Nvidia's board [reinstated Jensen Huang](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/jensen-huang-reinstated-as-nvidia-ceo-board-chair/) as CEO and chairman today. He returned eleven days after resigning on November 12 amid an affair investigation and export-control scrutiny. The board said senior U.S. officials confirmed his return poses no national security risk. Nvidia kept an independent review committee, formed a National Security and Export Control Committee, and opened a search for a chief compliance officer. Shares jumped roughly 30 percent in early trading after falling about 25 percent during his absence. Nvidia is also in advanced talks to [acquire diffusion-model lab Inception](https://x.com/steph_palazzolo/status/2123549936880930945) for more than $500 million.

Anthropic [closed its Nasdaq IPO](https://www.anthropic.com/news/ipo-close) under the ticker ANT at $83 per share. Public buyers took $93.5 billion including the underwriters' option, and seven partners bought $26.0 billion in a concurrent private placement. AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, and TSMC took Class N shares. Total gross proceeds hit $119.5 billion, and day-one trading closed at $97.17 for a diluted valuation near $1.8 trillion. Having seven silicon and cloud partners hold equity ties frontier lab capital straight to chip supply.

Independent mathematicians and the Lean community [formally verified Levent Alpöge's proof](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/) of the Riemann Hypothesis. Released November 20, the proof arrived as a 17-million-line Lean library built with Claude. Dozens of machines compiled all 175,187 modules against Mathlib, Lean's standard math library. Independent checkers including `con-leche` and `con-ron` verified the raw proof export alongside Lean's official kernel. Skeptics of short-term agent reliability now have a live benchmark for machine-checked math on three standard axioms.

Thinking Machines Lab open-weighted [Inkling 2 Mini](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/), a Mixture-of-Experts model with 26.4 billion total parameters and 3.4 billion active per token. It runs its middle layers three times in a loop and trains natively in NVFP4 four-bit floating point. The model fits in roughly 20 GB of memory, scoring 48.2 percent on DeepSWE v1.1 and 46.5 percent on Vals Index v2.2. That places it first among open-weights models under 100 billion parameters. [Testing by Parallel](https://x.com/p0/status/2124249202389843982) also ranked it first in efficiency at $25.80 per correct answer. The lab released a variable-loop variant too so researchers can study unverbalized reasoning.

OpenAI shipped [GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/17/gpt62-astra-and-sol/). Astra costs $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, 20 percent below GPT-6 Astra, with cached input cut to $0.40. Sol stays at $2 and $10 per million tokens. Early reactions were split. Simon Willison found neither beat Claude Fable 5.2 or Gemini 4 Carbon on his Three.js benchmark. Theo Browne shared a pre-release run where Astra stalled two hours in to ask an obscure internal question. OpenAI also rated Astra High for AI self-improvement risk.

Google DeepMind answered a day later with [Gemini 4 Neon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-4-neon/). It costs $3 per million input tokens ($0.15 cached) and $15 per million output tokens with a 1-million-token context window. Neon scored 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at `xhigh` thinking, second on Vals Index at 70.9 percent, and 1,336 on Agent Arena. [Parallel's web research leaderboard](https://x.com/p0/status/2123497882322141453) put Neon first overall at up to 10 times lower cost per task than the runner-up. Developers get near-frontier judgment at less than half the token price of Astra or Fable 5.2.

METR published its [Q4 2026 Time Horizons 2.0 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/). Across its combined task suites, 50 percent task-completion horizons double roughly every 3.6 months. GPT-6 Astra hit a 40.3-hour horizon and Claude Opus 5.5 hit 39.0 hours. However, 95 percent confidence intervals stretch from 11.9 hours to 110.2 days. The researchers warn that models still lag human experts on judgment calls without fast tests, and model judges overrate strong peers.

GitHub updated [Copilot Fusion on Terminal-Bench 4.1](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-11-17-github-copilot-fusion-updates-more-availability-benchmarks-and-models/). Copilot Fusion Max hit a 71.2 percent resolution rate, beating Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2 by 5.4 points. It also cost 88 percent less than Fable 5.2. GitHub also added Claude Sonnet 5.5 to Fusion Max, GPT-6.1 Terra to Fusion High, and Microsoft's MAI-Code-2 to Fusion Standard. Multi-model routing inside one harness is fast becoming the default way to beat single-model costs.

Independent researcher Will DePue [reproduced a 13-row weight fingerprint](https://unemploymentlabs.com/checkpoint-forensics) in Moonshot's Kimi K3. Using seed 20256586 on a rented 152-SM Nvidia GB200 cluster, he matched the published checkpoint at 0.999995 correlation while adjacent seeds matched zero rows. DePue cautioned that the signature proves which GPU layout initialized the weights, not where full pretraining ran. Export-control researchers already maintain public repos to verify similar weight signatures.

Cursor told the U.S. Department of Homeland Security that it may [phase out China-developed open-weight models](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2123841504242446572) over geopolitical risk ahead of a House report, according to Andrew Curran. Days earlier, Vercel added Alibaba's [Qwen4-Max on AI Gateway](https://vercel.com/changelog/qwen4-max-now-available-on-ai-gateway) with a 1M context window and 35 percent fewer tokens on Work360. [Sebastian Raschka argued](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2124142247285235990) that open U.S. and Chinese competition is what drove proprietary prices down this year. An ideological split in IDE model catalogs will directly hit developer token budgets.

Even as coding prices drop, [François Chollet warned](https://x.com/fchollet/status/2124246742644461569) that GPU spot prices keep climbing. HBM and DRAM memory production is effectively sold out through 2029, while frontier training compute doubles roughly every five months. On the serving side, [Gergely Orosz noted](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2123795984002232634) why coding inference stays profitable. Coding prompts repeatedly hit cheap KV cache (saved prompt attention states) while speculative decoding speeds up generation. Memory supply and cache hit rates now govern unit economics.

Go check your cached-input hit rates before your next model switch. Even with a 95 percent cache discount on Neon or Astra, an uncached loop still burns full freight.
