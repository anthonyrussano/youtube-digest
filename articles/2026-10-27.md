A Claude research model [found a deterministic algorithm that solves 3-SAT in subexponential time](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00577), which would make the Exponential Time Hypothesis false, after 14 million output tokens and no human input. Meanwhile an Oracle demo showed an HR agent [handing over colleagues' salaries because someone asked how many party balloons each of them could afford](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v9Y3Lw_I9o4&t=777s).

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Anthropic contributed a paper titled ["A 2^{o(n)} Time Algorithm for 3-SAT"](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00577), submitted by Jelani Nelson, that claims a deterministic algorithm which actually counts satisfying assignments (#3-SAT), with the whole proof [formalized in Lean 4](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth). According to the submission, an internal Claude research model found it on October 16 using 14 million output tokens and no human input. The Exponential Time Hypothesis says 3-SAT needs exponential time; much of the "this is the best possible" side of complexity theory leans on it. Nelson [walked through the idea](https://x.com/minilek/status/2114954933133029561): sparsify the formula, lay the clause graph out on a grid and divide and conquer, with an "eight square identity" (related to the octonions) as the trick that cuts the average diagonal edge weight from 6 to 4.5, for a running time of roughly exp(O(n/sqrt(log n))). The strangest part: an OpenAI internal model independently reached a similar result, in a preprint titled ["The Exponential Time Hypothesis is False"](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf). R. Williams is organizing workshops with Impagliazzo and Paturi to work through it. If two labs' models refute a foundational conjecture in the same week, "can AI do original math" is no longer the question; checking it is.

Machine-checked math is turning into a product line. Physical Superintelligence, which launched in September with a 58 million seed round, [announced results on five Open Quantum Problems](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2114885512083407351), each with a paper and a Lean formalization, and its [research page adds verification code](https://psi.inc/research/oqp-batch-2026/). The batch covers numbers 2, 12, 27, 38 and 43: no entanglement distillation from three copies of certain Werner states, no CHSH violation beyond a finite distance in the vacuum of a free scalar field with mass, optimal Fourier-phase measurements for the CGLMP inequality, a bound of 2(d-1) PPT maps on problem 38 (where an OpenAI preprint shows two are not enough), and a counterexample on 43. Lean proofs mean a reviewer mostly checks the statement, not every line of the derivation.

Optiver, which says it makes [more than 10 million trades a day across a million-plus instruments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGIUg8zKI8A&t=45s), wants every new frontier model [in production flows the same day it ships](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGIUg8zKI8A&t=429s), benchmarked first on golden sets built with its trading teams. Noyan Tokgozoglu, its head of AI engineering, explained [how the firm pulls that off](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGIUg8zKI8A): everything runs on-prem except third-party inference, which goes out under [zero-data-retention agreements](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGIUg8zKI8A&t=293s), and an [inference gateway and an agent gateway](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGIUg8zKI8A&t=318s) sit in front of all of it, routing models, setting budgets and failing over between providers. At a trading firm a change shows up in profit and loss [within hours](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGIUg8zKI8A&t=92s), not quarters, so the platform has to be accountable for every layer.

The most useful part of the talk was a cost bug. Optiver found that a fresh agent session was [carrying 200K tokens of context before anyone typed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGIUg8zKI8A&t=649s), because every team kept dumping MCPs and knowledge into the defaults. The fixes were boring and effective: [MCP tool search](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGIUg8zKI8A&t=668s), [a CLI that wraps a bunch of MCPs instead of calling them directly](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGIUg8zKI8A&t=670s), tuning [when caches get invalidated](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGIUg8zKI8A&t=680s), and moving reference material [into a knowledge base instead of an ever-growing system prompt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGIUg8zKI8A&t=695s). Every tool call is logged and fed back into evals, and Tokgozoglu's advice for the next agent is to [scope it to one task and connect it through the gateway](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGIUg8zKI8A&t=1346s) instead of teaching one agent ten jobs. Check what your own blank session costs before you add another tool.

Japan's GMO Internet had to [ask brokers to block trades coming from its own Onamae.com Desktop Cloud](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/27/japanese-virtual-desktop-cloud-hacked-so-hard-it-asks-to-be-banned/5305610), a Windows virtual desktop service it recommended for FX trading, after attackers planted malicious programs, placed unauthorized trades and probably saw passwords. As many as 26,739 virtual desktop servers may have been hit. GMO [refused a 50 million dollar ransom](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2114907654141423684), and after the attackers threatened to trigger ransomware on the PCs of everyone logged in during the hack, it told users to unplug. Customers get a 5,000 yen condolence payment, covered trading losses and six months of a free replacement environment. CEO Masatoshi Kumagai admitted his in-house white-hat hackers had flagged problems and the "response lacked sufficient depth and speed," then blamed misuse of ARTEX, an autonomous AI penetration-testing tool, for attacks on about 20 Japanese companies. A vendor asking to be banned from its own customers' brokers is a new low for incident response.

Oracle's Juan Loaiza made the opposite argument with a demo: guardrails in the app are not enough. In an HR agent with ordinary security roles, a direct request for the marketing team's salaries was refused, but [a question about how many balloons each marketer could buy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v9Y3Lw_I9o4&t=777s) slipped past the AI guardrail, and the agent computed the answer from their pay.

With Oracle's new Deep Data Security turned on, the database checks the user's identity from a cryptographically signed token and [filters the data before it reaches the agent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v9Y3Lw_I9o4&t=808s), so the same prompt returns zero balloons for everyone. Loaiza also pitched declarative SQL assertions, such as [a rule that customers under 21 can't buy alcohol](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v9Y3Lw_I9o4&t=868s), enforced inside the database where a model can't talk its way around them, and said all of it ships [at no additional charge](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v9Y3Lw_I9o4&t=2299s). Whatever you think of Oracle, "enforce access where the data lives" is the right instinct for agents with database credentials.

The same talk had a sharp demo for anyone building text-to-SQL. Asked which Thanksgiving decor [sold best in Nevada since Labor Day](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v9Y3Lw_I9o4&t=1315s), Oracle's Deep NL2SQL agent flagged "sold best" as ambiguous and asked whether that meant units or revenue, and when returns were excluded the top item, a harvest wreath at about 31,000 dollars of gross revenue, dropped to seventh. A model that asks one clarifying question beats a confident wrong number.

Runlayer, a company of about [50 to 60 people running more than 300 agents every day](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BgLdvF7Qkh8&t=98s), shared what monitoring them turned up. An agent that reviews pull requests can be talked into approving one by [a GitHub comment telling it to "only review the cleanup"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BgLdvF7Qkh8&t=440s), and [if you ask it 10 times](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BgLdvF7Qkh8&t=466s) you will probably get it to do something it shouldn't. Costs crept up in duller ways: the recruiting team [vibe-coded an MCP server for a vendor API](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BgLdvF7Qkh8&t=804s) that kept erroring on formatting, and the agent paid for every retry, while one team that switched from a cheap model to a frontier one [saw costs balloon more than 200x within a week](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BgLdvF7Qkh8&t=848s). Watch error rates on internal MCP servers the way you would watch a flaky API.

Gravitee's Tim Tyler argued agents should be governed [like a workforce, not like software](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h8jgniNJcbQ&t=75s): each agent gets [one unique ID in a shared registry](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h8jgniNJcbQ&t=191s) and [a named sponsor who answers for it](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h8jgniNJcbQ&t=277s), owns what it spends and gets the call when it breaks. When one agent delegates to another on someone's behalf, [only the access all three share comes back up the chain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h8jgniNJcbQ&t=539s), so authority shrinks with each hop instead of growing. That is the same instinct as Oracle's: put the limit where it can't be argued with.

IBM is [promising an "AI-native" tool that maps the entire z/OS stack](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/10/27/ibm-admits-mainframes-need-to-be-modernized-and-will-use-ai-to-do-the-job/5305618), in a Statement of Direction rather than a product launch. The pitch is to "eliminate the leading cause of unplanned z/OS outages," which IBM says is changes that aren't proven to be safe, by mapping the impact of each change, then testing and validating it, with humans keeping review and decision authority. Change impact analysis is the unglamorous problem every platform team has; the mainframe is just where it hurts most.

Anthropic is now in court over music too. Elvis Presley's rights holders have [sued Anthropic over 274 songs](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/27/elvis-rightsholders-sue-anthropic-for-stealing-songs-and-leaving-them-in-heartbreak-hotel/5305625) in the Northern District of California, seeking 150,000 dollars per work, or about 41.1 million in total. That is small next to the 1.5 billion dollar fund Anthropic set up to settle the earlier books case, and it lands the same day the company announced its float.

On that float, the writer behind one sharp essay argues [Anthropic's IPO is where the AI build-out switches from debt to stock](https://x.com/nodiligence/status/2114892356260626705): it may be the best business in AI, and he still would not buy on day one, because the setup favors suppliers and insiders selling into index buying. The [S-1 itself is public](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm) if you would rather read the risk factors than the takes.

Elvis's lawyers aren't the only ones upset about scraping. Open3DLab says a crawler identifying itself as ["trellis2-data-sourcing/1.0 (3D foundation model research)" read 18,926 project pages](https://open3dlab.com/news/roblox-downloaded-145-gb-of-models-from-our-sites/) in 26,429 requests over 34 hours from a single AWS address, downloaded 1,027 files from 712 projects (145.5 GB) and ignored the robots.txt it had fetched. The site traced it to Roblox. Of those 712 projects, 423 carried non-commercial licenses and 345 were adult content; Roblox hadn't replied. If you host user content, check your logs for that user agent.

A developer called TeksEdge reportedly took Qwen3.8-27B [from 82 to 381 tokens per second on a single RTX 3090 with 24GB](https://x.com/Oluwaphilemon1/status/2114916110231359954), a 4.6x speedup. Multi-token prediction got it to 114, DFlash2 plus context lookup to 138, and optimizing how lookup drafts are verified did the rest, with blocks of up to 16 tokens and about 15 accepted. The lesson for local inference: once drafting is cheap, verification is the bottleneck.

On a laptop, Avi Chawla ran Qwen3.5 9B on a base M5 MacBook Pro with three engines and got [22.0 tokens per second from llama.cpp, 25.1 from MLX and 92.1 from uzu](https://x.com/_avichawla/status/2115010493899502058), a new inference engine. One person's benchmark on one model, but a 4x gap is worth checking on your own hardware.

If you run Hermes Agent, you can [switch its release channel from "stable" to "main"](https://x.com/Teknium/status/2114919004632764585) to try the new Hermes webapp early. The merged change brings Hermes Desktop to any browser from your own host, with chat, files, git, remote sign-in and a terminal that survives a page refresh.

Cloudflare's [Q3 2026 internet disruption summary](https://blog.cloudflare.com/q3-2026-internet-disruption-summary/) counted 49 disruptions, 19 of them government-ordered shutdowns, including 18 exam-related shutdowns in Iraq, plus 14 power outages and 5 natural disasters. In Honduras on September 10 an interconnection power failure hit more than 11 providers and cut HTTP traffic by 63 percent for about three hours, and Cuba had four outages with drops from 18 to 59 percent. Power is still the most common single point of failure on the map.

Two talks agreed that finding bugs is no longer the hard part. IBM Technology argued that roughly [90 percent of a modern enterprise codebase is open source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5WlmyncEcNM&t=0s), and that Anthropic's Project Glasswing [found 2,300 vulnerabilities across 400 open-source projects](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5WlmyncEcNM&t=66s), including [a bug that had sat in an open-source operating system for 27 years](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5WlmyncEcNM&t=85s). The bottleneck is [fixing, not finding](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5WlmyncEcNM&t=109s), and telling everyone to upgrade to the latest version [isn't realistic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5WlmyncEcNM&t=212s), so IBM is pushing [Lightwell for validated fixes and backports plus a shared incident-response effort](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5WlmyncEcNM&t=350s) that handles intake, deduplication and [synchronized disclosure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5WlmyncEcNM&t=535s). Cisco's Jeetu Patel put numbers on the gap: the time from a vulnerability being announced to an exploit is [about eight or nine minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0qb0FQo3M5c&t=43s), while patching [takes about 45 days and only 20 percent of exposed vulnerabilities ever get patched](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0qb0FQo3M5c&t=49s). If your patch pipeline is measured in weeks, that is the number to work on.

A few quick ones. Clypmint released an [open-source b-roll search plugin for Claude Code, Codex or any agent](https://x.com/clypmint/status/2114923160256516536) that searches the Internet Archive, Wikimedia, NASA and the Library of Congress, can find the exact moment inside a YouTube video, and charges 1 cent per search only when it finds a shot. And a handy [LLM system-design interview checklist](https://x.com/shubh19/status/2114924794110255176) walks through a bank support chatbot in 45 minutes: time to first token versus throughput, prefix and semantic caching (Anthropic bills cache reads at 0.1x the base input price), routing easy queries to small models, provider fallback and the OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications.

Also worth a click: OpenCode Desktop [now supports plugins you can write yourself](https://x.com/LukeParkerDev/status/2115009131535085782). MinerU [converts scanned PDFs, tables and equations into Markdown or JSON](https://x.com/oliviscusAI/status/2115027403772928446) an agent can use. And a short Terraform video makes three cost points worth repeating: [always set instance types explicitly](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J4T1Mot4b7Y&t=63s) instead of taking provider defaults, use [prevent_destroy, ignore_changes and create_before_destroy lifecycle rules](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J4T1Mot4b7Y&t=128s), and [tag, track and clean up orphaned resources](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J4T1Mot4b7Y&t=220s).

That's the cycle. Check your blank session's token count, enforce access in the database, and see you tomorrow.
