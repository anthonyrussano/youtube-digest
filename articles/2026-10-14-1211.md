Google says Gemini 4 Argon can [emit a million tokens in a single decode step](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jh6mgf9M06M&t=1372s). Meanwhile Credit Karma's agent stack [cut one Artifactory query from about 24,000 tokens to 2,400](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZycGA_Fbfo&t=1560s).

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Josh Woodward, Melvin Johnson, Sergey Brin and Tulsee Doshi sat down to [introduce Gemini 4 Argon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jh6mgf9M06M). It is [live in the Gemini app, AI Studio, the Gemini API on Cloud, and Antigravity](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jh6mgf9M06M&t=62s). Google says [about 100,000 people inside the company tested it](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jh6mgf9M06M&t=185s) before launch, more than any prior model release, and early Fairwind cyber defenders are already [finding and patching vulnerabilities across critical systems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jh6mgf9M06M&t=84s). The jaw-dropper is the window: Google claims [industry-leading one million output tokens in a single step](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jh6mgf9M06M&t=1372s), not the multi-step million that older Flash models could stitch together. Brin puts that at roughly [half a million seconds of thinking, about a month of human work in one request](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jh6mgf9M06M&t=1561s). Internally, Antigravity teams are [migrating external Fortran codebases to Rust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jh6mgf9M06M&t=540s), agents [monitor hero runs, patch bugs and flag misconfigurations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jh6mgf9M06M&t=484s), and one engineer even [fed a hardware glitch to Argon and got a real motherboard issue back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jh6mgf9M06M&t=624s). Writing quality was a deliberate fight against AI slop with professional editors turning marked-up output into goldens, so the model should feel [more concise and precise](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jh6mgf9M06M&t=343s) for sales, legal, finance and code alike.

At AI Engineer NYC, Harish Gaggar of Intuit Credit Karma walked through a [paved path for enterprise agents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZycGA_Fbfo). Register an agent in an internal portal and you get more than a repo: [workload identity, memory, tracking tables, secrets and a Kubernetes namespace](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZycGA_Fbfo&t=420s) provisioned with the same template. A [centralized MCP gateway](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZycGA_Fbfo&t=600s) sits between every assistant and every tool, handing out JWT sessions via Redis so auth and routing hitch to each call. Custom MCP servers expose only [security-reviewed tools](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZycGA_Fbfo&t=540s), with OpenTelemetry traces in Tempo and metrics in Prometheus so you can see which tool was slow or which auth rejection spiked. LangGraph runs interpret → plan → fetch evidence → policy checks, with [human approval before sensitive actions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZycGA_Fbfo&t=900s). A PII layer aims to [block secrets before they hit the model, memory or traces](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZycGA_Fbfo&t=1380s). The crowd-pleaser was the context optimizer: prune, re-rank, dedupe and compress. On a JFrog Artifactory query, Gaggar showed [about 24,000 tokens drop to 2,400](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZycGA_Fbfo&t=1560s) with the same answer shape, calling it roughly [90% savings](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZycGA_Fbfo&t=1576s), and said most runs land closer to [70–78%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZycGA_Fbfo&t=1620s). The lesson he kept repeating: build the enterprise framework, not just the agent.

Natalia Quintero interviewed Matt Hartman of Factorial Capital, who was on the Betaworks team that [wrote the first check into Hugging Face](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RbUHf4HeBzo) when it was still a teen chatbot. Hartman traces the path from [word2vec-era conversational software](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RbUHf4HeBzo&t=360s) to a hub: the team [converted a hot paper into PyTorch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RbUHf4HeBzo&t=540s), watched it go viral on GitHub, then [moved weights into a richer home on Hugging Face Hub](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RbUHf4HeBzo&t=630s) instead of leaving research as loose repos. He still bets on [technical founders](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RbUHf4HeBzo&t=1170s) because their pivot foot stays planted when the market moves. On the side he vibe-coded Ghost Pepper, an open-source voice-to-text app, with Claude and a skill called Superpowers, and Quintero notes it now has [over 3,000 GitHub stars](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RbUHf4HeBzo&t=2250s). His current mantra: when AI makes coding faster, [product insight becomes exponentially more valuable](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RbUHf4HeBzo&t=1800s) than the code itself.

Sergii Makarevych described [an agentic factory built on subscription tokens and a headless harness](https://x.com/sermakarevich/status/2110374100791074924). The factory supports dynamic agentic graphs with manual, scheduled and event triggers, and he says [splitting a task into a workflow improves accuracy](https://x.com/sermakarevich/status/2110374100791074924) over one-shot agents. If the harness holds up, routine work starts looking like a plant floor instead of a chat box.

David Y. Tan claims a local LLM engine is hitting [273 tokens per second on a single RTX 3090](https://x.com/coffeecup2020/status/2110392058439418030) and [175 tok/s on a 16 GB RTX 5060 Ti](https://x.com/coffeecup2020/status/2110392058439418030), saying it is [37% faster than llama.cpp on a 35B MoE](https://x.com/coffeecup2020/status/2110392058439418030) with identical output with or without speculative decode. Those numbers are his; if they replicate, the local-inference race just got another contender that is [still rewriting itself with agents](https://x.com/coffeecup2020/status/2110392058439418030).

Alexey Grigorev argues that if you have limited time to become an AI engineer, focus on [RAG, agents and evals](https://x.com/Al_Grigor/status/2110370163032002765), with evals mattering most. In his monthly scrapes, the share of AI engineering job postings that mention LLM evaluation [grew from 20%](https://x.com/Al_Grigor/status/2110370163032002765). The market is writing the syllabus out loud.

Rohan Paul highlighted Expand, a startup whose Expand Fetch tool [lets agents read web pages without losing icons, tables and hidden data](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2110412100980048195). On Expand's own private test, agents using it [answered 74% of hard web questions](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2110412100980048195); treat that as a vendor number until someone else reruns it, but the problem it targets—agents that skim and miss structure—is real.

Tom Dörr pointed at a tool that [maps self-hosted infrastructure by scanning networks, Zigbee and Z-Wave devices](https://x.com/tom_doerr/status/2110439150855790651) and then generates automated documentation. For homelabbers drowning in unlabeled gadgets, the inventory writes itself.
