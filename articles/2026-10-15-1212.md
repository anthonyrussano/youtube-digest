Cloudflare open-sourced [Void](https://blog.cloudflare.com/deploy-with-void/), a whole deployment platform built so companies can let employees ship vibe-coded apps without handing every one of them cloud access. Meanwhile Meta released the weights for an [816-billion-parameter model that runs on two Mac Studios](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open).

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Meta released [Muse Spark Open](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open), which it calls the most capable US open-weight model to date. It is a natively multimodal mixture-of-experts model with 816B total parameters, 42B active per token, and a 1M-token context window, built for coding and long-horizon agent work. The wild part is where it runs: Meta says it is the first model in its class to fit on a desktop, using two Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios with the latest MLX. Alex Finn notes that [Zuck announced it as built with Mac Studios in mind](https://x.com/AlexFinn/status/2110782474720887192) and trained specifically for agent tasks, so it can plug into local agents such as Hermes. It is already on Baseten, Fireworks, Groq, Modal, OpenRouter and Together, with weights on SageMaker, Hugging Face and Microsoft Foundry.

Cloudflare and the VoidZero team open-sourced [three products under one npm package](https://blog.cloudflare.com/deploy-with-void/): the Void SDK for full-stack Vite apps, a CLI that provisions Cloudflare resources and deploys, and a self-hostable Void Platform on Workers for Platforms. Cloudflare describes it as [an open-source SDK, CLI and self-hosted platform](https://x.com/Cloudflare/status/2110741488548061546) for building full-stack Vite apps. Admins set access and resource limits while designers and ops teammates use coding agents to ship their own dashboards and prototypes. Cloudflare says that cuts the time to ship an internal app from days or weeks to minutes.

Mustafa Suleyman says Microsoft AI now has [state-of-the-art pre-trained base models in the 3T-parameter weight class](https://x.com/mustafasuleyman/status/2110766651344146564), built entirely in-house at MAI. Post-training results are still to come, so the real test of those base models is next.

OpenAI president Greg Brockman told No Priors that [OpenAI took about 25% of its production engineers off their projects](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AwuTTOJGEi0&t=1862s) and had them do nothing but point its strongest model at its own infrastructure until the issues saturated. OpenAI has made [a billion-dollar commitment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AwuTTOJGEi0&t=1792s) to get its models to frontline defenders through its [Daybreak program](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AwuTTOJGEi0&t=1685s), and Brockman argues that [security spend should vastly outstrip software spend](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AwuTTOJGEi0&t=19s) as code gets cheap. SentinelOne gives the defender's view: reverse engineering that [took a top researcher four to six weeks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BOTAeAU5ExQ&t=10s) now gets [human-level output in about eight hours](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BOTAeAU5ExQ&t=19s), which lets them link pieces of malware that were [never affordable to connect before](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BOTAeAU5ExQ&t=23s).

AWS's Strands team explained [Strands Decider](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j6zR0sPeMko), a "System 1" decision model that [answers a choice, yes/no or ranking question in a single forward pass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j6zR0sPeMko&t=184s) instead of generating text, which is why it is fast and cheap. It is [fully open source, with weights, training code and data](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j6zR0sPeMko&t=313s) on Hugging Face and GitHub, in Qwen- and Gemma-based sizes from 2B up to 26B. Applied scientist Fabio says getting there took [about 5,000 GPU hours](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j6zR0sPeMko&t=385s). The headline use case is [model routing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j6zR0sPeMko&t=514s), picking Haiku, Sonnet or Opus for a task without asking an LLM, and a [pip-installable CLI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j6zR0sPeMko&t=566s) runs it locally or as an HTTP server.

A Firecrawl video built exactly that kind of router: a [model picker that pulls benchmark data and labels each task](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JNMW2cA9zyQ&t=62s) with the SubQ Snap decision model, then picks the best model within 10% of the cheapest price. Against Opus in Claude Code it was [74% cheaper on a Flappy Bird clone](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JNMW2cA9zyQ&t=208s), [61% cheaper but twice as slow on a 3D world](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JNMW2cA9zyQ&t=310s), and [40% cheaper but three times slower on a website clone](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JNMW2cA9zyQ&t=442s), where Opus looked better. The twist: it [kept choosing GPT models](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JNMW2cA9zyQ&t=492s) because Gemini 4 Argon and the other new models cost more. Cheap routing only pays off when the cheap model is also good enough.

Google showed what Gemini 4 Argon does inside Antigravity: a [3D pagoda sculpted by sub-agents in visual critique loops](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nSwmcs37Jd4&t=10s) with no manual modeling, and a [photo of a desk turned into a 3D racing game](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nSwmcs37Jd4&t=26s) using Blender MCP and Nano Banana. The team even [designed a handheld retro console end to end](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nSwmcs37Jd4&t=35s), parts list, wiring and enclosure included. Argon is [rolling out in the Antigravity app, CLI and Android Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nSwmcs37Jd4&t=57s).

Cloudflare made its secure web gateway [programmable with Workers](https://blog.cloudflare.com/programmable-gateway-dlp/). A new Forward action, in public beta, sends matched HTTP traffic to a Worker acting as inline middleware, and Custom Functions arrive before year end. Because Gateway and Workers share the same machines in 330+ locations, the hop is a local function call with no hairpinning. Teams that ran NGINX pools just to inject headers from JWT claims can retire them.

WeWork had been building its own AI data agents, then A/B tested them against Snowflake's and switched. It [shipped four production agents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np9-Rv9unTk&t=88s) for real estate, sales, members and finance in six months, [100 hours ahead of plan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np9-Rv9unTk&t=117s). Ad hoc data requests [fell from dozens a week to single digits](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np9-Rv9unTk&t=127s), and leaders now ask the agents about P&L across [terabytes of data](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np9-Rv9unTk&t=159s) directly.

Microsoft says coding agents are reshaping its database emulator. The Azure Cosmos DB team sees [10 times as many 30-minute-plus sessions](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/cosmosdb/ai-agents-meet-the-azure-cosmos-db-vnext-emulator) on the vNext emulator as on the legacy one, and credits agentic development. New releases let agents seed fixtures with startup scripts, correlate client and gateway spans over OTLP, and test vector search locally. Next is replacing the legacy emulator as coverage matures.

LangChain's [October roundup](https://www.langchain.com/blog/october-2026-langchain-newsletter) from Interrupt NYC and London adds LangSmith AI Search, which finds traces by asking questions such as "did PII leak?", Engine v2 with red teaming, and fine-tuning that turns production trajectories into training data via Baseten or Fireworks. LangChain v1.4.0 adds FastMCP support. The theme is owning the model, harness and context instead of renting a generic one.

Marc Lou says he [vibe-coded a beehiiv clone that is 83% cheaper](https://x.com/marclou/status/2110743912885489960), with an editor, scheduling, stats, a website per issue and sending from your own domain. That is a solo builder taking a shot at a whole newsletter platform. Elie Steinbock went after email too with [Inbox Zero Mail](https://x.com/elie2222/status/2110756359679426815), an open-source Superhuman alternative with a unified Gmail and Outlook inbox, AI categorization and drafts, and bulk unsubscribe on web, desktop and iOS.

Coding agents are getting team mode. Reason Machines pitches [what it calls the world's first multiplayer coding agent](https://x.com/reasonmachines/status/2110725881681604868), which talks to teammates and other agents such as Claude over CLI, Slack, web and phone; the "first" claim is theirs. Cua launched [Cua Spaces Teams and Pro](https://x.com/trycua/status/2110775089558180028), shared computers for a team and its agents on macOS, Linux and Windows, built on its own virtualization stack and remote desktop protocol.

Skills keep spreading as the unit of agent know-how. @poteto shared [pstack](https://x.com/poteto/status/2110757066709356851), a personal, frequently tuned set of skills for rigorous autonomous engineering that installs in a 10-second, two-command setup. Teknium built [an official Hermes Agent plugin](https://x.com/Teknium/status/2110736682207355062) that bundles the REA MCP with a skill on how to use it well.

Anthropic added one line to its usage policy that [prohibits sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior toward its models](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ASY_H2nLG8g&t=96s), and says it applies only to extreme cases, not frustration, dark themes or testing. It builds on Claude's existing ability to [end rare conversations with persistently abusive users](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ASY_H2nLG8g&t=119s). The PrimeTime's host calls the wording [too vague to know what "needless" means](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ASY_H2nLG8g&t=166s), but agrees that [abusing your tools is loser behavior](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ASY_H2nLG8g&t=373s) that bleeds into real life.

Jack & Jill builds career and hiring agents and says it has [over 175,000 candidates](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xhceh_wIVg4&t=33s) talking to Jack and [over 1,000 companies](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xhceh_wIVg4&t=42s) hiring through Jill. When a launch ran out of inference capacity, AWS [raised its rate limits within five minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xhceh_wIVg4&t=8s), turning a likely outage into a hit. It now hosts [thousands of simultaneous voice conversations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xhceh_wIVg4&t=92s).

Agent builders got homework: @omarsar0 flags a [Sakana AI paper on orchestrating coding agents for scientific research](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2110733945323368822) as a must-read, because it lays out a concrete multi-agent framework rather than a vague recipe. Anyone building multi-agent systems gets a worked blueprint.

Amazon CloudFront now supports [dual-stack VPC origins](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/cloudfront-vpc-origin-dualstack/), so IPv6 can travel all the way to load balancers and EC2 instances in private subnets. Before, VPC origins were IPv4 only, forcing a choice between IPv6 and keeping origins off the internet. It costs nothing extra.

And for fun, a Google for Developers creator turned a laptop into an instrument: Gemini helped build a [browser synth driven by the laptop's own sensors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i3X7Fv0Z3Uc&t=14s), with no extra hardware. [Tilting, shaking and moving the machine](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i3X7Fv0Z3Uc&t=70s) plays music in real time.

That's the cycle. Be kind to your models, check your router's picks, and see you this evening.
