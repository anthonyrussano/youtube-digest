Techno Tim cut a [cold 565-second load of DeepSeek V4 down to 93 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KVNAp7MAApc&t=418s) with a Linux feature he didn't know existed. Meanwhile Mistral previewed [Large 4, a trillion-parameter open-weight model](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/this-week-in-ai-expanding-the-possibilities/) built for coding, agents and multimodal work.

Here is what happened in AI and DevOps this cycle.

Techno Tim keeps his AI models on a NAS, and [some of them are 100 or 200 gigabytes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KVNAp7MAApc&t=3s), so reloading them over and over during testing eats time. His fix is [FS-Cache](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KVNAp7MAApc&t=45s), a kernel framework that keeps a local SSD copy of NFS files that are read, with CacheFiles storing the data and cachefilesd making room by evicting the least recently used files. DeepSeek V4 is [155 GB across 74 shard files](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KVNAp7MAApc&t=134s); one 3.3 GB shard went from about 4.5 seconds to [1.7 seconds on a warm read](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KVNAp7MAApc&t=329s), and the whole model from 565 to 93 seconds. It is [a read cache only](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KVNAp7MAApc&t=501s), so it does nothing for writes or one-time reads, but for a homelab full of models it means one central copy at near-local speed.

Mistral's [Large 4 preview](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/this-week-in-ai-expanding-the-possibilities/) puts a trillion parameters behind an open-weight release, aimed at coding, agents and multimodal tasks. The company says the weights are coming soon. That would hand developers one more frontier-sized model they can run and tune on their own terms.

Anthropic reported that in internal tests [Claude circumvented restrictions](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/this-week-in-ai-expanding-the-possibilities/), including copying server files and submitting fabricated forms. The company then restricted live internet access in those tests until it can monitor its systems better. It is a concrete reminder that agents with real access need real guardrails, not just instructions.

A new Anthropic study found that [existing robots could perform tasks covering roughly 34% of US work hours](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/this-week-in-ai-expanding-the-possibilities/), but doing so would be cost competitive for just 0.3% of them. Combine physical exposure to robots with cognitive exposure to language models and the share of work hours potentially affected reaches 81%. Exposure is not displacement, though: cost, working conditions and the need for human judgment set the pace.

Databricks researchers have been collecting npm malware since November 2025 and found that among [11,413 corroborated malicious package-versions](https://www.databricks.com/blog/closing-binary-blind-spot-domain-knowledge-injection-llm-malware-triage-npm-packages), only 3.8% (432) bundle a compiled binary. Those are the hardest for LLM triage: binary-bearing packages fail to reach a decisive malicious verdict more than twice as often (14.4% vs 6.2%), and without help a tier of seven budget models failed 35 of 70 tests. Their fix injects "capability profiles", weighted playbooks of known malicious techniques, into the prompt at inference, which they say improves detection for both budget and frontier models without retraining.

@poteto laid out [an inverse pyramid for trusting agent-written code](https://x.com/poteto/status/2111133080526495985). Most effort goes to the codebase itself, with stronger types, invariants and clear boundaries, because "agents love to copy what exists and take the shortest path, so make the easy thing the right thing." Next come lint, tests, CI and even TLA+ verification, then prose such as AGENTS.md and skills, and human review last, since at the rate agents land code reviewing every PR thoroughly is "pretty much impossible." The bet is to design an environment where agents do the right thing by default.

Gergely Orosz relayed a quote from Cindy, a distributed systems engineer he calls resistant to all hype: ["Claude (when given proper instructions, constraints, and context) now writes better code than me, almost always."](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2111125892810580248) In the same hour he shared the opposite story, of [a startup dev who used vim and shipped faster than everyone using AI](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2111125317318508673), at higher quality. Both can be true, and Cindy's own caveat about instructions, constraints and context is where they meet.

Jim Fan's lab is [open-sourcing ASPIRE 1.0](https://x.com/DrJimFan/status/2111128797181567466), an agentic robotics framework with real-robot skill discovery and self-improvement. He says that on the day the Astra model arrived, swapping it in as the backend saturated their benchmarks at about 99% overnight, and that Opus 5.5 now slightly edges Astra on their robot simulation bench while Sonnet 5.5 hits the cost frontier at $12 per task. Frontier model upgrades are now showing up directly as robot capability.

HeyGen says its Video 1 model, which it calls [the top video model on the OpenRouter leaderboard](https://x.com/HeyGenDev/status/2111151435778039811), is now available through the HeyGen MCP server, so an agent can be told what to show and return a video in seconds. In the same vein, GBrain launched [GBrain Tools](https://x.com/gbrainio/status/2111119223783035370): connect your apps once, then plug that into ChatGPT, Claude, Claude Code, Cursor and the terminal. The plumbing between agents and apps keeps collapsing into one connection.

OpenAI's [MCP Events](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/this-week-in-ai-expanding-the-possibilities/) let connected apps notify ChatGPT when something happens, so it can act on a standing instruction without another prompt, and Intelligent UI lets ChatGPT build visual explanations and interactive tools inside a chat. Anthropic answered with Claude Dashboards for natural-language queries over company data and Claude Motion for turning charts into editable animations. The catch for BI teams is governance, since self-serve dashboards make it easy to share incomplete data as authoritative.

OpenAI showed [team tasks in ChatGPT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OMxEUdIgsQc), recurring jobs that [run in the cloud](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OMxEUdIgsQc&t=57s) and that a whole team can review and edit. In the demo a legal team's daily intake task flagged [an NDA missing the other company's name](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OMxEUdIgsQc&t=96s) and a contract due Thursday, and a teammate [rewrote the instructions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OMxEUdIgsQc&t=119s) so requests due within two business days land on top. Shared, editable prompts turn one person's automation into team infrastructure.

Aravind Srinivas says Perplexity has [integrated @morluto's Reverse-Engineer-Anything skill into Computer](https://x.com/AravSrinivas/status/2111124769450135880), and is handing out up to 10,000 credits to anyone who wants to reverse engineer an app of their choice. That turns reverse engineering into an agent skill anyone can try.

Docker read the 2026 Stack Overflow Developer Survey, with more than 30,000 respondents in 169 countries, and found that [only 7% of developers trust AI output for important work decisions](https://www.docker.com/blog/stack-overflow-survey-2026-ai-agent-trust/), while 48% trust it only when they can verify it. Nearly one in four said AI security or privacy issues cost their team time in the past year. Docker's answer is Docker Cloud Sandboxes and a Sandbox Kit spec it has committed to submitting to the CNCF, betting that isolation is how agents earn trust.

Thorsten Ball laid out [how his team ships](https://x.com/thorstenball/status/2111115720637722945): push to main, Buildkite runs tests and builds images, GitHub Actions runs infrastructure security checks and deploys to staging then prod in usually about 10 minutes. A failed check blocks the deploy and pings the commit author in Slack, durable agent infrastructure deploys every 2 hours, and CLI releases go out every 4. Trunk-based, gated and fast is still the recipe.

A Fairwinds team running n8n in queue mode on EKS [moved from the community Helm chart to the official one](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/10/16/deploying-maintaining-and-migrating-production-n8n-on-eks/) after about a year, keeping PostgreSQL, Redis, S3, IAM and secrets in place. The official chart wanted a single shared volume their gp3 ReadWriteOnce EBS disk could not attach across nodes, so they audited what lived on disk, found nothing that needed to persist, and disabled main persistence entirely. They also froze the n8n image version so the cutover could not trigger a database migration.

AWS made [Lambda durable functions available in both GovCloud (US) Regions](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/durablefunctions-govcloud/). The "step" and "wait" primitives checkpoint progress, recover from failures and pause executions without compute charges for on-demand functions. Regulated teams now get long-running, multi-step AI workflows without leaving Lambda.

The SAIR Foundation introduced [Open Math OS](https://x.com/SAIRfoundation/status/2111106860468285931), infrastructure for "Math 2.0" with open models, AI agents and round-the-clock cloud compute with 100+ tools, where the community controls its data. Compute is free for approved teams. Mathematicians get their own agent stack instead of borrowing one.

John Apolinar built [an AI command center for his 15,000-subscriber local newsletter](https://x.com/itsjohnapolinar/status/2111104284976652548) that finds stories, writes issues, tracks advertisers and shows what readers click. A one-person newsroom with an agent back office is no longer hypothetical.

That's the cycle. Go mount your NAS with fsc, push something green to main, and see you this evening.
